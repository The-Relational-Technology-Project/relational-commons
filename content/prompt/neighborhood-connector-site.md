# Neighborhood Connector Site

> A comprehensive prompt to build a warm, content-first website using AI builders like Lovable. It creates a digital front porch that points neighbors toward real-life groups and local connection, prioritizing 'showing up' over app engagement.

Build a simple, welcoming, content-first website called [NeighborhoodName]people.org.

Purpose:
Help people who live in or near [Neighborhood Name] meet each other through real, existing groups and low-pressure ways of showing up locally.

Tone:
Warm, neighborly, slightly playful, not techy. Feels like someone waving from across the street and saying “oh hi.” Avoid jargon. Avoid hype. This is about showing up, not optimizing.

Core idea:
Friendship happens through shared places, repeated presence, and doing ordinary things together. This site curates those paths.

### Site structure

Single-page site with clear sections and anchored navigation.

Top navigation (simple text links):
* how to make friends in [neighborhood]
* local groups
* more fun
* contact

### Hero section

Large headline:
oh hi, people of [Neighborhood Name]

Subheadline:
A friendly guide to meeting people and finding community in [Neighborhood Name].

Optional short line:
You do not need an app. You just need a place to show up.

Hero imagery:
Wide, calm local visuals. [Describe local landscape features, e.g., Dunes, ocean light for Sunset; Trees, stoops for Brooklyn]. Not busy. Not posed.

### “How to make friends in [Neighborhood]” section

Short, grounded guidance in plain language. This should feel like advice from a neighbor, not a self-help article.

Example structure:
* Show up more than once
* Go to the same place at the same time
* Say hi first, even if it feels awkward
* Join something that already exists
* Let it be slow

Keep this section short and scannable.

### Local groups section

Headline:
local groups

Subhead:
A growing list of groups that make it easier to meet people in [Neighborhood].

Design:
Accordion or expandable list by category. Categories should be visually distinct blocks.

Suggested categories:
* Outdoors & Movement
* Care & Mutual Aid
* Making & Creativity
* Neighborhood & Civic Life
* Faith & Spiritual Life
* Food, Markets & Gathering
* Walking, Running & Low-Key Sports

Each group entry:
* Group name
* One-line description focused on how to show up
* Optional link out

[Provide specific local examples here like 'Beach Clean Up', 'Community Garden', 'Run Club']

Descriptions should emphasize:
When it happens, where it happens, and what it feels like to show up the first time.

### More fun section

Headline:
more fun

Content:
A clear callout pointing people to local event calendars or newsletters if they exist.

### Add a group / contribute section

Explicit invitation to participate.

Headline:
know a group we should add?

Copy:
This list is better when more people help shape it.
If you know a group, club, or recurring gathering in [Neighborhood], we would love to include it.

Simple form:
* Name
* Email
* Group name
* Link (optional)
* Short note

Low friction. No account creation.

### Contact section

Friendly, human closing.

Copy example:
Questions, ideas, or just want to say hi?
We would love to hear from you.

### Footer

Minimal.
* [neighborhood]people.org
* A small line like: Made by neighbors, for neighbors.

## Visual Design

### Overall feel
[Describe the vibe of the neighborhood - e.g. Coastal, spacious, calm, OR Vibrant, brick, energetic].
This should feel like [Neighborhood], not “Tech Startup.”

### Color palette
Primary:
* [Background color typical of the area]
* [Soft distinct divider color]
* [Accent color]

Secondary accents:
* [Local nature colors]

### Typography
Large, friendly sans-serif headline font. Rounded, generous letter spacing.
Body text should be highly readable and calm.
Headlines should feel confident but not loud.

### Interaction style
* Smooth, subtle transitions
* No flashy animations
* Plus icons for expandable lists
* Everything should feel easy and forgiving

### Emotional goal
The site should make someone think:
“Oh. I could do this.”
and then
“I might actually go.”
