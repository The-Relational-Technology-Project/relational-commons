# LittleSis + Oligrapher

> 'The opposite of Big Brother': a free who-knows-who database of business and government power, with an open-source relationship-mapping tool neighborhoods use to map their landlords and developers.

LittleSis — run by the nonprofit Public Accountability Initiative — is a free, collaboratively edited database of the relationships among the people and institutions that hold power: boards, donors, lobbyists, landlords, development interests. Its embedded mapping tool, **Oligrapher**, turns that data (or your own) into clear network maps of who's connected to whom.

For the organizing arc, this is question four's power tool: where the 'Tracking Down the Power' exercise maps actors on a wall, LittleSis lets a tenants union or block association research the actual interlocks — which LLC owns the buildings, who sits on which board, who funds whom — and publish the map. Neighborhood groups have used it to map local landlords and development networks.

Everything is GPL-3.0 open source, and the database itself is a commons anyone can contribute research to.

**Neighborhood remix:** the block's landlord map — every LLC on the street resolved to its owners and their other holdings, built in an evening from public records, ready for the tenants' meeting.
