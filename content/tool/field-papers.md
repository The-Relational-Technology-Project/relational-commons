# Field Papers + OpenStreetMap

> Print a paper atlas of your blocks, walk them with a pen, photograph the pages, and turn the annotations into open map data. Neighborhood mapping with no GPS, no GIS, no app.

Field Papers — descended from Walking Papers, built for the OpenStreetMap community, and stewarded since 2023 by OpenStreetMap US — closes the loop between paper and the world's open map: print a multi-page atlas of anywhere, walk it and annotate with a pen, photograph the pages, upload, and transcribe what you marked into OpenStreetMap, where it becomes commons data anyone can use.

Its lineage runs through the most famous community mapping story there is: Kibera in Nairobi was a blank spot on the world's maps until residents mapped it themselves in 2009 (Map Kibera), using exactly this paper-first pattern.

The tool's deeper insight is that **paper is a feature**: a printed atlas can be carried by kids, marked by elders, passed around a stoop — participation without devices. The transcription evening afterward is its own gathering.

**Neighborhood remix:** a Saturday 'map our block' walk — benches, shade trees, little libraries, step-free entrances, water taps — and by Sunday the block has contributed its own commons layer to the map everyone's apps read.
