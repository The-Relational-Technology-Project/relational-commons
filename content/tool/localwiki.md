# LocalWiki

> Dead-simple editable pages and maps for one place's collective knowledge — born as DavisWiki, where one in seven residents contributed. The software is dormant; the model is undefeated.

LocalWiki grew out of DavisWiki (2004), where founders Philip Neustrom and Mike Ivanov built an openly editable encyclopedia of one California college town — and watched it become core infrastructure, with roughly one in seven residents contributing (the full story is on this shelf). A Knight Foundation grant generalized the software; at its 2015 peak the hosted platform served 300+ communities and ~400,000 monthly readers, all content under Creative Commons Attribution.

Honest status: the sites are online, but development has been largely dormant for years — the code (GPLv2, Python/Django) is old, and the nonprofit's energy has dispersed. It sits on this shelf anyway, for two reasons. First, the model — *every* local thing deserves a page a neighbor can edit: the history, the third places, the characters, the how-things-work — has never been beaten. Second, it's a graveyard-adjacent lesson in what actually made Davis work: not features, but resident habit and a decade of care.

**Neighborhood remix:** don't necessarily run the old software — rebuild the idea at block scale. A 'living field guide' to one neighborhood: every porch, mural, tree, elder, and unwritten rule gets a page neighbors can edit from their phones, licensed openly so it outlives any one steward.
