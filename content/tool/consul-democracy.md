# CONSUL Democracy

> Madrid's open-source citizen participation suite — debates, proposals with signature thresholds, participatory budgets — used by hundreds of cities and now stewarded by an independent foundation.

CONSUL was built inside Madrid's city hall in 2015 to run decide.madrid.es, and grew into the most widely deployed open-source participation suite: citizen debates, proposals that advance when they clear signature thresholds, consultations, collaborative legislation, and participatory budgeting. Madrid's own participatory budget grew from €60M to €100M a year with tens of thousands of participants; the platform is used by 250+ cities and organizations from Munich to Montevideo, and is now stewarded by the CONSUL Democracy Foundation.

It's AGPL-3.0, accessibility-badged, and verified as a Digital Public Good. Like Decidim, self-hosting is an institution-sized job — but the mechanics are the remixable part.

One honest note from Madrid's record: researchers found final city outcomes only partly reflected what citizens voted for. The tool creates the channel; keeping institutions accountable to its results is organizing work, not software work — which is exactly why this shelf ships alongside the Community Organizing one.

**Neighborhood remix:** CONSUL's proposal-plus-threshold mechanic at porch scale — any neighbor's idea that collects ten door-knock endorsements automatically goes on the next potluck agenda for a yes/no decision.
