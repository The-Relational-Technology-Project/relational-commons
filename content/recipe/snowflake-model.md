# The Snowflake: Distributed Leadership

> A structure where leadership is distributed into interconnected small teams, everyone maintains a sustainable number of relationships, and every leader's first job is developing more leaders.

*The need:* Movements that depend on one heroic coordinator die when that person burns out — and most do.

The snowflake model is the organizational shape of the Ganz tradition: interconnected teams, with leadership distributed and mutual accountability instead of top-down management.

Its working parts:
- **Core and local teams.** A core leadership team keeps the whole coherent and holds long-term strategy; local teams deliver short-term goals, adapt strategy to their turf, and feed lessons back. Decision-making is decentralized whenever possible.
- **Sustainable relationship ratios.** Nobody maintains more relationships than they truly can: about five if you're organizing part-time (most neighbors), about ten if full-time. The snowflake grows by adding *teams*, not by overloading *people*.
- **Mutual accountability.** The arrows point both ways: a local team can task the core team just as the core team tasks locals. Commitments are agreed, not assigned.
- **Clear roles.** Every task belongs to a named person; every person knows what others count on them for.
- **Exponential growth through leadership development.** Everyone is responsible for identifying, recruiting, and developing new leaders. Leaders develop leaders who develop leaders — which is how five people become 25, then 125, without anyone holding 125 relationships.

People step up the **ladder of engagement** — supporter, volunteer, team member, leadership prospect, organizer — through real tests: run the phone bank once before you're asked to captain it. Follow up within 48 hours at every rung; invest in the people who show up rather than chasing the ones who don't.

## Details

- **ingredients:** one team that works, a norm of developing leaders, patience for the math of small numbers
- **source note:** From 'Organizing: People, Power, Change' (2014): the snowflake model, sustainable relationship ratios, ladder of engagement, and team stages.
- **how it works:** Start with one team: purpose, roles, norms., Cap everyone's active relationships at what they can truly maintain (about five, part-time)., Test and escalate: give growing members real responsibility, then real leadership., When the team is strong (4+ core organizers), split or seed a new team on new turf., Keep core and local teams accountable to each other in both directions.
- **min resources:** Free.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** no single point of failure, new leaders run things founders used to run, growth comes in teams, not in one person's overloaded contact list
