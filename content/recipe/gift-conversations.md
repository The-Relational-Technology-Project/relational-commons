# Gift Conversations: Head, Hands, Heart

> The ABCD interview: four questions that surface what a neighbor knows, can do, cares about, and belongs to — because 'a gift isn't a gift until it is given.'

*The need:* Most neighborhoods have never once been asked what they're good at.

The gift conversation (ABCD's 'learning conversation') is a doorstep or kitchen-table interview whose only agenda is discovering capacities. John McKnight's four questions:

1. **Gifts of the head** — What do you especially know about? Birds, mathematics, neighborhood history?
2. **Gifts of the hands** — What do you know how to do? Carpentry, cooking, guitar, baseball?
3. **Gifts of the heart** — What do you especially care about? Children, the environment, veterans, elders?
4. **Belonging** — What clubs, groups, and associations do you and your family take part in?

Pastor Mike Mather's Indianapolis congregation added two of the best follow-ups in the tradition: 'What three things do you do well enough that you could teach others?' and 'Who, besides God and me, is going with you along the way?'

The craft is what happens after: **name** the gift back to the person (many have never heard their capacity named), **bless** it (say publicly that it matters), and **connect** it — to a neighbor who needs it, an association that could use it, a group of others with the same gift. Twenty-two solo musicians become a band; four mothers become a childcare swap; twenty-nine people with repair skills become the block's fix-it crew. The original Capacity Inventory campaigns in Chicago surfaced more than a hundred women with care experience who formed a home-care enterprise that connected over eighty of them to paid work.

The discipline: this is a conversation, not a survey. Consent, not collection. And it is pointless without connectors committed to acting on what they hear — 'a gift isn't a gift until it is given. Before the giving, it is only a beautifully wrapped box in a drawer.'

## Details

- **ingredients:** the four questions, a porch or kitchen table, a connector who will act on what's heard
- **source note:** Four questions quoted from McKnight's 'A Basic Guide to ABCD Community Organizing' (Nurture Development); name/bless/connect and the follow-up questions from Broadway UMC's roving listener practice (Faith & Leadership; Mather 2018); Chicago home-care outcome from 'How to Use a Capacity Inventory' (Kretzmann & McKnight).
- **how it works:** Ask the four questions: head, hands, heart, belonging., Listen for what they'd teach, not just what they'd take., Name the gift back to them; bless it publicly., Connect it: person to person, person to association, association to association., Keep the inventory light and consented — the relationship is the record.
- **min resources:** Free.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** a neighbor teaches something for the first time, gifts get asked for within a month of being named, people at the margins are interviewed first, not last
