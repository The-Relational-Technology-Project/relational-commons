# Dinner Party Grief

> A small dinner with peers who have also lost someone. Real food, real conversation, no expert.

## What it is

A peer grief support model that meets around dinner instead of in a clinical circle. People who have experienced significant loss (a parent, partner, sibling, child, close friend) gather in groups of 6 to 8 to share meals and talk about how grief shows up in their lives. The original model serves people ages 18 to 45, but the format works at any age.

The Dinner Party, founded in 2010 by Carla Fernandez, now has Tables in 150+ cities. The format is a model for what informal, peer-led grief support can look like outside therapeutic settings.

## Why it works

Grief in most cultures is socially short. People expect grievers to be done in months. Real grief takes years and keeps showing up. Most grief support is clinical, age-mixed, or both. Neither fits the daily life of a young griever back at work the week after a funeral.

A peer Table changes both. Same life stage, same loss landscape, food on the table. The combination produces conversations grievers have nowhere else.

## What you need

- **Time:** 2 to 3 hours per gathering, every 2 to 6 weeks. Ongoing.
- **People:** 6 to 8 peer participants. A trained or experienced peer leader.
- **Space:** A home, a private restaurant room, a community kitchen, or video.
- **Materials:** Food and drinks. Optional discussion prompts.
- **Skills:** Peer support. Active listening. Comfort with grief and silence.
- **Energy level:** Medium. Emotionally engaging.

## How to host it

1. **If you can, join an existing Table first.** [The Dinner Party](https://www.thedinnerparty.org/) matches you with peers near you or virtually. Going through the experience is the best way to learn the format.
2. **Or, start your own.** Reach out to 5 to 10 people you know who've experienced significant loss. Be specific about the format and the loss type if there's a shared one.
3. **Pick a host home or rotation.** Many Tables rotate hosting; the host cooks or coordinates potluck.
4. **Set the meeting rhythm.** Most Tables meet every 4 to 6 weeks. Frequent enough to maintain relationships, sparse enough to fit in real life.
5. **Open with a check-in.** Each person, briefly: how grief showed up since last time. No fixing. No ranking. Listen.
6. **Share the meal.** Conversation moves naturally between grief, work, relationships, what's hard, what's surprising. The Table is the structure; the meal is the medium.
7. **Hold confidentiality.** What's said at the Table stays at the Table. Members can choose to talk outside, but it's not assumed.
8. **End with a closing ritual if you want one.** A toast, a poem, a moment of silence. Or just goodnight.
9. **Stay together over time.** The relationships are the work. The Table that runs for two years is doing something different than the one that runs for two months.

## Variations

**Loss-specific Table.** A Table for people who've lost parents, partners, siblings, or children. The shared loss type deepens recognition.

**Older Table.** The Dinner Party's original model is 18 to 45, but the format works at any age, and other groups have built versions for older grievers.

**Buddy system.** The Dinner Party also offers one-to-one peer matches. Some Tables have an internal buddy system between gatherings.

**Online Table.** Video-based. Works across cities. Less of the meal-as-medium effect but the peer-recognition effect is intact.

## Stories from neighborhoods

**Rey Sosa** lost his mother to COVID-19 at age 26. He joined an in-person grief group but was the only person under 50, the only man, surrounded by people grieving jobs, not parents. When he found The Dinner Party, he found peers at the same life stage.

**Elena Lopez Del Carril** was matched through the Buddy System with someone whose grief story mirrored hers. Both had lost fathers weeks apart. Both were the youngest in their families. The connection, forged across continents, became a lifeline.

**Carla Fernandez** founded The Dinner Party in 2010 with a paella dinner in Los Angeles for friends who had also lost a parent. The first Table was four people. The model now runs in 150+ cities.
