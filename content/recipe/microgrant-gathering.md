# Microgrant Gathering

> Give a neighbor $50 to $100. They host a gathering. They tell you what happened. Almost no friction. Wide reach.

## What it is

A funding mechanism that distributes small grants ($50 to $100) to neighbors who want to host community gatherings: block parties, potlucks, porch sits, seasonal celebrations, community conversations. The application is short (3 to 5 minutes). The approval is fast (often within a day). The reporting is light (a photo and brief note about what happened). The program trusts that neighbors know what their communities need.

The model has been developed and refined by [Connective Tissue](https://connectivetissue.substack.com/), Trust for Civic Life, and other organizations exploring how to lower the barrier to civic participation.

## Why it works

The barrier to hosting a community gathering is rarely desire or idea. It is the psychological lift of *can I do this* combined with the practical lift of *who pays for the snacks*. A small grant resolves both. The money signals permission. Someone external believed enough to fund it. The neighbor can show up to their own gathering with confidence.

The light reporting and high trust matter. Every layer of bureaucracy reduces who can apply. Three minutes and a photo is a real recipe. Two hours of paperwork and receipt tracking is a different program with worse outcomes.

## What you need

- **Time:** 3 to 5 minutes to apply. 2 to 4 hours to host. 10 minutes to document.
- **People:** A host plus 8 to 15 attendees. A coordinating team to review applications and distribute funds.
- **Space:** Any welcoming neighborhood space. Porch, backyard, park, community room, library.
- **Materials:** $50 to $100 in funds. Basic supplies for whatever the gathering type is.
- **Skills:** Basic facilitation. Ability to reach neighbors.
- **Energy level:** Moderate.

## How to host it

### If you're a neighbor applying for one

1. **Find a program.** Some neighborhood foundations, libraries, or organizations offer this. The [Connective Tissue](https://connectivetissue.substack.com/) site documents several models. Trust for Civic Life pilots have run in multiple cities.
2. **Apply.** Most applications ask: what's the gathering, when, where, roughly who will come, why it matters to your block. Aim for clarity, not length. Three sentences each.
3. **Host the gathering.** Use the funds for supplies, food, simple decorations, accessibility (translation, childcare, transportation if relevant).
4. **Document lightly.** A photo. A few sentences about what happened. Any commitments or next steps that emerged.

### If you're running a microgrant program

1. **Decide on the budget.** Even $3,000 distributes 30 grants of $100 each. That's 30 neighborhood gatherings.
2. **Define the criteria.** Common ones: open to anyone in a defined geography, must be a community gathering (not a private party), must invite people beyond the host's immediate circle, alcohol policy if relevant.
3. **Build the application form.** 5 questions max. Make it accessible online and on paper. Multilingual if your neighborhood is.
4. **Set up fast review.** A small committee that turns applications around in 1 to 3 business days. Speed is part of the design.
5. **Distribute funds simply.** Some programs use Venmo or PayPal for speed. Some send a check. Some give a Visa gift card. Choose what fits your community.
6. **Set the documentation requirement.** A photo and a paragraph. No receipts. No itemized accounting. Trust.
7. **Capture stories.** The program's outcomes are stories, not just numbers. Compile what neighbors say about their gatherings. Share back.
8. **Reiterate annually or seasonally.** A program that runs once is a pilot. A program that runs each year becomes infrastructure.

## Variations

**Recurring gathering microgrant.** A slightly larger grant ($200 to $500) for a host who'll run a monthly gathering for a season. Higher commitment, deeper relationships.

**Themed microgrants.** Grants specifically for block parties (see [Block Party](../reference/block-party.md)) or [story-sharing suppers](../recipe/story-sharing-potluck-supper.md) or holiday gatherings.

**Microgrant + coaching.** Some programs pair the money with light coaching from an experienced host. Useful for new conveners.

**Tiered grants.** $50 for a porch hang. $200 for a block party. $500 for a longer project. The bigger ones require slightly more application.

## Stories from neighborhoods

**Connective Tissue's Charlottesville pilot** in spring 2023 received 70 applications and distributed $100 microgrants to 30 hosts. Within a few weeks, the program reached nearly 500 neighbors across 25 different neighborhoods. Almost every attendee committed to concrete next steps afterward. Some hosts started recurring monthly gatherings. Others organized neighborhood improvements. Many simply deepened relationships with people they had never met.

One host's reflection captured the program's logic: *the barrier wasn't desire or ideas. It was the psychological lift of "Can I do this?" The $100 answered that question.*

**Trust for Civic Life** and other funders have piloted similar programs in multiple cities, with consistent outcomes: dramatically expanded participation, ideas surfaced from neighbors who would never have applied to a traditional grant process, and a flowering of small gatherings that bigger funding mechanisms can't catalyze.
