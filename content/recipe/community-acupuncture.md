# Community Acupuncture

> Acupuncture in a shared room, on a sliding scale. Trust-based pricing. People who can afford to pay more support people who can't.

## What it is

A model of acupuncture practice in which patients sit together in a quiet, shared room and pay on a sliding scale (often $10 to $70) based on what they can afford. The model eliminates the financial barrier that excludes most working- and lower-income people from acupuncture in the U.S. and creates an unexpected social space: a room of people healing in shared silence.

## Why it works

Conventional U.S. acupuncture practice, in private rooms with per-session fees of $80 to $150 or more, is inaccessible to most people who could benefit from it. Community acupuncture solves the cost problem through sliding scale and group treatment, and turns what could be a healthcare transaction into a small daily ritual that builds trust between practitioner, patient, and the broader community.

The shared-room format is also therapeutic in its own right. Many patients report that sitting in a room with others in a quiet healing state amplifies the effect.

## What you need

- **Time:** Treatment sessions are typically 45 to 60 minutes. Weekly or biweekly ongoing scheduling.
- **People:** Licensed acupuncturist(s). Admin staff (often part-time). Patient community. Optional donors for "pay-it-forward" treatments.
- **Space:** A large quiet room with reclining chairs or beds, comfortable temperature, accessibility.
- **Materials:** Acupuncture needles and sterilization equipment. Intake and waiver forms.
- **Skills:** Licensed acupuncture practice, community facilitation, trust-based economics management.
- **Energy level:** Low to moderate for patients. Moderate for practitioners.

## How to host it

Community acupuncture clinics are typically launched by licensed acupuncturists. The recipe for community members is closer to *how to support an existing clinic or bring one to your neighborhood*.

### If you're a licensed acupuncturist starting a clinic

1. **Connect with the [People's Organization of Community Acupuncture (POCA)](https://www.pocacoop.com/).** This is the field's cooperative network. They support new clinics with model documents, training, and a community of practitioners.
2. **Find space.** A storefront, a community center room, sometimes a faith space. Quiet, accessible, large enough for 8 to 12 reclining chairs.
3. **Set the sliding scale.** Common ranges: $10 to $70. No income verification. Trust.
4. **Stock simply.** Needles, basic equipment, comfortable seating, soft lighting and sound.
5. **Open for treatments.** Many community clinics run drop-in or short-appointment models. Sessions can overlap (one patient is receiving needles while two others are resting).
6. **Build the practice.** Word of mouth is the dominant channel. Patients return weekly or biweekly for chronic conditions, pain management, mental health, fertility, and many other concerns.
7. **Hold trust.** Some patients will overpay. Some will underpay. Most will pay close to the middle. This is the design. Don't audit. Don't enforce.

### If you're a community member supporting an existing clinic

1. **Try it.** A single session is the cheapest way to find out what the model is.
2. **Pay generously if you can.** The sliding scale only works if some patients pay above the middle.
3. **Spread the word.** Most community acupuncture clinics grow through word of mouth in their neighborhoods.
4. **Pay-it-forward.** Some clinics accept donations earmarked to subsidize treatments for people who can't afford even the minimum. Often a few hundred dollars covers many sessions.

## Variations

**Mobile community acupuncture.** Some clinics travel to schools, recovery programs, shelters, and other community spaces.

**Specialty community clinics.** Some clinics specialize in fertility, addiction recovery, veterans' health, or other domains.

**Auricular (ear) acupuncture protocols.** **NADA (National Acupuncture Detoxification Association)** has trained tens of thousands of practitioners in a specific 5-needle ear protocol used in addiction recovery, mental health, and disaster response. Lower barrier to entry; lower scope of practice.

**Healing clinic.** Some community spaces host rotating practitioners (massage, herbalism, acupressure, energy work) on similar sliding-scale models.

## Stories from neighborhoods

**Community Acupuncture clinics** operate across the country. Examples: Albany Community Acupuncture (NY), Little Pine (Portland), Boston Acupuncture Project, Brooklyn Acupuncture Project, Acupuncture for the People, Open Care Community Acupuncture (NYC). The **POCA Cooperative** counts hundreds of member clinics nationally.

The model has expanded substantially in response to healthcare access gaps. It pairs naturally with harm reduction networks, community mental health programs, and chronic pain management settings where traditional medical care is expensive or inaccessible.
