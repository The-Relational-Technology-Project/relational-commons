# Coaching: Enabling Others in Five Steps

> The practice that makes 'are we stronger now?' come true: help people find their own solutions — inquire, diagnose (head, heart, or hands), intervene with questions, let them act, then check in.

*The need:* Founders who solve every problem themselves produce groups that can't solve anything without them.

If organizing is enabling others, coaching is how. The coach's role is not to have answers — it is to ask questions that help people uncover answers in themselves, so they build the capacity to act, and to coach others in turn. That chain is what makes a community stronger after every campaign, not just older.

The five steps:
1. **Inquire & observe.** Be present. Listen, watch, and backtrack — "What I'm hearing you say is ___, is that right?" — before drawing conclusions.
2. **Diagnose.** Organizing challenges come in three kinds: **head** (strategy — they can't see a path), **heart** (motivation — they're discouraged or afraid), **hands** (skills — they don't know how yet). The diagnosis decides the intervention.
3. **Intervene.** Push gently toward a plan that is theirs: "If you knew you couldn't fail, what would you do?" "What's the very next step?" "What do you need, and by when?"
4. **Action.** Step back. Give them room to do it. Resist the urge to do it for them — that urge is the whole disease.
5. **Check in.** Hold them accountable with warmth: debrief what happened, celebrate what worked, and re-diagnose what didn't.

Coaching is not false praise, and it's not a performance review. It happens in maintenance 1:1s, after actions, and whenever someone says "I don't know how." A team with a culture of coaching turns every difficulty into leadership development — which is the quiet answer to the sixth question.
