# The House Meeting

> Fred Ross's instrument: a small gathering in someone's living room, hosted by a resident who invites their own people. Cycles of house meetings build a base — and reveal who the real leaders are.

*The need:* Rallies reach the already-convinced. Living rooms reach networks.

The house meeting, refined by Fred Ross Sr. organizing with the Community Service Organization in the 1940s–50s, is a small gathering — a living room's worth of people — convened by a **host who invites their own network**: family, coworkers, church friends, the neighbors they actually talk to. The organizer doesn't supply the guest list; the host's relationships are the meeting.

The shape: the host welcomes people they trust into a comfortable setting (food matters — it marks the gathering as hospitality, not procedure). The organizer or a developing leader shares story and purpose, listens hard to what these particular people care about, and closes with asks — including the ask that makes the method compound: *who here will host the next meeting, with their own people?* Ross ran roughly three-week cycles of house meetings building toward one large organizing meeting, then further cycles toward a founding convention of a real organization.

The house meeting does three jobs at once. It **recruits through trust** — people come because someone they know asked. It **surfaces what the community actually cares about**, in a setting where people speak freely. And above all it **reveals leaders**: watch who convenes people, who speaks with authority in their own kitchen, who follows through. That's how Ross found Cesar Chavez — at a house meeting in Chavez's own living room (the story is on this shelf) — and it's how the UFW and the 2008 neighborhood teams built bases that lists never could.

A chain of house meetings is the six questions running on hospitality: who are my people, what do we care about, and who will commit to convening next.

## Details

- **ingredients:** a host with real relationships, their living room, food, a story, an ear, and an ask, the next host
- **source note:** Method and three-week cycle structure per the Zinn Education Project and Beyond Chron's history of the Ross house-meeting tradition; leader-spotting function per Thompson's biography.
- **how it works:** Find a host; the host invites their own people — the organizer never supplies the list., Gather small: a living room's worth, with food., Share story and purpose briefly; spend most of the time listening., Close with commitments — including who will host the next meeting., Run cycles of meetings toward one big founding gathering; watch for who convenes and follows through — those are your leaders.
- **min resources:** Free. Someone's couch and a pot of coffee.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** each meeting produces its next host, the organizer hears concerns they didn't arrive with, leaders emerge whom no sign-up sheet would have found
