# Mutual Aid Pod

> Five to twenty neighbors pool skills and resources to meet each other's actual needs. No charity, no nonprofit, just neighbors.

## What it is

A mutual aid pod is a small, committed network that meets practical needs through direct, reciprocal support. It is distinct from charity in a specific way: everyone gives and everyone receives. There are no helpers and helped, just people who, in different seasons, need different things.

Mutual aid has deep roots in working-class, immigrant, and Black radical organizing. Dean Spade's 2020 book *Mutual Aid* is the modern foundational text. The practice is older than the term.

## Why it works

Most needs in a neighborhood are small enough that one person can solve them and large enough that no one is going to navigate a bureaucracy to ask. A working pod removes the bureaucracy. Someone needs a ride to a medical appointment. Someone needs groceries this week. Someone needs help carrying a piece of furniture upstairs. Pods make these solvable in a text thread.

The deeper effect is that the pod becomes a place where you can ask for help without shame. That is rare and worth a lot.

## What you need

- **Time:** 2 to 4 hours monthly for coordination, plus the actual help.
- **People:** Start with 3 to 5. Grow to 5 to 20 organically.
- **Space:** Mostly digital communication. Occasional in-person.
- **Materials:** A group chat, a simple shared spreadsheet or document, optionally a shared calendar.
- **Skills:** Coordination. Comfort with vulnerability. Willingness to ask.
- **Energy level:** Moderate, scales with the scope of needs.

## How to host it

1. **Start with 3 to 5 trusted people.** Don't try to launch a 30-person pod from day one. Build relationships first.
2. **Map your existing pod.** Write down everyone you'd already feel okay asking for help: rides, meals, childcare, emotional support, money in a pinch. This is your starting network, even before the pod is named.
3. **Pick a name and a channel.** A group text or Signal group is usually enough. Larger pods sometimes use a dedicated Discord or a shared sheet.
4. **Identify one shared commitment to start.** Examples: meals during anyone's hardship, a tool-share, rides to medical appointments, a babysitting trade, monthly mutual budget check-ins. One thing, done well.
5. **Use a simple request rhythm.** Someone has a need, they post it. Someone who can, replies. Confirm in DM. Follow through. Report back to the group.
6. **Hold monthly check-ins.** 30 to 60 minutes. What's working, what isn't, who needs what, who has capacity. This is when small frictions get named and unstuck.
7. **Grow slowly.** Each new member should be vouched for by at least one existing member. Trust scales slower than logistics.
8. **Decide together what scope you can hold.** Pods that try to do everything burn out. Pods that pick a few things and do them well last.

## Variations

**Building pod.** A pod within a single apartment building or condo complex.

**Mom pod / parent pod.** A pod of caregivers trading childcare, sick days, meals. Often the gateway form for people who haven't been in mutual aid before.

**Crisis pod.** Activated during emergencies (illness, eviction, disaster), then either disperses or evolves into ongoing support.

**Larger network.** Multiple pods that coordinate occasionally on bigger projects. See [Mutual Aid Networks](https://www.mutualaidnetwork.org/) and the [Mutual Aid Hub](https://www.mutualaidhub.org/).

## Stories from neighborhoods

**A Brooklyn mutual aid pod during 2020** started with 10 people organized through Instagram. They identified that seniors needed grocery shopping and medication pick-up. They created a simple system: people texted needs, volunteers confirmed pickup, payment ran on an honor system. Within months the pod grew to 40 people, added a cooking team, coordinated rent support, provided tech help, and connected people to benefits. By 2023 it had formalized while maintaining mutual aid principles. Members emphasize that the magic is that everyone gives and receives.

The U.S. saw an explosion of mutual aid pods in 2020, documented at the [Mutual Aid Hub](https://www.mutualaidhub.org/), which lists thousands of active pods. Many of the strongest pods have continued and deepened in the years since.
