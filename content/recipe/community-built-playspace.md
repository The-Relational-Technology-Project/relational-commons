# Community-Built Playspace

> Fifty to two hundred neighbors show up over a long weekend and build a playground together. By Sunday night there's a place for kids to play and a community that wasn't there before.

## What it is

A concentrated community design and build that turns a vacant lot or under-used space into a playground over a few days. Kids participate in the design. Adults build it. Volunteers, businesses, families, and expert facilitators collaborate. The playground is the visible product; the community that emerges from the build is the deeper one.

The signature organization in this space is [KABOOM!](https://kaboom.org/), which has supported more than 17,000 community-built playspaces since 1996, engaging 1.5 million community members.

## Why it works

A playground built for a community is just a playground. A playground built by a community is shared infrastructure with an origin story. Every parent who pushed a kid's swing knows another parent who hammered the same swing's frame. The relationships built during the four days of construction become the social infrastructure of the playground for years afterward.

The compressed timeline (typically four days of building) creates a different kind of intensity than ongoing volunteer projects. It is closer to a barn-raising. The community arrives on day one as strangers and leaves as a network.

## What you need

- **Time:** 5 to 12 weeks of planning. 4 concentrated build days.
- **People:** 50 to 200+ volunteers across the build. Children and adults from the community. Expert build managers (provided by KABOOM! or a similar partner if you go that route).
- **Space:** A vacant lot or park area of about 2,000 to 5,000 square feet.
- **Materials:** Playground equipment, lumber, hardware, safety surfacing, tools, paint. Often coordinated through the partner organization.
- **Skills:** Facilitation, project management, basic construction (welcoming various skill levels). Expert oversight on the actual build.
- **Energy level:** High. Physical construction across multiple days.

## How to host it

1. **Decide your partnership.** Most communities work with [KABOOM!](https://kaboom.org/) or a similar organization. The partner provides design expertise, materials coordination, build management, and a process refined over thousands of builds. Some communities do it independently (it's possible but harder).
2. **Apply or scope the project.** With KABOOM!, the community partner applies, and if selected, a Community Mobilization Manager helps shape the project. Independently, your core team owns this step.
3. **Hold a kids' design day.** This is the heart of the planning phase. Kids in the neighborhood draw what they want the playground to be. Their drawings shape the actual design. Adults adapt the drawings into a buildable plan.
4. **Build the planning team.** Logistics, volunteer recruitment, materials, food and hospitality, communications, safety. Distribute the work.
5. **Recruit volunteers.** Multiple shifts across multiple days. Neighbors, businesses, faith communities, schools. KABOOM! builds often have a corporate sponsor that contributes volunteer crews.
6. **Prepare the site.** Before the build, the site is graded, utilities marked, materials staged.
7. **Build days.** Typically:
   - Day 1: site preparation, footings.
   - Day 2: structure assembly.
   - Day 3: structure completion, surfacing.
   - Day 4: finishing, safety check, ribbon cutting.
   Expert Build Managers oversee safety. Different volunteer stations handle different skill levels.
8. **Hand off to stewards.** Identify who will maintain the playground over time. Often a partnership of the neighborhood association, the city parks department, and a local school.
9. **Celebrate.** A community party on day four. Most playgrounds open to kids that afternoon.

## Variations

**Pocket park build.** A smaller variant: a green space, a few benches, planters, a pollinator garden. Days of work rather than weekends. Lower stakes.

**Skate park or fitness equipment.** Same model, different demographic. Build for teens or adults.

**Outdoor classroom or maker space.** A school grounds or community center grounds. The same compressed community-build energy.

**Renovation rather than new build.** Existing playgrounds that need repair or refresh can use the same model.

## Stories from neighborhoods

**KABOOM!** has supported more than 17,000 community-built playspaces since 1996.

At **Detroit's St. Jude Family Childcare Center**, a KABOOM! build transformed a vacant lot into a beloved playground. A local resident began opening and closing it on weekends so neighbors could use it. That single act of stewardship by one neighbor extended the life and reach of the project.

A long-running partnership with **Discover** has put 13,000+ corporate volunteers across 75,000+ hours into 50+ community builds over 30 years. The model has demonstrated that the build itself often catalyzes lasting civic engagement, not just the playground.
