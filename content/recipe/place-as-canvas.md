# Place as Canvas

> Document one hyperlocal place so intensively it becomes a mirror for the whole community's memory and identity.

*The need:* Communities losing connection to place lose part of themselves.

Places hold memory, identity, and possibility, and when communities lose connection to place through displacement, development, or erasure, they lose part of themselves. This recipe turns one specific place into a canvas for collective memory: pick a single street, corner, or building and document it so intensively that it becomes a mirror for the whole community to see its history and its future. Specificity creates universality, displays change regularly to build anticipation, all histories are included (especially the ignored ones), and the space itself is allowed to determine the format.

**How it works**
1. Choose a place that is under threat or undervalued.
2. Document everything: who was here, what happened, what it means.
3. Layer past onto present, such as old photos in current windows and elder stories on young walls.
4. Make the invisible history visible in the actual place.
5. Change the display regularly to pull people back.
6. Let the place tell its own story through accumulated details.

**Ingredients**
- one specific place that matters
- 10-15 people with stories about it
- ways to make documentation visible
- someone who loves details
- permission to claim space

**Minimum resources:** Free with phones; $ for printing if physical.

**You'll know it's working when**
- people return to see what's new
- 'I never knew this happened here' becomes common
- the place becomes protected through community love

**Field tips**
- specificity creates universality
- change displays regularly
- include all histories
- let the space determine the format

**Building from (real projects)**
- Westminster Stories
- Greene Street
- The Wall of Wrigley Field
- Oakland Lowdown
- Memories of the Market
