# The Debrief: Did It Work, and Are We Stronger?

> After every action, ask two different questions: did we move our goal, and did we grow — more people, more skills, deeper relationships? Celebrate honestly either way.

*The need:* Groups that skip the debrief repeat their mistakes; groups that skip the celebration repeat their burnout.

Every action and every campaign closes with a debrief — and the debrief asks two distinct questions, because a group can win a fight and come out weaker, or lose one and come out stronger.

**Question one: did it work?** Compare outcomes to the measurable goal you set. Be honest in both directions: never claim a victory that isn't yours, and never pretend a loss is a win — either lie robs the effort of its value. If you lost, contextualize the loss, interpret what happened, accept responsibility, and prepare for next time.

**Question two: are we stronger now?** Count what capacity-building actually looks like: How many new people took action for the first time? Who took on a bigger role than last time? Which relationships deepened? What can the team now do that it couldn't before? What did we learn about our opposition and our own leverage? This is the sweet-spot test applied in the rear-view mirror — and it's the question that separates organizing from mere mobilizing.

**Then celebrate.** Recognize specific contributions by name. When we win, we sometimes party without learning; when we lose, we sometimes analyze without honoring the courage and work people gave. Do both, every time.

Write down the three biggest lessons where the next campaign will find them. There is always a next time, and the debrief is where the next campaign quietly begins.
