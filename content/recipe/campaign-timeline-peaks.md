# The Campaign Timeline: Foundation, Kickoff, Peaks

> Campaigns have a rhythm: a quiet foundation of relationship-building, a kickoff that starts the clock, rising peaks that each build capacity, a mountaintop, and then — win or lose — evaluation and celebration.

*The need:* Groups either simmer forever without ever starting, or sprint to a rally and collapse the week after.

The rhythm of organizing is the campaign: a focused burst of coordinated activity toward a goal, with a shape you can draw on a napkin — capacity rising through time like a snowball gathering size.

**Foundation.** Before anything public: 1:1s, house meetings, small gatherings. Build the broadest base you can without letting things heat up too early. This is where leadership gets developed.

**Kickoff.** The moment the campaign officially begins — a big meeting or gathering where the story is told, the plan is ratified, leadership is recognized, and commitments are collected. Its real purpose isn't media; it's people and commitment. Setting the date creates the urgency that gets everything else done.

**Peaks.** The campaign climbs through preliminary peaks — each with a measurable goal (people at the meeting, signatures gathered, organizations pledged) that breaks through to a new level of capacity for the next climb.

**The mountaintop.** Maximum mobilization, timed for when it matters most. Beware peaking at the kickoff.

**Evaluation and next steps.** Campaigns are won or lost — and resolving one means being honest about which. Never claim a victory that isn't yours, and never pretend a loss is a win; it robs the effort of its value. Win: celebrate *and* learn why. Lose: contextualize, take responsibility, honor what people gave — and prepare, because there is always a next time.

## Details

- **ingredients:** a strategic goal, a kickoff date, measurable peak goals, a debrief on the calendar before you start
- **source note:** From 'Organizing: People, Power, Change' (2014), Acting: Tactics & Timelines.
- **how it works:** Foundation: build relationships and leaders before going public., Set a kickoff date — the deadline that focuses recruiting and planning., Design each peak with a number attached, so success is observable., Time the mountaintop for when power is needed most, not when enthusiasm is highest., Close with evaluation AND celebration, win or lose.
- **min resources:** Free.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** capacity is visibly higher after each peak, the campaign peaks at the decision moment, not the kickoff, the debrief happens even after a loss
