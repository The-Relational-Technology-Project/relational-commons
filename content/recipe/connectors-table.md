# The Connectors Table

> Find the people who are already at the center of the room — gift-centered, trusted, joyful about introducing neighbors — and convene them as the neighborhood's connective tissue.

*The need:* Every neighborhood already has connectors. Almost none has ever put them in the same room.

McKnight's distinction: 'A leader is a person at the front of the room... A connector is in the center of the room, often unrecognized but always creating new relationships.' Connectors are gift-centered (they see the full half of everyone), well-connected (a practiced connector knows 80–100 neighbors), trusted, believers in their community, and people who get real joy from convening and inviting. Cormac Russell adds: they 'go guarantor' — they spend their own social capital vouching for the gifts of marginalized neighbors.

The practice is simple: identify a handful of them — ask 'who knows everyone around here?' and follow the answers — and invite them to a **Connectors Table**: a recurring, informal gathering whose work is (1) sharing what they're hearing in gift conversations, (2) making introductions on purpose (person to person, person to association, association to association), and (3) noticing what the neighborhood could do next with what it has.

Why associations are the table's real leverage: 'Voting is the power to give your power away... An association is a means to make power.' Associations decide what needs doing, decide how, and do it — no client role anywhere. They also stretch beyond their founding purpose (a motorcycle club becomes a child-protection network), and they can federate: an association of associations is, in McKnight's words, the most powerful tool a neighborhood has.

The Connectors Table is where 'who are my people?' and 'what resources do we have?' braid together — and it's the ABCD tradition's answer to the organizing tradition's leadership team.

## Details

- **source note:** Connector definition and traits from McKnight's Basic Guide and Russell's 'Touchstone One' (Nurture Development); association-of-associations argument from the same Basic Guide.
- **how it works:** Ask around: who knows everyone? Who do people trust?, Invite four to eight of them to a recurring, informal table., Share what gift conversations are surfacing., Make introductions deliberately — and inventory the associations., Let the table notice what the neighborhood could do next with what it has.
- **min resources:** Free. Coffee.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** introductions happen weekly, on purpose, associations start collaborating with each other, the table can name the gifts of the block's most overlooked residents
- **connector traits:** gift-centered, well-connected, trusted, believes in the community, joyful about convening, goes guarantor for the marginalized
