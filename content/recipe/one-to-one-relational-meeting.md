# The One-to-One

> A scheduled 30–60 minute conversation between two people, built on stories rather than small talk, that ends with a commitment. The basic unit of organizing.

*The need:* You can't organize people you don't know, and you don't know people until you know why they care.

The one-to-one meeting is the foundational tool of relational organizing: a deliberate, scheduled conversation — usually 30 to 60 minutes, often over coffee — whose purpose is to build a public relationship, not to chat. Every 1:1 has four movements:

1. **Purpose** — Be up front about why you're meeting. If you plan to ask for a commitment at the end, say so when you set it up.
2. **Exploration** — Most of the meeting. Ask probing questions to learn the other person's story, values, and resources: "Why is this important enough for you to act?" "Can you remember the first time you stood up for something you believed in?"
3. **Exchange** — Share your own story where it connects. A relationship forms when both people commit their most valuable resource — time — and leave with something: insight, support, a connection.
4. **Commitment** — End with a specific next step together. Never leave without one.

The don'ts matter as much as the dos: don't try to persuade instead of listen; don't skip stories to "get to the point"; don't end without a plan; split the bill (paying makes it feel transactional).

Organizers use three types of 1:1s: **recruitment** (start a relationship), **maintenance** (every two weeks with the people you organize alongside — don't wait for a problem), and **escalation** (invite someone into more responsibility). And because relationships take real attention, each part-time organizer can genuinely maintain about five; a full-timer, about ten. In a neighborhood, the 1:1 is simpler and older than any of this: it's how neighbors become people to each other.

## Details

- **field tips:** Listen 70%, talk 30%, Ask 'why?' more than 'what?', Split the bill, Maintenance 1:1s every two weeks, before problems, not after
- **ingredients:** two people, 30-60 scheduled minutes, a public-ish place (coffee shop, park, porch), a real question and a real ask
- **source note:** Structure (purpose / exploration / exchange / commitment), the three types of 1:1s, and sustainable relationship ratios from 'Organizing: People, Power, Change' (2014). The relational meeting as a named discipline descends from the Industrial Areas Foundation.
- **how it works:** Schedule it and name the purpose up front., Spend most of the time exploring: their story, their values, what they'd bring., Exchange — connect their story to yours; offer something back., End with a specific commitment and a next date., Log who you met and what you learned; follow up within 48 hours.
- **min resources:** Free. Two chairs.
- **practice kind:** recipe
- **success signals:** you can say what they care about and why, they made a commitment, a second meeting exists on a calendar
