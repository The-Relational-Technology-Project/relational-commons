# Facilitation guardrails for a neighborhood deliberation

> What keeps a deliberative tool honest: ground rules on the page, plain language, small groups over open microphones, a named host, room for the quiet and the offline, and a tool that informs decisions but never makes them.

Guardrails to build into the tool and the room, not bolt on after:

- **Ground rules on the page.** One voice at a time; disagree with ideas, not people; what is said here is summarized, not attributed, unless someone asks to be named.
- **Plain language throughout.** The statement deck, the report, and the flyer all read at a middle-school level, in every language the block speaks.
- **Small-group formats over open microphones.** Tables of four to six with a shared prompt beat a line at the mic.
- **A named host and steward.** A person and an organization on every public page: who convenes, who answers, who carries the result forward.
- **Room for the quiet and the offline.** Paper ballots, large print, a phone number, a table at the library; the tool is one door, not the only one.
- **Never let the tool auto-decide.** It informs and records human decisions; the people name the groups, write the summary, and choose what goes to the decision-maker.
- **Honest about signal.** Participation is not representativeness. Say who was heard and who was not wherever results appear.
- **Close the loop.** Read the report aloud where it matters, carry it to the hearing, bring it back to the people who took part.
