# The Sweet Spot Test for Tactics

> A good action does three things at once: makes measurable progress toward the goal, strengthens the organization, and develops the people who carry it out. Two out of three is a warning sign.

*The need:* Busy groups mistake activity for progress and burn through volunteers doing tactics that build nothing.

Before committing to any action — a canvass, a petition, a community dinner, an app — run it through the sweet spot test. A tactic is worth doing when it is:

1. **Strategic** — it produces concrete, measurable progress toward the campaign goal;
2. **Organization-strengthening** — it attracts and engages new people and increases the community's capacity to work together;
3. **Individual-developing** — the people who carry it out grow in skill, leadership, and confidence.

The sweet spot is where all three overlap. A tactic that hits the goal but chews people up costs you the next campaign. A tactic that's fun but strategically pointless spends trust. 

The companion practice is **motivational engagement** — designing the work so people want to do it again. Motivational actions are *meaningful* (the person can see the difference it makes), grant *autonomy* (responsibility matched to skill, with room to make real choices), and build in *feedback and learning* (progress is visible; coaching is available). Five design checks: Does the person do a whole thing start to finish? Do they see its impact? Does it use varied skills — head, heart, hands? Do they own decisions? Can they see results as they work?

Nearly any task can be redesigned to meet these criteria — turning data entry into scoreboard-keeping, or a lone flyer route into a paired story-collecting walk. People don't leave because work is hard. They leave because it's meaningless, invisible, or lonely.
