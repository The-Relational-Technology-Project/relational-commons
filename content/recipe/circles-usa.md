# Circles USA

> A long-term mentoring model that builds intentional friendships across income lines. People in poverty matched with Allies for 18+ months of weekly meetings.

## What it is

A community model where "Circle Leaders" (people living in or near poverty) are matched with "Allies" (volunteers from middle- or upper-income backgrounds) for a minimum of 18 months of weekly meetings. The relationship provides mentorship and access to the Ally's network, while the broader Circles group provides community, training, and accountability.

The model is grounded in research showing that the strongest predictor of someone moving out of poverty is the strength and diversity of their social network. Money helps; relationships across class lines change the topology of opportunity.

## Why it works

Most "anti-poverty" interventions move dollars or services. Circles moves relationships. Long-term, weekly, intentional friendship across class lines does something specific: it gives Circle Leaders direct access to networks they would not otherwise enter (job leads, professional advice, school connections, social capital). It also disrupts the patterns of Allies who think of poverty as a problem of "those people" rather than a structural condition that everyone is implicated in.

The 18-month minimum matters. Most cross-class relationships die in the first three months because the gulf is too wide. Sustained over 18 months, the relationship deepens past charity into mutual friendship.

## What you need

- **Time:** 18+ months minimum. Weekly meetings of 1 to 2 hours.
- **People:** Circle Leaders, Allies, group facilitators, program coordinators. Diverse socioeconomic mix is essential.
- **Space:** A regular community location for weekly group gatherings. One-on-one meetings often happen at restaurants, coffee shops, or homes.
- **Materials:** Curriculum guides, communication tools, network maps, goal-tracking resources. [Circles USA](https://www.circlesusa.org/) provides licensed materials.
- **Skills:** Relationship building, active listening, cross-class communication, goal facilitation.
- **Energy level:** Moderate to high. The weekly commitment is real.

## How to host it

1. **Connect with [Circles USA](https://www.circlesusa.org/).** This is one of the recipes in the book that benefits significantly from formal training and a curriculum. The national organization licenses the model and supports local chapters with materials, training, and evaluation.
2. **Build the host organization.** Most local Circles are anchored in a community organization, faith community, or social service agency that can provide space, coordination, and continuity.
3. **Recruit and train Circle Leaders.** People living in or near poverty who are ready for an 18-month commitment to a relational program. The screening is mutual: this isn't for everyone.
4. **Recruit and train Allies.** Volunteers from middle- or upper-income backgrounds. The training is significant. It addresses class assumptions, helps Allies see structural poverty rather than personal failing, and prepares them for the long arc of the relationship.
5. **Match thoughtfully.** Circle Leader and Ally pairings are made carefully, with attention to compatibility, scheduling, and proximity. Not all matches stick; some are re-paired in the first months.
6. **Hold weekly group meetings.** All Circle Leaders and Allies gather weekly. Includes a shared meal, education on relevant topics (employment, finance, mental health, parenting), and time for pair meetings.
7. **Run pair meetings.** Circle Leaders and Allies meet weekly outside the group time. Goal-setting, network introductions, mutual learning. The Ally is not a savior; they are a partner.
8. **Track outcomes.** Most Circles programs track income, employment, education, and network growth. National data shows 71% average income increase after 18 months, alongside expanded networks and life satisfaction.

## Variations

**Workplace Circles.** Some employers host Circles as a benefit, pairing employees in financial distress with executive-level Allies.

**Single Mothers' Circles.** A variant focused on single-parent households. Cohort dynamics produce additional peer support.

**Returning citizen Circles.** Adapted for people coming out of incarceration, where the network gap is especially severe.

**Urban and rural variants.** Circles operates in both contexts. The dynamics differ; the structure adapts.

## Stories from neighborhoods

Circles operates in multiple cities including Indianapolis, Baltimore, Denver, and Northwest Arkansas. National evaluation data shows substantial poverty-reduction outcomes alongside the relational outcomes that participants name as the deepest impact.

A common pattern: Allies report that Circles changed them as much as it changed Circle Leaders. The Ally's understanding of poverty shifts from individual moral failing to systemic condition. Their actions in their own networks change. The work expands beyond the pair.
