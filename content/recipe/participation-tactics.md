# Participation Tactics

> Twenty-nine small moves that turn a gathering from one-mic-and-a-hundred-listeners into a room where everyone speaks.

## What it is

Not one recipe, but a toolkit. Specific facilitation moves that shift gatherings toward distributed participation. Things like opening with a warm-up that requires everyone to talk and laugh in the first ten minutes. Using "1-2-4-All" so people think privately, then share with a partner, then a small group, then the whole room. Rotating who makes announcements. Embedding "raise your hand if..." moments throughout a presentation.

The underlying frame is moving from "Hub" structures (power centered on a presenter) toward "Web" structures (everyone connecting to each other).

## Why it works

Most meetings default to a single speaker model that consolidates power in whoever holds the mic. The default model also lets quieter people stay invisible. Tactics that explicitly distribute speaking, listening, and decision-making produce more participation without anyone needing to be braver than they already are.

The key principle: structure quiet warm-ups before high-stakes participation. By the time the whole-group conversation happens, everyone has already spoken to one other person and a small group. The big share is the third or fourth time they've used their voice in the session.

## What you need

- **Time:** Variable. Tactics range from 5-minute warm-ups to full-session designs.
- **People:** Works at any group size from 5 to 200+.
- **Space:** Flexible. Works in-person and online.
- **Materials:** Depends on the tactic. Timers, notecards, flip charts, shared documents.
- **Skills:** Comfort with facilitation, awareness of power dynamics, willingness to experiment.
- **Energy level:** Moderate to high. Active facilitation is required.

## A starter set of tactics

### Openings

1. **Everyone talks and laughs in the first 10 minutes.** A short warm-up question. People at tables of 4 take turns. By 10 minutes in, every voice has been heard at small scale.
2. **Collaborative agenda check.** Show the agenda. Ask: anything missing, anything you'd cut, anything that needs more time? Adjust on the fly.
3. **Rotate the welcome.** Different people open each meeting. Different person makes announcements.
4. **Pulse check.** Quick reading of the room: "Show me on your fingers how present you are right now, one to five." Useful temperature read.

### Discussions and shares

5. **1-2-4-All.** Pose a question. Each person thinks for 2 minutes (silent, written). Pair-share for 4 minutes. Groups of 4 for 6 minutes. Whole group for 10 minutes. Builds confidence before anyone has to risk speaking publicly.
6. **Raise your hand if...** Embed throughout a talk. Forces engagement without requiring speech.
7. **Rotating speakers.** Multiple voices presenting in turn instead of one long presentation.
8. **Unconference style.** See [Open Space Technology](../methodology/open-space.md).

### Decision-making

9. **Warm up with low-stakes practice.** Don't try a new decision method on a high-stakes decision. Use it on something easy first.
10. **Multiple modes.** Some people decide better in conversation, some after time alone with the materials. Build in both.
11. **Dot voting.** Each participant gets a few stickers; place them on the options they support. Quick, visual, distributed.
12. **Polls plus discussion.** Take a quick poll. Show the room the distribution. Then discuss. Surfacing the spread changes the conversation.

### Throughout

13. **Stack the speakers list.** Track who has spoken. Invite voices that haven't. Don't shame the quiet ones; just open space.
14. **Move bodies.** People standing, walking, moving to corners by their position. Movement shifts attention.
15. **Build in silence.** Two or three minutes of genuine silence after a hard question. Real thought takes longer than the social pressure to fill space.

(There are many more. The full list and the philosophy behind them is at [grouphug.substack.com/p/pluralize-the-power](https://grouphug.substack.com/p/pluralize-the-power-group-participation-ideas).)

## Variations

The tactics combine. Most well-facilitated gatherings use 4 to 8 of them across the day. The trick is to use them deliberately, not as gimmicks. Each tactic is solving a specific problem (quiet voices, unequal access to the mic, decision-making without enough preparation). Match the tactic to the problem.

## Stories from neighborhoods

**Oakland's participatory budgeting process** shifted from public hearings (where mostly the already-powerful spoke) to participatory design using many of these tactics: group warm-ups, small-group conversations, 1-2-4-All for brainstorming, rotating facilitators. Participation more than tripled.

A quieter community member said it directly: *"I never would have spoken up, but starting with a pair conversation made it feel safe. By the time we talked as a whole group, I had things to say."*
