# The Hard Ask (and the Three Nos)

> Ask for a specific commitment — a date, a time, a role — without apologizing. Organizing is an opportunity, not a favour. And learn to hear the difference between 'not now', 'not that', and 'not ever'.

*The need:* Most people never join because nobody actually asked them.

A hard ask is a request that results in a commitment to a specific action. "Would you maybe be interested in getting involved sometime?" is a soft ask; it protects the asker and loses the person. A hard ask sounds like: *"Can you come to our team meeting Wednesday at 6pm at Alisha's?"*

The practices:
- **Be concise, plain, and specific.** If it's an event, include date, time, and place. If it's a role, name the role.
- **Never apologize for asking.** You are not imposing a burden; you are offering someone a way to act on what they already care about. Organizing is an opportunity, not a favour.
- **Convey urgency, and make them the solution:** "We need one more canvass lead to hit our goal. Will you take it on?"
- **Then stop talking and let them answer.**

When the answer is no, listen for which no it is. **"Not now"** — offer another time ("we also meet Sunday at 1, can you make that?"). **"Not that"** — offer another way in ("no door-knocking? come help plan the welcome night instead"). **"Not ever"** — thank them warmly and move on; no guilt, theirs or yours.

And secure what you win: follow up within 48 hours, never let anyone leave without knowing the next time you'll see them, confirm commitments so people know you're counting on them — and plan for half your yeses to no-show (need four people? schedule eight). The deepest reason people don't return isn't overwork — it's that no one explained why their work mattered, or no one asked.
