# Microgrant Organizer Toolkit

> With Neighbors' nine-stage guide to designing and running a neighborhood microgrant program, from reflecting on your local context through funding more rounds — built on the permission / accountability / commitment model, with worksheets and templates, over a roughly four-month cycle. The practice half of the pair; the Microgrant Program System is the software half.

With Neighbors' complete guide to designing and running a neighborhood microgrant program — small grants ($50–$200) that fund neighbors to host gatherings. This commons entry summarizes the toolkit and links to the full version, which is free at [withneighbors.org/toolkit](https://withneighbors.org/toolkit), including downloadable worksheets and templates.

It is the practice half of a pair: the software that manages applications, review, emails, and payouts is the [Microgrant Program System](/commons/e/microgrant-program-system), described feature-by-feature in this same collection so you can remix it for your place.

## Why microgrants

A small grant does three things for a would-be host, beyond covering pizza: it grants **permission** (someone said yes to your idea), creates **accountability** (money received makes the gathering real), and invites **commitment** (hosts who gather once tend to gather again). The money is the smallest part of what changes hands.

## The nine stages

1. **Introduction** — what a neighborhood microgrant program is and why $50–$200 is enough to matter.
2. **Reflecting on your local context** — reading your place before designing the grant: who gathers already, who doesn't, and what "gathering" means here.
3. **Setting up the grant** — criteria, amounts, eligibility, and the mechanics of how money will actually move.
4. **Launching + promoting the opportunity** — getting the invitation in front of neighbors who would never call themselves "organizers."
5. **Grant review + notifying applicants** — deciding well, declining kindly, and telling everyone where they stand.
6. **Host support** — what hosts need between "approved" and "gathered": checklists, encouragement, and someone to ask.
7. **Storytelling, archival + follow-ups** — collecting photos, stories, and reflections; closing the loop with hosts and funders.
8. **Fund more microgrants!** — sustaining and growing the program after the first round proves the case.
9. **Case studies + credit for inspiration** — real programs to learn from, and the lineage this work builds on.

The toolkit recommends roughly a **four-month cycle** from designing the grant to gathered stories.

## Using it with the program system

Stages 2–4 shape what your remixed program's public site says; stage 5 is the system's review pipeline and email templates; the money mechanics in stage 3 become your payout rail (PayPal, Venmo, gift cards, or checks); stage 7 is the built-in reflection flow — where your program's own question becomes its theory of change.

## Where this has run

The toolkit distills programs run with With Neighbors partners across the country — parent meetups in Seattle, an island civic association, a city joy fund, Rhode Island driveway parties (Pizza Strip Fund), and Vermont farm dinners (Long-Handled Spoons Dinners, with NOFA-VT). Organizers running larger programs can talk with the With Neighbors crew about hands-on support and matched funding — see [withneighbors.org](https://withneighbors.org).
