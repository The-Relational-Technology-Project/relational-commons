# Starting tensions: name the one your question sits on

> A neighborhood question almost always sits on a real tension. Naming it early keeps the process honest instead of adversarial: belonging and freedom, care and self-reliance, voice and speed, safety and welcome, preservation and change, privacy and knowing each other.

Before designing prompts or picking a tool, name the tension underneath the question, and design so both poles stay respectable. The goal is a room that can disagree well, not a poll that declares a winner.

- **Belonging and freedom**: everyone's welcome, nobody's obligated. How do we hold both?
- **Care and self-reliance**: leaning on each other without anyone feeling managed.
- **Voice and speed**: hearing everyone, and still deciding in time to matter.
- **Safety and welcome**: feeling secure without turning the block inward.
- **Preservation and change**: what we keep, what we let grow.
- **Privacy and knowing each other**: being known without being watched.

Rough cues: newcomers, gentrification, cliques point at belonging and freedom; elders, check-ins, meals at care and self-reliance; meetings, committees, consensus at voice and speed; traffic, cameras, patrols at safety and welcome; housing, zoning, trees at preservation and change; cameras, data, Nextdoor at privacy and knowing each other. A suggestion the group can override, never a classification.

The first three come from RTP's Deliberation Studio work; the rest extend the same move.
