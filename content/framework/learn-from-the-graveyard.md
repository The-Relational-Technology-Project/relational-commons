# Learn from the Graveyard

> The Civic Tech Field Guide keeps a cemetery of dead projects, and hyperlocal tech fills a whole row. The patterns behind the headstones are the most useful design guidance this shelf has.

The Civic Tech Field Guide's Graveyard commemorates hundreds of dead civic tech projects 'for research and field learning.' Its stewards name the uncomfortable patterns: most innovation projects fail; funding success doesn't guarantee project success; and social networks for local information fail *repeatedly*.

The hyperlocal row, with lessons attached:

- **EveryBlock** (2007–2013): Adrian Holovaty's block-level news feeds — Knight-funded, widely admired, shut down by its corporate parent NBC with no notice. Data feeds about neighbors aren't community, and corporate parents kill hyperlocal on a whim.
- **Neighborland** (2011–2019): a beloved participatory-planning platform used by 200+ agencies — acquired by Nextdoor and closed. Communities that don't own the platform lose the archive of their own participation.
- **PledgeBank** (mySociety, 2005–2015): 'I'll do it if N others join me' — mutual commitment productized. It worked at small scale, repeatedly, and still couldn't sustain itself as a destination site. Commitment mechanics want to live *inside* an existing community, not on their own island.
- **The sharing-site cluster** (StreetBank, OpenShed, Favabank, OurGoods): stand-alone borrow-a-ladder platforms die of empty-marketplace loneliness. Sharing thrives where a trusted forum already exists — Front Porch Forum's borrowed ladders outlived every dedicated sharing site.
- **The disaster-born tools** (Red Hook Hub, Recovers.org): coordination tech born in crisis evaporates between crises unless it gets a peacetime job.

The survivors share a shape: owned by or accountable to their community (Front Porch Forum's public benefit corporation, Loomio's worker co-op, Decidim's association, Amsterdam's Gebiedonline cooperative), paid stewardship rather than pure volunteerism (Detroit's digital stewards, City Bureau's paid Documenters), and modesty of scope. Before building anything, visit the graveyard and find your idea's headstone — then design against its cause of death.

## Details

- **source note:** Graveyard framing and project fates per the Civic Tech Field Guide (CC BY-NC-SA); EveryBlock and Neighborland details per Holovaty's own post-mortem and CTFG's entries.
- **practice kind:** framework
- **survivor patterns:** community ownership (PBC, co-op, association), paid local stewardship, modest scope with a peacetime job, embed in existing trust rather than build a destination
