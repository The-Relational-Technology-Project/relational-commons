# To, For, With, By

> Cormac Russell's four modes of change: things done TO communities, FOR them, WITH them, and BY them. Community power lives in 'by' — and the ordering of help matters more than the amount.

A four-word audit for any community effort — including any tool you're about to build:

- **To**: done to communities, without consent. Coercive, however well dressed.
- **For**: done for communities — benevolent and well-intentioned, but professionals and outsiders stay in the driver's seat, and residents learn to be clients.
- **With**: done alongside communities, in more equal and reciprocal relationships.
- **By**: communities as the architects and drivers of their own change, with outside support playing a supplementary role.

Asset-based community development's home is *by*. The discipline that keeps it there is an ordering rule from John McKnight and Cormac Russell: first ask what residents can do themselves; then what they can do with a little outside help; and only then what they need outside agencies to do for them. The order is critical — start with the third question and you preclude citizen power before the work begins.

Russell's summary of the whole stance: if we want to help people in a way that does no harm to their capacities, start with what's strong, not with what's wrong.

For builders: every feature can be sorted with the same four words. A dashboard about a neighborhood is *to*; a service portal is *for*; a co-designed tool is *with*; a tool neighbors can run, change, and own is *by*.

## Details

- **source note:** The four modes per Russell's community-driven development framework; the ordering rule verbatim in spirit from 'The Four Essential Elements of an ABCD Process' (CC BY-NC 4.0).
- **practice kind:** framework
- **ordering questions:** What can communities do best themselves?, What do communities require help with?, What do communities need outside agencies to do for them?
