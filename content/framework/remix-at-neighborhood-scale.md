# Remix at Neighborhood Scale

> The thesis of this shelf: civic tech doesn't have to be built for your neighborhood to work in your neighborhood. Global open-source platforms, proven civic patterns, and other places' stories are all remixable at block scale.

Most civic technology is built for cities, countries, or the whole world. Almost all of it can be *used* smaller.

Ushahidi was built for crisis mapping across Kenya — and it can map the third places of one neighborhood. FixMyStreet was built for reporting potholes to councils across the UK — and its pattern (see something, report it, watch it get fixed in public) works for a block's broken streetlights or a park's maintenance backlog. Participatory budgeting was born across Porto Alegre — and it works on a single ward's discretionary fund, or a block association's $500.

Three kinds of remixable material:

1. **Open-source platforms** — software you can run for your own place (Ushahidi, Decidim, LocalWiki, Loomio). The license is the permission slip; the neighborhood use case is yours to invent.
2. **Patterns** — designs proven somewhere and rebuildable anywhere, even in an afternoon with an AI builder: adopt-a-hydrant's 'civic objects with volunteer stewards', FixMyStreet's public issue ledger, a timebank's hour-for-hour exchange.
3. **Stories** — what neighbors in other places actually did, which is where the judgment lives: what they built, what they skipped, why it worked.

Two disciplines keep the remix honest. First, the technology is the smallest part — the map is the 10%, the door-knocking is the 90%; every tool on this shelf assumes organizing around it (that's the other shelf). Second, learn from the graveyard: civic tech has a rich history of dead projects, and the Civic Tech Field Guide keeps the cemetery well-labeled. The pattern behind most headstones: tech that substituted for relationships instead of strengthening them, or maintenance nobody committed to. Remix accordingly.

- **disciplines:** the tech is the 10% — organizing is the 90%, learn from the graveyard
- **remix kinds:** open-source platforms, patterns, stories
