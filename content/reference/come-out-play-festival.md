## What It Is

An annual festival and year-round organization that promotes new forms of street games and urban play. Through festivals and recurring community events, Come Out & Play transforms public spaces into interactive playgrounds, bringing together game designers and diverse players for innovative gaming experiences.

## How It Works

Come Out & Play organizes multiple types of events throughout the year. The flagship festival features themed days including 'After Dark' (evening games), 'Field Day' (outdoor activities), and 'Family Day' (kid-friendly options). Events showcase short, interactive games designed specifically for public spaces and social interaction. Beyond the annual festival, the organization runs recurring community play sessions like 'Thursday Night Play,' featuring weekly rotating games in locations like the DUMBO Archway in New York City. Game designers and community members collaborate to create games that activate public spaces, build cross-demographic interactions, and revitalize street culture. Events range from large-scale festivals to intimate neighborhood gatherings, all emphasizing accessibility, novelty, and the joy of shared public play.

## Who 'S Done It

Come Out & Play has grown from New York City roots to organize events across multiple cities including Pittsburgh and San Francisco. Thousands of participants have engaged through festival events and recurring community play sessions. Players report renewed appreciation for public space and unexpected connections with neighbors. Game designers have used the platform to experiment with urban play concepts that influence broader play design fields. The Thursday Night Play series demonstrates sustained community engagement, with regular attendees building relationships across repeated play sessions.

## Connections

Directly descended from the New Games Movement philosophy. Relates to Bernard DeKoven's work on playfulness and community. Influences and draws from contemporary game design culture, interactive art movements, and civic placemaking initiatives. Part of broader 'play in public space' global movements including festivals in Europe and Latin America.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Recurring sessions: 1-2 hours; festivals: 1-3 days; requires ongoing coordination
- *People*: 10-20 core organizers; 50-500+ participants at events depending on scale
- *Space*: Public outdoor spaces—parks, plazas, streets; accessibility to diverse neighborhoods
- *Materials*: Game designs, props, signage, sound equipment, rules sheets, promotional materials
- *Skills*: Game design, event facilitation, public engagement, community outreach, logistics management
- *Energy Level*: High; requires creative energy, sustained community engagement, and significant event coordination
