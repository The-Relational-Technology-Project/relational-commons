# Open Space Technology

## What It Is

A self-organizing conference methodology created by Harrison Owen that replaces pre-planned agendas with participant-driven topics. Groups gather around a compelling central question, and attendees themselves propose and facilitate the sessions they want to participate in, guided by four principles and one law of movement.

## How It Works

To run Open Space, first identify a clear, compelling theme—a question, challenge, or purpose that matters to participants. Gather the group in a large circle and introduce the theme and the four principles: Whoever comes is the right people; Whatever happens is the only thing that could have been; Whenever it starts is the right time; When it's over it's over. Present the Law of Two Feet: if you find yourself not learning or contributing, move to somewhere more productive. Next, open a marketplace where participants post session topics on a board with time and location. Participants then attend sessions of their choosing, free to move between sessions using their two feet. Facilitators are typically responsible for creating a marketplace wall, managing logistics, and ensuring note-taking. Sessions unfold organically with minimal structure. Open Space works particularly well when no single entity has all answers and diverse perspectives are essential to solving the challenge.

## Who 'S Done It

Harrison Owen developed Open Space Technology in response to his observation that the most valuable moments in conferences occurred during coffee breaks. The method has been used globally by organizations ranging from corporate strategy sessions to international development projects. In the UN, the World Health Organization, and across nonprofits and municipalities, groups have applied Open Space to address climate change, education reform, and community resilience. It has been deployed with groups from 5 to 2000+ participants in over 140 countries.

## Connections

Open Space Technology emerged from Owen's reflection on systems theory, dialogue practices, and community organizing traditions. It connects to self-organization principles found in complex adaptive systems theory and to participatory democracy movements. It's frequently used as one component within larger Art of Hosting frameworks.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2 hours minimum; typically 4 hours to 3 days
- *People*: 5-2000+ participants; diversity of perspectives crucial
- *Space*: Large gathering space plus multiple breakout rooms; bulletin board for marketplace
- *Materials*: Index cards, markers, large wall/bulletin board, name tags, note-taking materials
- *Skills*: Understanding of self-organization, comfort with uncertainty, ability to let go of control
- *Energy Level*: High; requires active movement and decision-making
