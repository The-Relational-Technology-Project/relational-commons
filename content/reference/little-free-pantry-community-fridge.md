# Little Free Pantry / Community Fridge

> A small accessible structure stocked by community members and placed in a public location where anyone can take or leave food and supplies without paperwork, judgment, or eligibility requirements. It runs on a gift economy: people give what they can and take what they need.

## What It Is

A small accessible structure stocked by community members and placed in a public location where anyone can take or leave food and supplies without paperwork, judgment, or eligibility requirements. It runs on a gift economy: people give what they can and take what they need.

## How It Works

Secure permission from the property owner and research local zoning. Choose a high-traffic, visible location with safe access at all hours. Build or repurpose your structure — wood cabinet, old bookcase, or purchased kit — weatherproof it. Stock regularly with nonperishable foods (cans, pasta, rice, beans), hygiene products (menstrual products, soap, toothpaste), and household items. If using a fridge, include perishables. Build community awareness through door-to-door conversations and social media. Maintain weekly by removing damaged items. The pantry runs on a gift economy: people give what they can and take what they need.

## Who 'S Done It

Jessica McClard of Fayetteville, Arkansas noticed Little Free Libraries popping up and had an idea: what if we did the same for food? In May 2016, with a $250 microloan, she installed the first Little Free Pantry on her church grounds. Today, thousands exist across North America. The movement proved especially vital during COVID-19 and demonstrated that neighbors are eager to care for one another when given simple, dignified structures to do so.

## Connections

Builds on the Little Free Library movement and emerges from gift economy principles, mutual aid traditions, and food security work. Connects to food justice, food sovereignty, and direct mutual aid as alternative to institutional charity. Reflects participatory economics, commons stewardship, and the understanding that dignity matters — people access food without applications or shame.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 weeks setup, 1-2 hours weekly maintenance
- *People*: 1-2 organizers, 5-10 community volunteers for stocking
- *Space*: Public or semi-public location with high foot traffic
- *Materials*: Pantry/fridge structure, weatherproofing, shelving, food and supplies
- *Skills*: Basic construction or repurposing, community organizing
- *Energy Level*: Low-to-medium
