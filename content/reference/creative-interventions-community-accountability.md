# Creative Interventions (Community Accountability)

## What It Is

A framework and toolkit for communities to respond to interpersonal violence — abuse, assault, harassment — without relying on police or the criminal justice system. It centers survivors, mobilizes friends and family, and sometimes includes the person who caused harm in a process of accountability and transformation.

## How It Works

The toolkit provides step-by-step guides for different roles. For survivors: think through what you need, who you trust, what safety looks like. For friends/family: concrete ways to support — believing them, offering practical help, not pushing toward any particular choice. For someone who caused harm: accountability practices — understanding impact, taking responsibility, changing behavior. For the broader community: creating conditions where violence is less likely. The process: 1) name what happened clearly, 2) identify who should be involved (survivor decides), 3) direct conversations, 4) accountability plan, 5) monitoring and support, 6) evaluation. It's slow, hard, relational work — not a quick fix, but a genuine alternative.

## Who 'S Done It

Creative Interventions started in 2004 in Oakland, California, from activist communities and survivor-led movements. Since then, thousands worldwide have used the toolkit — activists, survivors, advocates, faith communities. The toolkit has been translated into Spanish and French, with German and Japanese in progress. Communities report that this approach honors survivors more fully, holds people accountable more meaningfully, and sometimes transforms harm rather than containing it.

## Connections

Draws on survivor activism, particularly from communities of color and LGBTQ+ communities who recognized that calling police often meant further harm. Connects to abolition, transformative justice, Indigenous restorative practices, and relational ethics. Strengthens when embedded in strong community relationships — block parties, co-ops, and neighborhood gatherings create trust that makes accountability possible. It's the deepening of community that's only possible when people already know each other.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Weeks to months for accountability processes; survivor support is ongoing
- *People*: Survivor, 2-8 trusted people, sometimes the person who caused harm, possibly a trained facilitator
- *Space*: Safe, private space for conversations
- *Materials*: Creative Interventions Toolkit (free online), facilitator guides
- *Skills*: Deep listening, conflict facilitation, understanding of trauma and power
- *Energy Level*: High — emotional intensity; shared among multiple people
