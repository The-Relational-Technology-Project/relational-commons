## What It Is

A philosophy and practical process for transforming public spaces into vibrant community places through intensive community engagement, observation, and iterative design. Rather than expert-driven planning, placemaking centers the wisdom and aspirations of people who live, work, and play in a space.

## How It Works

Placemaking begins with deep observation and listening. Spend time on site studying how people use the space, what assets exist, and what's missing. Conduct interviews and conversations with diverse community members—residents, workers, merchants, visitors. Meet with stakeholders to understand their visions and constraints. Synthesize observations into a vision statement articulating what the place could become. Create a site plan reflecting community priorities, then move into action through 'lighter, quicker, cheaper' experiments: temporary parklets, pop-up activities, repainting, community gardens, or programming that test ideas before full implementation. Monitor what works, gather feedback, and iterate. Project for Public Spaces guides this through their five-step process: meet community and identify stakeholders, assess site and assets, create vision, develop implementation plan, and manage ongoing activation. The community becomes both design partner and steward.

## Who 'S Done It

Project for Public Spaces (PPS), founded in 1975, has pioneered placemaking methodology and documented hundreds of transformations. They've worked on urban squares in major cities, neighborhood parks, streetscapes, and waterfront areas worldwide. Cities including New York, Portland, San Francisco, and international cities have applied PPS placemaking principles. The organization provides training, consulting, and a robust resource library for communities undertaking their own place transformations.

## Connections

Placemaking builds on Jane Jacobs' observations about successful neighborhoods, urban design principles emphasizing human scale, and participatory design traditions. It connects to community organizing, asset-based community development, and the broader movement toward co-creation in urban planning. It shares philosophy with Art of Hosting regarding collective intelligence and emergence.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-12 months for initial transformation; ongoing stewardship indefinite
- *People*: Community members, local organizations, city partners, 30+ engaged in core work
- *Space*: The public space being transformed; meeting venues for design sessions
- *Materials*: Camera/recording equipment for observation, flip charts for visioning, paint/materials for experiments, photography documentation
- *Skills*: Community engagement, observation, facilitation, design thinking, project management
- *Energy Level*: Moderate to high; includes physical activity (building, painting) and strategic thinking
