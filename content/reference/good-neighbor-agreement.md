# Good Neighbor Agreement

> A non-binding written agreement developed collaboratively between a neighborhood and a business or institution, designed to address specific concerns through mutual understanding and compromise. Good Neighbor Agreements formalize what neighbors and businesses want from each other

## What It Is

A non-binding written agreement developed collaboratively between a neighborhood and a business or institution, designed to address specific concerns through mutual understanding and compromise. Good Neighbor Agreements formalize what neighbors and businesses want from each other and create ongoing channels for communication.

## How It Works

Start with a neighborhood organization with legitimacy in the area. Organize listening sessions to understand what residents care about — not vague complaints but specifics (noise hours, parking, waste, local employment). Document everything. Then convene a negotiation meeting with neighborhood reps and business leadership. Each side explains perspective, constraints, and goals. The agreement addresses specifics: operating hours, noise management, local hiring, waste disposal, process for problems, regular check-in meetings. It's explicitly non-binding — not a legal contract but a good-faith understanding. Both parties sign. Schedule regular check-ins (quarterly or annual) to discuss how it's working and adjust. Examples from CHUN Denver include agreements with restaurants, dispensaries, and shelters.

## Who 'S Done It

CHUN (Capitol Hill United Neighborhoods) in Denver has facilitated Good Neighbor Agreements since 2010. Park Tavern, a bar in a residential neighborhood, was generating late-night noise concerns. Instead of hostility, they proposed a Good Neighbor Agreement. The negotiation resulted in: 11pm noise cutoff on weeknights, designated parking areas, a resident contact person, quarterly check-ins. The bar also hired from the neighborhood and created a community advisory group. Several years later, when a new building added residents nearby, the bar proactively suggested updating the agreement.

## Connections

Rooted in community organizing and environmental justice traditions, particularly around managing conflicts between different land uses. They connect to community benefit agreements, participatory planning, and conflict resolution. They're a formalization of the principle that neighbors have stake in how businesses operate in their community. They complement neighborhood mapping and mutual aid by institutionalizing ongoing relationship.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-6 months of negotiation, ongoing annual check-ins
- *People*: 3-5 community organizers, neighborhood residents, business leadership
- *Space*: Neutral meeting space
- *Materials*: Template agreement, notes from listening sessions, written documentation
- *Skills*: Negotiation, listening to multiple perspectives, willingness to compromise
- *Energy Level*: Moderate — emotionally demanding to hold both perspectives
