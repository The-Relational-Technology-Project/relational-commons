## What It Is

A global organization serving 'opportunity youth' (ages 16-24, or 15-29 in some countries) who are out of school and unemployed. YouthBuild combines education, job training, construction skills, and community leadership development, with young people building actual housing projects while earning credentials.

## How It Works

YouthBuild operates through an integrated model connecting education and construction: (1) Youth enroll in comprehensive programs addressing education and employment simultaneously. (2) Students work on real construction projects that build affordable housing, community facilities, or renovations—gaining hands-on trades training (construction, electrical, plumbing, etc.). (3) Parallel classroom instruction supports high school diploma or GED completion plus additional skill training (healthcare, IT, retail as alternatives). (4) Leadership development emphasizes community service, civic participation, and personal development. (5) Job placement and continued support help transition youth into employment. The dual focus ensures youth develop both practical construction skills and educational credentials while building something tangible for their communities. Leadership development connects housing work to broader community benefit.

## Who 'S Done It

YouthBuild operates 340+ programs across seven countries, serving tens of thousands of young people. In the U.S., 84% of enrollees earn high school diplomas or equivalent credentials. Alumni demonstrate increased economic mobility, stable employment, and community leadership. Participants describe experiencing 'a place to grow, a place to achieve things you never thought you could achieve.' The model has successfully positioned opportunity youth as solutions—building both physical infrastructure and human potential.

## Connections

Emerges from youth development, workforce development, and housing movements. Relates to affordable housing initiatives, trades training programs, and youth leadership development. Part of broader movements recognizing young people's capacity as assets rather than problems.
