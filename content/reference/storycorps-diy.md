## What It Is

A community-based oral history program where organizations conduct recorded interviews between community members, preserving voices, building connections, and creating archives of lived experience. StoryCorps DIY provides training and resources for organizations to implement their own storytelling projects.

## How It Works

Organizations using StoryCorps DIY first complete training through self-paced online courses covering interview fundamentals, recording technology, archiving, and community engagement. Recruit interview pairs from your community—often elders paired with younger generations, or community members from different backgrounds. Prepare interviewers with question guides and listening practices, emphasizing open-ended questions, patience with silence, and genuine curiosity. Conduct interviews in quiet, comfortable spaces, typically 30-45 minutes. Record audio (and optionally video). Transcribe or archive recordings in your organizational system. Create community listening events where people hear stories together, building collective understanding and connection. Use stories in publications, presentations, or ongoing programming. StoryCorps DIY emphasizes that storytelling is most powerful when embedded in organizational work—whether in education, advocacy, or community organizing.

## Who 'S Done It

StoryCorps, founded in 2003 by Dave Isay, has collected over 75,000 interviews in their flagship mobile recording booths and archive program. StoryCorps DIY, their self-directed training program, has supported hundreds of organizations—schools, nonprofits, libraries, and community groups—in creating sustainable storytelling programs. Libraries have used DIY to document local history; schools have embedded it in civics curriculum; environmental organizations have preserved indigenous knowledge; immigrant organizations have honored family journeys.

## Connections

StoryCorps DIY draws from oral history traditions, participatory action research, and therapeutic listening practices. It connects to community documentation movements, narrative approaches to social change, and traditions of elders passing wisdom to younger generations. It shares philosophy with narrative-based organizing and counter-story movements.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-4 weeks for training; 6-12 months for sustained program
- *People*: 2-4 core organizers; 10+ interview pairs and willing listeners
- *Space*: Quiet recording space; gathering space for listening events
- *Materials*: Recording equipment (smartphone or dedicated recorder), microphone, editing software or transcription service, archive system
- *Skills*: Interviewing, deep listening, audio recording, community facilitation, basic transcription or archiving
- *Energy Level*: Moderate; requires patience, emotional presence, and attention to detail
