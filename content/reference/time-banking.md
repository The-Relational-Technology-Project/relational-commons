## What It Is

A reciprocal exchange system where community members trade services and skills using time-based currency (time credits) rather than money. One hour of service = one time credit, regardless of the type of service, creating systems of mutual aid based on the principle that everyone's time has equal value.

## How It Works

To start or join a timebank, recruit core members (10-30 people) interested in mutual support. Establish clear practices: one hour of service = one time credit. Document all exchanges in a registry or online platform. Services exchanged can include: childcare, gardening, home repair, cooking, transportation, companionship, tutoring, tech support, and countless others. Members post availability and needs; others volunteer services, earning credits they can use when they need support. Use TimeBanks.org platform or create your own tracking system. Regular community meetings—potlucks, skill-shares, celebrations—build relationships beyond transactional exchange. Establish agreements about confidentiality and participation. Many timebanks create local connections by organizing regular gatherings and skill-share events.

## Who 'S Done It

TimeBanks.org, founded by Edgar Cahn in the 1980s, has grown into a global movement with 3+ million hours exchanged through timebanks. Thousands of timebanks operate in neighborhoods, senior centers, schools, and organizations worldwide. Reports show older adults developing 90% new friendships through participation, and members saving $300-$1,200 annually through service exchanges. Timebanks have been particularly powerful in addressing isolation, building intergenerational connection, and creating pathways for participation regardless of income.

## Connections

Time banking builds on principles of reciprocal exchange found in traditional cultures and mutual aid traditions. It connects to alternative economics, local currency movements, and gift economy philosophy. It shares values with barter systems, cooperative economics, and care-centered organizing.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 1-3 months to establish; ongoing indefinitely
- *People*: 10-30 founding members; grows organically
- *Space*: Minimal; uses members' own homes and community spaces
- *Materials*: Tracking system (platform like TimeBanks.org or simple spreadsheet), communication tools, optional gathering space
- *Skills*: Relationship building, community organizing, basic record-keeping, conflict resolution
- *Energy Level*: Low to moderate; emphasizes connection and accessibility
