# Playworks

## What It Is

A national nonprofit organization that partners with schools and community organizations to harness the power of play for children's physical activity, social-emotional development, and academic engagement. Through on-site coaches, staff training, and digital platforms, Playworks embeds play-based strategies into school cultures.

## How It Works

Playworks implements programming through multiple mechanisms: (1) On-site coaches work directly with students during recess, facilitating games and building peer leadership. (2) Staff training programs teach educators play-based strategies for recess and classroom application. (3) SparkPlay, a digital learning platform, provides schools with play-based instructional resources. (4) Consultative partnerships allow districts to customize play integration approaches. Rather than prescribing specific games, Playworks trains adults to facilitate play that naturally builds cooperation, fairness, and communication. Coaches help resolve conflicts through play, develop student leaders, and transform recess from unstructured time into intentional social-emotional learning. Schools track measurable outcomes including physical activity levels, peer relationships, and academic engagement.

## Who 'S Done It

Playworks operates in schools across the U.S., serving tens of thousands of children over nearly 30 years. Research shows that 95% of educators at Playworks schools agree the organization creates engaging learning environments, 94% report increased physical activity, and 96% note improved peer relationships. Teachers report that children participating in Playworks recess show better classroom participation, improved attendance, and higher grades. The model has expanded from individual school partnerships to district-wide implementations.

## Connections

Draws from New Games Movement philosophy and contemporary play theory. Relates to broader movements emphasizing play in education, social-emotional learning, and childhood development. Connects to initiatives exploring recess as critical infrastructure for child well-being and community building.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing; typically 1-2 hour daily recess sessions plus staff training
- *People*: 1-2 on-site coaches per school; whole-school staff participation; all students
- *Space*: School playgrounds, gymnasiums, multipurpose spaces
- *Materials*: Games, sports equipment, conflict resolution tools, digital platform access, training curricula
- *Skills*: Play facilitation, coaching, conflict resolution, leadership development, data tracking
- *Energy Level*: Medium-to-high; requires sustained staff engagement and coach presence
