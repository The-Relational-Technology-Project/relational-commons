# Talking Circles (Indigenous Dialogue Practice)

## What It Is

An Indigenous dialogue practice originating from First Nations traditions where people gather in a circle to listen deeply, share truthfully, and make decisions collectively. Rooted in the sacredness of the circle as representing wholeness and the cycles of life, talking circles ensure all voices are heard equally and honored through structured turn-taking and respectful listening.

## How It Works

Participants sit in a circle (a sacred arrangement in Indigenous traditions). A facilitator or elder often opens with prayer or intention-setting. A talking piece (stick, feather, or sacred object) is held by the person speaking; only the holder may speak. When finished, they pass the talking piece left (clockwise) to the next person. Listeners practice deep listening without interrupting, judging, or planning responses. The circle continues until all have opportunity to speak. No rushing, no cross-talk, no debate—each person speaks from the heart and is fully heard. Decisions emerge through shared understanding, not voting.

## Who 'S Done It

Talking circles originated in First Nations governance and continue in tribal communities. Modern applications include use in schools, universities, conflict resolution centers, drug and alcohol treatment programs, group homes, peace-building initiatives, and community organizations nationwide. Growing adoption as alternative to hierarchical meeting structures.

## Connections

Rooted in First Nations, Indigenous Canadian, and Native American traditions of collective decision-making and justice. Connected to restorative justice, peacemaking circles, council practices, and Indigenous governance models. Part of broader movements to center Indigenous wisdom in community practices and conflict resolution.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 1-3 hours per circle; ongoing practice can be regular
- *People*: Diverse participants, optional facilitator or elder, all with equal voice
- *Space*: Room where circle can be arranged, ideally safe and quiet, accessible
- *Materials*: Talking piece (stick, stone, feather, or other sacred object), optional: candle or altar
- *Skills*: Deep listening, speaking from truth, patience, respect for silence, facilitation (optional)
- *Energy Level*: Moderate—sitting, speaking, listening, emotional presence
