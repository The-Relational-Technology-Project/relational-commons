## What It Is

Formal civic structures where residents organize to advocate for neighborhood interests, improve local conditions, and participate in municipal governance. Neighborhood associations range from grassroots nonprofit organizations to formally-recognized governmental bodies, providing structured infrastructure for collective voice and neighborhood-level decision-making.

## How It Works

Residents within defined geographic boundaries organize into neighborhood associations, often with elected officers, regular meetings, and stated mission addressing neighborhood concerns. Associations may engage in advocacy, community beautification, crime prevention, planning participation, or service provision. Some neighborhoods also have formal neighborhood councils—governmental structures recognized by city code that give neighborhoods official voice in municipal decisions. Both models enable residents to collectively address shared concerns, represent neighborhood interests to city government, and build community.

## Who 'S Done It

Most major US cities have neighborhood association systems. Portland, Oregon has formalized neighborhood associations through city code (Chapter 3.96), with recognized districts and officer structures. Los Angeles has neighborhood councils with formal governmental status. Smaller cities and towns have neighborhood associations that function as advocacy groups and community organizers.

## Connections

Emerged in earnest in late 1980s-early 1990s as cities formalized neighborhood governance structures. Connected to participatory democracy, civic participation, decentralized governance, and neighborhood-level organizing. Related to block associations, civic councils, and community organizing traditions.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Monthly meetings minimum; variable ongoing engagement based on role
- *People*: Resident members, elected officers, committee members, community partners, city liaisons
- *Space*: Regular meeting location (library, school, community center), neighborhood boundaries
- *Materials*: Bylaws, meeting agendas, communication channels, planning documents
- *Skills*: Meeting facilitation, advocacy, community organizing, relationship building
- *Energy Level*: Moderate to high—ongoing meetings, advocacy work, volunteer leadership
