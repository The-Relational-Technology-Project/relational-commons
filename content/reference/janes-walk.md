## What It Is

A global grassroots movement of free, citizen-led walking conversations that celebrate public spaces and neighborhood stories inspired by urbanist Jane Jacobs. Walking groups gather annually (especially around May 4th, Jacobs' birthday) to explore neighborhoods, uncover hidden histories, and build civic dialogue through movement and conversation.

## How It Works

Anyone can organize a Jane's Walk—no formal training required. Organizers plan a walking route through their neighborhood and invite others to join. Walks are not led by experts but by passionate residents who share stories about their communities: local history, culture, challenges, and visions. Walks typically last 1-2 hours, are completely free, and welcome all ages and mobility levels (with options for different paces). No pre-registration required.

## Who 'S Done It

Since 2006 in Toronto, Jane's Walks now occur in over 500 cities globally across North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, and beyond. Walks happen in major cities (New York, London, Vancouver) and small towns, organized by neighborhood residents, community organizations, and cultural institutions.

## Connections

Inspired by Jane Jacobs' 1961 book 'The Death and Life of Great American Cities' which championed community-based city building and the importance of street-level public life. Connects to placemaking, community storytelling, public narrative, and neighborhood-based civic engagement. Related to porch conversations and walking groups as neighboring practices.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 hours per walk; 6+ weeks planning for organizers
- *People*: Walk organizer/leader, diverse neighbors, volunteers to help promote
- *Space*: Neighborhood streets, public spaces, parks—any walkable community area
- *Materials*: Maps, walking route cards, signage, optional printed histories or stories
- *Skills*: Community knowledge, storytelling ability, basic route planning, enthusiasm
- *Energy Level*: Moderate—walking pace, social engagement, storytelling
