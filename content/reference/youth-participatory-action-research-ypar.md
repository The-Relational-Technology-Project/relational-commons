## What It Is

A cyclical research methodology where young people investigate problems affecting their communities, analyze findings collaboratively, and use results to advocate for concrete solutions. YPAR positions youth as legitimate researchers and change agents, transforming who gets to produce knowledge about community issues.

## How It Works

YPAR operates through a connected cycle: (1) Youth collaboratively identify and investigate problems they experience directly—conducting surveys, interviews, focus groups, and collecting data. (2) Participants analyze evidence together, examining patterns and root causes. (3) Youth use research findings to advocate for concrete change through presentations, reports, policy recommendations, or direct action. Facilitators support youth in developing research skills, critical thinking, and evidence-gathering capacity while building what researchers call 'sociopolitical development'—deeper understanding of systemic roots of problems. The process emphasizes that youth are experts in their own experiences and that their knowledge production is valuable for community change.

## Who 'S Done It

YPAR has been successfully implemented in schools, community organizations, and youth-serving agencies, particularly with marginalized young people. Youth researchers have investigated homelessness, school discipline policies, housing discrimination, environmental justice, and other community issues. Projects have resulted in policy changes, institutional reform, and increased youth leadership. Participants report increased confidence, critical consciousness, and motivation for ongoing activism.

## Connections

Emerges from participatory action research traditions and critical pedagogy movements. Relates to youth organizing, popular education, and social justice frameworks. Connects to broader movements positioning young people as change agents rather than passive recipients of services.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 8-16 weeks typically; can be ongoing program
- *People*: 10-30 youth; 2-4 adult facilitators
- *Space*: Community organization, school, or trusted community space
- *Materials*: Research tools, data collection instruments, analysis guides, presentation materials
- *Skills*: Research design, data collection, critical analysis, facilitation, advocacy communication
- *Energy Level*: Medium-to-high; requires sustained engagement and opportunity for authentic voice
