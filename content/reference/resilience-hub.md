## What It Is

A community facility that serves neighbors year-round as a gathering space and resource center — and shifts into a coordination point and emergency support center during disasters. Rather than building separate emergency infrastructure, Resilience Hubs enhance existing trusted institutions with services, supplies, and backup power.

## How It Works

Identify an existing community-trusted facility — church, rec center, library, nonprofit. Assess what exists and what gaps residents face. Strengthen the hub: establish reliable communication systems (backup power for WiFi, phone charging), stockpile supplies (water, first aid, food), expand partnerships (nonprofits, health providers, food banks), create community leadership structure, and conduct outreach. During normal times, host programming — classes, meetings, celebrations. Before disaster, coordinate preparedness education. During emergencies, become a neighborhood command center: distributing supplies, providing information, offering shelter, coordinating mutual aid. After disaster, support recovery. Power comes from being community-led and operating continuously.

## Who 'S Done It

In Seattle, Lake City Collective opened the qwiʔqwuʔ center (meaning 'little water' in Lushootseed) in 2020 — hosting cultural celebrations, community gardens, tutoring, and serving as emergency hub. In LA, the Boyle Heights Arts Conservatory operates as resilience hub seven days a week. As their manager noted, they 'were a resilience hub before we were designated one because we're grassroots, community engagement.' The strongest hubs grow organically from authentic community work rather than top-down planning.

## Connections

Emerges from disaster justice, environmental justice, and community development. Connects to ABCD, participatory budgeting, and the principle that affected communities should lead their own solutions. Works alongside babysitting co-ops and neighborhood networks by strengthening the infrastructure those connections depend on. The USDN toolkit provides comprehensive guidance.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 6-12 months to establish, ongoing coordination
- *People*: 5-10 community leaders, volunteers, facility partner
- *Space*: Existing community facility (1,500+ sq ft) with accessibility
- *Materials*: Backup power, emergency supplies, communication equipment
- *Skills*: Community organizing, project management, emergency response knowledge
- *Energy Level*: High — requires sustained organizing, shared among many
