# Community Bail Funds

## What It Is

Mutual aid organized around posting bail for low-income people arrested and detained, operating on the principle that no one should be trapped in jail because of poverty. Community Bail Funds are part of broader efforts to challenge the cash bail system and reduce pretrial detention while building community power and support.

## How It Works

Community members pool money into a bail fund with the explicit purpose of paying bail for low-income people. When someone is arrested and cannot afford bail, volunteers from the fund pay their bail with no expectation of repayment, which is the key difference from traditional bail funds. Bail is refunded by the court at the end of the case; the community bail fund receives the refund and the money goes back into the pool for the next person. Community Bail Funds also often provide support services (navigation of legal system, voluntary supportive services during pretrial period) to help people return to court and access necessary services. The work is rooted in mutual aid—communities taking collective responsibility for each other—and is explicitly political, working to expose the injustice of money bail and incarceration of poor people. This builds community solidarity and collective power while meeting immediate material needs.

## Who 'S Done It

The Bail Project (operates nationally, millions of dollars in bail paid); National Bail Fund Network (90+ community bail funds across the country); local bail funds in major cities and smaller communities; organizations like Big Door Brigade and others working in this space. Over 20 cities have safely minimized use of cash bail through this work.

## Connections

Part of longer history of African Americans pooling resources post-emancipation, Black Panther mutual aid, and COVID-era neighborhood mutual aid. Connected to criminal justice reform, prison abolition, and racial justice movements. Related to broader mutual aid practices and community-based alternatives to state systems.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing; initial planning: 1-3 months; fundraising: ongoing; bail release: can be hours
- *People*: Community organizers, fundraisers, legal liaisons, community volunteers, people with lived experience of incarceration
- *Space*: Online platform for donations and coordination; office/meeting space optional; courthouses and bail hearings
- *Materials*: Fundraising platform; legal database; bail procedures documentation; optional: support service resources, community agreements
- *Skills*: Fundraising, community organizing, legal/court navigation, care coordination, relationship-building, financial management
- *Energy Level*: Medium to high—requires fundraising and rapid response
