# Axioms for Organizers (Fred Ross Sr.)

> The house-meeting master's aphorisms, distilled from forty years of organizing: 'Short-cuts usually end in detours, which lead to dead ends.'

Fred Ross Sr. — the organizer who built the Community Service Organization's California chapters living room by living room, and who trained Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta — compressed four decades of craft into 'Axioms for Organizers' (1989), a few pages of aphorisms that circulate freely through the organizing world.

The axioms are the anti-shortcut tradition in miniature: organizing is one person at a time, follow-up is everything, and the organizer's job is to develop other people's capacity rather than display their own. They pair naturally with this shelf's practices — read them after the 1:1 recipe and the hard ask, and they sound like a veteran leaning over your shoulder.

The definitive account of Ross's life and method is Gabriel Thompson's biography 'America's Social Arsonist: Fred Ross and Grassroots Organizing in the Twentieth Century' (UC Press, 2016).
