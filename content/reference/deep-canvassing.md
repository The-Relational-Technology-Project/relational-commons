## What It Is

A structured conversation practice designed to shift people's beliefs through long, empathic one-on-one conversations. Developed by LGBTQ+ organizers to win ballot campaigns, Deep Canvassing uses storytelling, active listening, and compassionate curiosity to help people reconsider their positions on social issues.

## How It Works

A canvasser initiates a conversation by introducing themselves and asking the person being canvassed to rate their support for an issue on a scale of 1-10. Rather than delivering a pitch, the canvasser practices open-ended questioning and active listening, inviting the other person to share their perspective, experiences, and concerns. The canvasser shares their own relevant personal story and listens deeply to understand the other person's values and motivations. The conversation is unhurried and genuine, focusing on building human connection rather than winning an argument. The canvasser leaves space for the person to reach their own conclusions, following up later to reinforce any shifts in thinking. Deep Canvassing emphasizes assertiveness balanced with empathy and is specifically designed to move people across ideological divides.

## Who 'S Done It

Developed by LGBTQ+ rights organizers (LGSM—Lesbians and Gays Support the Miners); scaled and researched by People's Action; used by progressive campaigns on immigration, animal rights, racial justice, and housing. David Broockman and Josh Kalla have conducted rigorous research documenting its effectiveness.

## Connections

Emerged from grassroots LGBTQ+ and labor movements; connected to relational organizing, listening practices, and narrative work. Related to street-level persuasion, one-on-one organizing, and public narrative frameworks. Part of broader tradition of movement-based voter and community engagement.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 8-15 minutes per conversation; 20-40 conversations per canvasser per week; training: 2-4 hours
- *People*: Trained canvassers (ideally from or close to affected communities); target audience (voters, community members, or decision-makers)
- *Space*: Doorstep conversations, phone calls, video calls, or public spaces; no dedicated space needed
- *Materials*: Conversation script (flexible framework, not rigid); personal stories/narratives; tracking sheet for noting support shifts; optional: follow-up contact information
- *Skills*: Active listening, storytelling, emotional intelligence, comfort with discomfort, assertive communication, patience
- *Energy Level*: High—emotionally and intellectually demanding
