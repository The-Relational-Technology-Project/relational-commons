## What It Is

A global movement of communities building resilience and sustainability by reducing dependence on fossil fuels and strengthening local economies. Transition Towns began in 2006 in Totnes, UK, and now spans 67 countries, with local groups taking ownership of sustainability in their communities.

## How It Works

Communities establish Transition groups through grassroots organizing by neighbors committed to local resilience. Groups identify priorities reflecting their specific context—some focus on local food systems, others on renewable energy, transport, or economic localization. They organize around 43 'ingredients' (a more flexible framework than original 12-step model) addressing themes: outreach and awareness, community resilience, economics and enterprise, food, energy, health and wellbeing, and more. Regular meetings build commitment; working groups tackle specific areas. Transition groups partner with local government, nonprofits, and businesses to implement projects: community gardens, renewable energy cooperatives, tool libraries, skill-shares, local currencies, repair cafes. Regional and national hubs provide training, resources, and connection with other groups. Implementation is iterative and locally determined—there's no single formula, allowing communities to respond to their unique conditions.

## Connections

Transition Towns emerged from peak oil literature, permaculture design, and concerns about climate change. Founders Rob Hopkins and others developed the model drawing from resilience thinking, community organizing, and local economics. It connects to alternative economics, environmental justice, and community localization movements. Transition groups often work alongside municipalties, nonprofits, and other community initiatives.
