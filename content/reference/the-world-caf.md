## What It Is

A structured dialogue method that enables large groups (25-2,000 people) to have genuine conversation about important questions. Small groups at separate tables rotate members while keeping one 'table host' in place, creating cross-pollination of ideas as people move between conversations.

## How It Works

Set up like a real café: multiple small round tables seating 4-5 each, covered with tablecloths, butcher paper for doodling and notes, colored markers, flowers, water. Create ambient feeling: light music, warm lighting, refreshments. Present the core question — open-ended, not one with a right answer. Examples: 'What does our neighborhood need?' or 'How can we solve this together?' Seat people. After 20-30 minutes of conversation, everyone moves to a different table. One person (the 'table host') stays behind, briefly welcomes newcomers, and shares themes from the first round. The new group builds on what came before. Do 2-3 rounds. As people rotate, they carry insights and cross-pollinate ideas. Finally, bring everyone together to 'harvest' insights. What themes emerged? What actions do people want to take?

## Who 'S Done It

In a divided neighborhood where police-community relations were strained, organizers invited 80 residents to a World Café exploring 'How do we want to live together?' Small tables mixed police, organizers, longtime residents, and young people. In the first round, people mostly restated positions. But in round two, a police officer heard a mother describe fear for her son. By round three, conversations were more textured and human. In the harvest, themes of accountability, understanding, and shared investment in kids' safety emerged. Participants said they wouldn't have heard each other without the small-group structure.

## Connections

Developed in the 1990s by Juanita Brown and David Isaacs, drawing on conversation theory, organizational learning, and contemplative practice. It connects to Open Space Technology, Appreciative Inquiry, and circle processes. It's a bridge method — useful when you need large numbers but still want conversational depth. The free 'Café to Go' quick guide makes it easy to start.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-4 hours for a full session including setup and harvest
- *People*: 25-2,000+ (smaller groups get depth, larger get diversity)
- *Space*: Large room or outdoor area with multiple tables and free movement
- *Materials*: Small round tables, tablecloths, butcher paper, markers, flowers, refreshments, microphone for harvest
- *Skills*: Facilitation, writing a good opening question, comfort with ambiguity
- *Energy Level*: Moderate — logistics are the main work; facilitation is light once people understand the format
