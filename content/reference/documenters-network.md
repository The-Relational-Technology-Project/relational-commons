# Documenters: Paying Neighbors to Witness

> City Bureau's model trains and pays residents to attend and document public meetings — 'putting every public meeting on the record' — now a national network, with open-source meeting scrapers underneath.

City Bureau, founded on Chicago's South Side in 2015, built the Documenters program around a simple exchange: train residents in note-taking and public-meeting procedure, then **pay them** to attend and document the school board, the zoning committee, the park district — the meetings where neighborhood decisions actually happen and no reporter sits anymore. The notes are published; the meetings enter the public record in practice, not just in law.

The model spread into a national Documenters Network across dozens of cities, and its technical substrate is open source: the City Scrapers project standardizes public-meeting schedules from dozens of fragmented government sites into one usable feed.

For this shelf it's the strongest working answer to a question every neighborhood has: who is watching the decisions that shape our block? The answer that works is not an app — it's paid, trained neighbors with a shared standard, plus scrapers so finding the meeting isn't the hard part. (It also models the 'pay local stewards' survivor pattern from the graveyard framework.)

**Neighborhood remix:** one block's documenter — a rotating, honoraria-paid neighbor who attends the community board meeting and posts plain-language notes where the block actually reads.
