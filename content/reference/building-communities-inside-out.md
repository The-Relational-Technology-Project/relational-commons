# Building Communities from the Inside Out (the Green Book)

> Kretzmann & McKnight's 1993 classic — the founding text of asset-based community development, with the needs-map/assets-map argument and the original Capacity Inventory.

The book behind this shelf's third question. 'Building Communities from the Inside Out: A Path Toward Finding and Mobilizing a Community's Assets' (Kretzmann & McKnight, 1993) — the 'Green Book,' over 100,000 copies sold — makes the case that neighborhoods described by their deficits learn dependence, while neighborhoods that inventory their gifts, associations, and institutions learn power. It includes the original **Capacity Inventory**: the four-part interview (skills, community work, enterprising interests, minimal personal info) that ABCD practitioners have adapted for thirty years.

The 11-page introduction — which carries the whole core argument — is freely reprintable with attribution and widely mirrored; the full book is sold through ACTA Publications. The 1990 precursor paper, 'Mapping Community Capacity' (McKnight & Kretzmann), is free online and contains the first asset maps and the association checklist.

Companions worth knowing: 'Discovering Community Power' (2005 workbook, free to download); McKnight & Russell's 'The Four Essential Elements of an ABCD Process' (2018, CC BY-NC 4.0 — the most openly licensed statement of the practice); Block & McKnight's 'The Abundant Community' (2010); and Russell & McKnight's 'The Connected Community' (2022). The ABCD Institute, founded by the authors at Northwestern in 1995, now lives at DePaul University's Steans Center.
