# Repair Café

> A free community event where people gather to fix broken items together with volunteer repair experts. Participants bring broken clothes, furniture, electronics, bicycles, and ceramics, and work alongside skilled volunteers to repair them rather than throwing them away.

## What It Is

A free community event where people gather to fix broken items together with volunteer repair experts. Participants bring broken clothes, furniture, electronics, bicycles, and ceramics, and work alongside skilled volunteers to repair them rather than throwing them away.

## How It Works

Request a starter kit from repaircafe.org which includes templates, registration forms, and guidance. Recruit volunteer repair experts — people passionate about fixing electronics, textiles, bicycles, furniture. Find an accessible venue (community center, library, church, garage). Stock basic repair supplies and tools, asking fixers to bring specialized equipment. Publicize through flyers, community boards, and online networks, highlighting specific items you can repair. Hold a potluck planning meeting to build camaraderie. Plan your event for 3-4 hours, charge nothing to participants. Budget about 4 hours weekly to organize, a full day for the event, and 2 hours weekly to maintain if ongoing. The atmosphere should be casual, conversational, and celebratory — as much about building community as fixing things.

## Who 'S Done It

Martine Postma, a Dutch journalist, launched the first Repair Café in Amsterdam on October 18, 2009, at Fijnhout Theater after noticing how many items neighbors threw away instead of fixing. With her observation during daily life, she sparked a movement. The Repair Café Foundation was established in March 2010. Today, over 2,000 Repair Cafés operate in 37 countries worldwide, proving that fixing things together is a universally compelling way to build community and combat throwaway culture.

## Connections

Repair Café emerged from sustainability and maker movements, positioned as a response to planned obsolescence and consumer culture. It draws on craft traditions of repair knowledge passed through generations. It connects to the Right to Repair movement, zero-waste communities, skill-sharing circles, tool libraries, and maker spaces. It aligns with circular economy, intergenerational knowledge transfer, and local economic resilience.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 4 hours/week to organize, full day for events
- *People*: 5-10 volunteer repair experts, community organizers, 20-50 participants
- *Space*: Community venue with tables, chairs, good lighting
- *Materials*: Repair supplies (solvents, thread, solder), spare parts, tools
- *Skills*: Repair knowledge (electrical, mechanical, fabric), facilitation, outreach
- *Energy Level*: Medium — hands-on problem-solving
