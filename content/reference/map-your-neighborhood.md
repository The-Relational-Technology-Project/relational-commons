## What It Is

A disaster preparedness program that strengthens community resilience by having neighbors systematically get to know each other and plan together for emergencies. Mapping reveals who has critical skills (medical, first aid), resources (tools, generator), and vulnerabilities (elderly living alone, disabled neighbor needing assistance).

## How It Works

Contact your local fire department or emergency management agency about their neighborhood mapping program. Organize a 2.5-hour community meeting with 15-20 neighbors on your block. Walk through: what emergencies could affect your area, what each household can provide (food, water, medical skills, tools), and what each household needs. Create a simple map. Identify where your water main is. Learn basic first aid together. Designate meeting places if phones don't work. Coordinate who has emergency supplies. Create a simple contact list and share it. The philosophy: learning to quickly and safely take actions that minimize damage means knowing your neighbors now, before crisis hits.

## Who 'S Done It

San Francisco's NERT program has trained thousands of neighbors since the 1990s. A Marina District neighborhood did the mapping work in summer 2018. When an earthquake hit in 2022, neighbors responded immediately: they knew Mr. Chen used a walker and needed assistance, the Garcia family had medical training, and two homes had generators and water. The group quickly coordinated to check on vulnerable neighbors, treat injuries, and share supplies while waiting for responders. Afterward, they said the disaster-preparedness work had also created lasting friendship and cohesion separate from the emergency.

## Connections

Neighborhood mapping connects to broader disaster preparedness and community resilience work. It's rooted in community organizing traditions that recognize neighbors as a resource. It complements mutual aid networks, block parties, and good neighbor agreements — all practices that make neighborhood-level collective action normal. In California, the Neighbor-to-Neighbor program invested $10M statewide in this approach, finding that knowing six neighbors dramatically increases disaster readiness.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 hours per session, plus informal mapping over months
- *People*: 15-20 households per project, 1-3 core organizers
- *Space*: Community center, library, church, park
- *Materials*: Simple forms or maps, contact list template, flip charts
- *Skills*: Basic facilitation, comfort talking about vulnerability
- *Energy Level*: Moderate — mostly listening and organizing information
