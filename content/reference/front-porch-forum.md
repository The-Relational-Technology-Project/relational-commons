## What It Is

A civility-focused digital neighborhood forum where neighbors post about local news, recommendations, concerns, and connections. Unlike algorithmically-driven platforms, Front Porch Forum uses real address verification, real names, human moderation, and daily email digests instead of infinite scrolls. It's designed to get people offline and into real relationships.

## How It Works

Enter your actual address — the platform verifies you live there. Create a real-name account. Post about anything neighbors discuss: lost pets, recommendations, street concerns, block parties, safety issues, buy/sell/give items. Posts arrive in daily or weekly digests rather than a notification stream. This is a radical design choice: you read once a day in a purposeful way. Moderators ensure posts stay respectful and on-topic. Conversations that start online often move offline: neighbors meet for coffee, help each other, organize collectively. The platform exists to strengthen actual community, not substitute for it.

## Who 'S Done It

Front Porch Forum began in Vermont and now serves 235,000 active members among Vermont's 270,000 households. Members post about everything from mechanic recommendations to wildlife sightings to organizing around local issues. The platform's success shows what's possible when digital tools are designed for place and relationship rather than engagement metrics. It creates something increasingly rare: a trusted, civil, genuinely local online space.

## Connections

Grows from the insight that the internet went wrong when it became global and algorithmic instead of local and human-moderated. Part of a growing 'local first' internet movement. Connects to resilience hubs and microgrant gatherings by providing digital infrastructure for local organizing. Pairs well with Public Narrative and block steward work.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 5-10 minutes daily to check posts
- *People*: Neighborhood with 100+ households, volunteer moderators
- *Space*: Digital
- *Materials*: Internet access, email account
- *Skills*: Basic digital literacy, moderation skills
- *Energy Level*: Low for users; moderate for moderators
