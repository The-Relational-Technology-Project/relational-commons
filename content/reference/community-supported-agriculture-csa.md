# Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)

## What It Is

A direct relationship between farmers and consumers where community members buy shares of a farm's harvest in advance, sharing both risks and benefits of food production. Rather than purchasing food from distant industrial supply chains, CSA members make a season-long commitment to support local farmers, receiving regular boxes of freshly harvested food.

## How It Works

Community members purchase shares or subscriptions at the beginning of the growing season (typically $400-700 for a season), paid upfront or in installments. In exchange, they receive weekly or bi-weekly boxes of seasonal produce throughout the harvest (roughly May-November in northern climates). Some CSAs offer additional items like eggs, dairy, meat, dried goods, or flowers. Members sometimes participate in farm work, making decisions about what's planted, or volunteering at harvest. The model eliminates shipping and processing costs, reducing environmental impact and price.

## Who 'S Done It

As of 2020, 7,244 farms in the US operated CSA programs. CSAs are widespread across North America and Europe with growing adoption globally. Urban and rural CSAs exist, including innovative models serving low-income communities with subsidized shares and delivering to multiple neighborhoods.

## Connections

Originated in Japan in the 1960s among women concerned with pesticides and food system sustainability; formalized in Europe in the 1970s. Connected to slow food movement, local food systems, buy nothing groups, and food sovereignty movements. Represents a shift from consumer passivity to producer-consumer interdependence and mutual aid economics.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: One growing season minimum commitment; typically May-November
- *People*: Farmers/growers, CSA coordinator, participating members, volunteer crew members
- *Space*: Farm land, distribution point (farm, community center, markets), storage/packing area
- *Materials*: Farm equipment, seedlings, packaging/boxes, communication about seasonal offerings
- *Skills*: Farming/growing, logistics coordination, communication, customer relationship management
- *Energy Level*: Moderate—receiving weekly deliveries, optional farm participation
