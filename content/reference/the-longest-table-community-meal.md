# The Longest Table Community Meal

> A free, open-to-the-public shared meal held outdoors at a long table in a public space where neighbors gather to eat potluck dishes, share stories, and strengthen neighborhood connection. Tables are provided by organizers; attendees bring food, friends, and themselves.

## What It Is

A free, open-to-the-public shared meal held outdoors at a long table in a public space where neighbors gather to eat potluck dishes, share stories, and strengthen neighborhood connection. Tables are provided by organizers; attendees bring food, friends, and themselves.

## How It Works

Secure a public space for a long table — streets, parks, civic centers. If using a street, arrange closure with city officials. Recruit co-organizers and volunteers. Publicize through Nextdoor, local networks, posters, emphasizing it's free and open to anyone, whether they bring food or not. On the day, arrange tables end-to-end with basic place settings. Attendees arrive with potluck dishes (or just themselves). Create an open, welcoming atmosphere. The event runs 2-3 hours. Organize cleanup with volunteers. Many organizers create a simple rhythm: opening remarks, eating and mingling, closing words before dispersal.

## Who 'S Done It

During the isolated early months of 2020, Maryam Banikarim envisioned a communal gathering inspired by shared meals in Egypt. She posted the concept on Nextdoor in Chelsea, NYC, receiving overwhelming response. Co-founder Andy Lerner joined. The first Longest Table on West 21st Street drew 500 neighbors to a 544-foot table made of 68 connected smaller tables. By recent events, over 1,000 neighbors gathered. 90% of attendees report feeling more positive about their neighborhood. The model has been replicated in Harlem, Kansas City, California, and Paris.

## Connections

Draws from ancient potlatch traditions, communal feasting, and the slow food movement's celebration of shared meals. Connects to tactical urbanism, placemaking, and the broader movement for public space reclamation. Pairs well with story suppers for more intimate versions, and block parties for more festive energy.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-4 weeks planning, 2-3 hours event time, 2-3 hours setup/cleanup
- *People*: 2-3 core organizers, volunteers, 50-1000+ participants
- *Space*: Public outdoor space (street, park, plaza)
- *Materials*: Long tables, chairs, plates/utensils, potluck dishes
- *Skills*: Community organizing, event coordination, logistics
- *Energy Level*: Medium
