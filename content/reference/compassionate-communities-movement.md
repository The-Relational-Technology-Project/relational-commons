## What It Is

A public health approach to palliative care empowering community members to support those experiencing serious illness, dying, and grief. Emerging from Dr. Allan Kellehear's 1999 concept of health-promoting palliative care, the movement normalizes these life experiences as part of everyone's journey while building community-wide capacity to care.

## How It Works

The Compassionate Communities model operates through several mechanisms: (1) Recognizing that serious illness, dying, and grief are normal life experiences affecting everyone, not individual crises. (2) Creating community support networks—including neighbors, faith groups, workplaces, schools—that provide practical and emotional support. (3) Distributing responsibility for grief care across communities rather than confining it to professional domains. (4) Emphasizing early detection and support during loss, not just crisis response. (5) Organizing projects and initiatives implementing these principles across specific regions. Ontario's compassionate communities strategy currently operates 17+ projects across 12 regions.

## Who 'S Done It

The Compassionate Communities model has been implemented across Canada and internationally. Community initiatives demonstrate increased support for grieving individuals, normalized conversations about death and dying, and distributed caring capacity. Participants report feeling less isolated in grief and experiencing meaningful community support.

## Connections

Emerges from palliative care and public health frameworks. Relates to broader movements around death positivity, grief justice, and community resilience. Connects to practices addressing end-of-life care, collective grief, and community accountability.
