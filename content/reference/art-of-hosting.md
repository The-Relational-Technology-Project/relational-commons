## What It Is

A facilitation methodology that blends multiple dialogue approaches—Circle, World Café, Open Space Technology, and Appreciative Inquiry—to enable groups to tap collective intelligence and respond to complex social challenges. It reframes facilitators as hosts who create space for emergence rather than experts with predetermined answers.

## How It Works

Art of Hosting relies on four core practices deployed in sequence or combination. Circle processes begin with check-ins where participants share their presence and concerns, creating accountability and trust. World Café involves small conversation groups (4-5 people) that rotate between tables, allowing ideas to cross-pollinate across the larger group—effective for groups of 10 to 1000. Open Space Technology harnesses diverse groups around a central compelling question, letting participants self-organize sessions based on genuine interests rather than pre-planned agendas. Appreciative Inquiry focuses conversations on moments of excellence and what's working, building from strengths. Facilitators prepare by establishing clear purpose, inviting the right mix of people, arranging physical space to enable conversation, and creating ground rules that encourage participation. Sessions typically unfold over half-day to multi-day timeframes.

## Who 'S Done It

Art of Hosting emerged in the late 1990s through an international community of practitioners including Chris Corrigan and other facilitation experts. It has been applied by nonprofits addressing social change, city planning initiatives, and organizations seeking to mobilize communities around complex issues. Toronto, Vancouver, and communities across North America have used these methods to address urban planning challenges, education reform, and civic engagement. Organizations working on climate adaptation, social justice, and community organizing have adopted Art of Hosting as their core facilitation framework.

## Connections

Art of Hosting synthesizes decades of facilitation innovation. It draws directly from Harrison Owen's Open Space Technology (1980s), David Bohm's dialogue practices, Circle traditions from Indigenous cultures and contemporary peacebuilding, and the World Café methodology. It connects to Appreciative Inquiry's strength-based approach and represents a broader movement toward facilitation as a hosting practice rather than expert-driven presentation.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-8 hours for single session; multi-day for intensive work
- *People*: 8-1000+ participants; diverse perspectives essential
- *Space*: Physical space with movable furniture, multiple breakout areas, welcoming atmosphere
- *Materials*: Flip charts, markers, name tags, optional recording equipment
- *Skills*: Facilitation training, comfort with emergence and uncertainty, ability to listen deeply, capacity to hold space
- *Energy Level*: Moderate to high; requires active listening and engagement
