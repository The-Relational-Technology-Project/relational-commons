# Community Plate Story Sharing Supper

> Community Plate facilitates free, public, intergenerational story-sharing potluck suppers designed to build connections, address loneliness, and preserve community histories through shared meals and storytelling. Events are non-partisan and focused on increasing social connection

## What It Is

Community Plate facilitates free, public, intergenerational story-sharing potluck suppers designed to build connections, address loneliness, and preserve community histories through shared meals and storytelling. Events are non-partisan and focused on increasing social connection and civil discourse.

## How It Works

To host a Story Sharing Supper, organizers download the Community Partner Guide from Community Plate's website and contact them at community@communityplate.me for support. Before the event, community members are invited to bring a dish to share along with a story related to that dish. Organizers provide story prompts that are personal, nonpartisan, and food-centered to guide conversation. During the meal, participants share stories across tables based on these prompts, creating informal cross-generational and cross-cultural dialogue. As dessert is served, prepared storytellers perform or read stories they've crafted on the evening's theme. After the supper, Community Plate compiles all recipes, stories, and photos into a community cookbook distributed to attendees, creating a lasting artifact of the event and the connections made.

## Who 'S Done It

Community Plate, based in Maine, has hosted Story Sharing Suppers in communities across Maine and beyond. Events have taken place in Westbrook, Norway, Orono, and multiple other locations. The model has attracted diverse participants including families, seniors, and cross-cultural groups. Press coverage documents how suppers have strengthened community ties, particularly in smaller towns and communities experiencing social fragmentation.

## Connections

The Story Sharing Supper builds on potluck traditions while adding intentional storytelling and documentation. It reflects contemporary interest in narrative justice, food sovereignty, and community-based placemaking. The model connects to broader food-based community building and oral history preservation practices.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 hours for the supper event; advance planning of 4-6 weeks
- *People*: Organizers/hosts; 20-50+ community members; prepared storytellers; Community Plate support
- *Space*: Community gathering space (library, church, community center, school)
- *Materials*: Dishes/food; story prompts; cookbook creation materials (photos, text compilation)
- *Skills*: Community outreach, facilitation, basic storytelling/writing for documenting
- *Energy Level*: High during event; moderate for planning and follow-up documentation
