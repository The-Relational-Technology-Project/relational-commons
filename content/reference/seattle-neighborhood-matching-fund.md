## What It Is

A municipal funding program that provides matching grants for neighborhood-led community improvement projects. Since 1988, the program has empowered residents to lead projects addressing their own community priorities, with the city matching community contributions of volunteer time, materials, or cash.

## How It Works

Community groups (informal groups, nonprofits, neighborhood organizations, business groups) identify a project addressing neighborhood priorities: infrastructure improvement, cultural activation, organizing, environmental restoration, or other community-determined needs. Apply during annual funding cycles with project description, budget, and planned community match. Develop a community matching plan documenting how neighbors will contribute (labor, materials, donations). The city matches community contributions dollar-for-dollar up to funding limits ($50,000 for Community Partnership Fund; $5,000 for Small Sparks). Once approved, organize implementation with community volunteers. The city provides technical assistance and fiscal sponsorship if needed. Projects must be open and inclusive, actively engaging diverse neighbors, and significantly composed of Seattle residents. Annual grants distribute $1+ million supporting 25+ projects.

## Who 'S Done It

Seattle's Department of Neighborhoods created the Matching Fund in 1988, making it one of the longest-running municipal programs of its kind. Over 38 years, more than 5,000 projects have received $64+ million in city matching funds while generating $72+ million in community match. Projects range from neighborhood murals and street improvements to community gardens, youth programs, and organizing initiatives. The program demonstrates municipal government enabling rather than directing community priorities.

## Connections

Seattle's program draws from participatory budgeting traditions and represents a model of subsidiarity—decisions made at the neighborhood level closest to affected people. It connects to placemaking, community organizing, and civic infrastructure movements that strengthen local democratic participation.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-6 months from application to completion
- *People*: 5+ core organizers; 20+ community members involved in matching contribution
- *Space*: The neighborhood and specific project site
- *Materials*: Application materials, project budgeting, community documentation of match
- *Skills*: Community organizing, project management, grant writing, budgeting, community engagement
- *Energy Level*: Moderate to high; depends on project scope
