## What It Is

A consumer-driven membership model where older adults aging in place create formal or virtual villages—geographically-based communities where neighbors commit to mutual support for aging. Rather than entering senior housing, elders remain in their own homes while accessing a coordinated network of volunteers, services, and social programming.

## How It Works

Members pay annual membership dues ($300-800+) and in exchange receive access to services including volunteer support for light home maintenance and transportation, coordination of social and educational programs, wellness activities, technology coaching, and referrals to professional services. Villages are member-governed with a coordinator managing operations. Members volunteer to support each other, creating reciprocal relationships and mutual aid. Regular social events build community.

## Who 'S Done It

The movement began in Boston's Beacon Hill in 2001 and has grown to over 300 active Villages with many in development across the US. The Village to Village Network founded in 2010 supports village development and shares best practices. Examples include villages in urban neighborhoods, suburbs, and increasingly in rural areas.

## Connections

Emerged as response to aging population and desire to age in community rather than in institutions. Connected to successful aging frameworks, aging in place movements, intergenerational communities, and mutual support models. Related to Circles of Support and broader community care approaches.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing annual membership; 1-2 hours per week typical engagement
- *People*: Member elders (typically 55+), volunteer supporters, coordinator/staff, board members
- *Space*: Members' homes primarily; gathering spaces for events and programs
- *Materials*: Volunteer coordination system, program materials, transportation coordination tools
- *Skills*: Community organizing, program coordination, volunteer management, relationship facilitation
- *Energy Level*: Low to moderate—flexible based on individual participation level
