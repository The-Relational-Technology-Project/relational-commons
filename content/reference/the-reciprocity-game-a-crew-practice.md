# The Reciprocity Game: A Crew Practice

> A specific practice tool for microsolidarity crews designed to deepen relationships, reveal values, and practice reciprocity. The Reciprocity Game is a structured conversation game that helps crew members understand each other's needs, gifts, and capacity for mutual support.

## What It Is

A specific practice tool for microsolidarity crews designed to deepen relationships, reveal values, and practice reciprocity. The Reciprocity Game is a structured conversation game that helps crew members understand each other's needs, gifts, and capacity for mutual support.

## How It Works

Crews gather for 2-3 hour sessions where: (1) each person identifies a current need (material, emotional, skill-based, social); (2) each person offers a gift or capacity they have available; (3) the group plays rounds where people make and accept offers of help based on the identified needs and gifts; (4) people commit to follow-through actions; (5) the group reflects on what they learned about each other, patterns of reciprocity, and blocks to asking/giving. The game is designed to break patterns of either over-giving (burnout) or isolation, creating muscle memory around balanced exchange.

## Who 'S Done It

Crews across Europe and North America have adapted the Reciprocity Game into their regular practice cycles. One example: a Toronto-based crew of social workers used the game to address unspoken resentment about labor imbalance—playing it revealed that one member felt unable to receive help and another felt obligated to give constantly. After the game, they renegotiated their agreements and established explicit time-banking.

## Connections

Part of the microsolidarity practice suite; draws from non-violent communication, alternative economics games, and therapeutic group practices.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 hours per session, can be done monthly or quarterly
- *People*: 3-8 people in an established crew
- *Space*: Comfortable gathering space with privacy
- *Materials*: Written cards for offers/needs, facilitator guide, reflection prompts
- *Skills*: Active listening, commitment, vulnerability, creative problem-solving
- *Energy Level*: Moderate to high—emotionally engaging but also generative
