## What It Is

Public facilities that provide refuge during extreme weather (warming centers in winter, cooling centers in summer) that are increasingly being reimagined as 'Climate Resilience Hubs'—multifunctional community gathering spaces offering emergency services, social services, education, and community connection alongside climate protection.

## How It Works

Traditional cooling/warming centers provide basic emergency relief—air conditioning or heating for people without adequate climate control. Modern Climate Resilience Hubs expand this model significantly. They operate out of trusted community institutions like libraries, churches, schools, community centers, or nonprofits that are already well-used in the neighborhood. Beyond climate control, hubs offer: emergency supplies and resources; phone charging; welfare checks on vulnerable neighbors; emergency preparedness education; social services (housing, food assistance, mental health support); community connection and programming; and they're retrofitted with solar panels and battery backup so they can operate independently during power outages. Hubs function year-round, with expanded services during extreme weather. They are rooted in community relationships and trust, led by community members, and address the interconnected challenges of climate change, economic vulnerability, and social isolation.

## Who 'S Done It

CREW (Climate Resilience Education and Work) network; Community Resilience Projects in Los Angeles and other cities; cooling centers in Maricopa County, Arizona; numerous city and county heat action initiatives; Community Resilience projects across the U.S.; programs increasingly adopted as climate adaptation strategy by cities and counties.

## Connections

Related to emergency preparedness, public health, climate adaptation, and mutual aid. Connected to community gathering spaces and neighborhood resilience. Part of broader climate justice and environmental health movements. Related to emergency response networks and disaster-preparedness practices.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Planning: 3-6 months; year-round operation with seasonal intensity; staffing: 1-5 people
- *People*: Community organizers, hub coordinators, trained staff, volunteers, community members
- *Space*: Existing trusted community institution (library, church, school, nonprofits, community center) with HVAC flexibility and accessibility
- *Materials*: Climate control equipment (A/C, heating); solar panels and batteries (for resilience); emergency supplies; communication equipment; resource materials; comfortable seating
- *Skills*: Community coordination, emergency response, systems thinking, care-work, climate literacy
- *Energy Level*: Medium—ongoing operation with varying intensity by season
