# Big Door Brigade Mutual Aid Toolbox

## What It Is

A comprehensive, online resource library covering concrete models for starting and maintaining mutual aid projects. The toolbox provides templates, guides, and lessons learned across multiple domains including housing, food distribution, immigration support, prisoner solidarity, and sex worker mutual aid.

## How It Works

The toolbox organizes mutual aid work by domain, offering for each: (1) analysis of the problem and why mutual aid is needed; (2) real examples of existing projects; (3) concrete tools for organizing (membership structures, decision-making templates, communication protocols); (4) resources for handling common challenges like burnout, scope creep, and interpersonal conflict. Users can select their focus area, study how others have organized similar work, and adapt models to their context. The toolbox emphasizes that mutual aid projects are not charity but political work that directly challenges state neglect and control. Each section includes both conceptual frameworks and practical documents that groups can modify and implement.

## Who 'S Done It

Thousands of groups have used Big Door Brigade resources since its launch in 2017. Food Not Bombs networks have used the food distribution guides to coordinate over 35 years of community feeding; housing mutual aid networks have adapted the affinity group models for building trust-based housing networks; immigrant justice organizations like No More Deaths have applied the frameworks to cross-border solidarity work.

## Connections

Grows out of decades of anarchist mutual aid practice and Black radical traditions; informed by and works alongside networks like The Base, CNA (Community Not Cops), and other autonomous organizing spaces.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Varies by project: 3-5 hours weekly for core team, ongoing
- *People*: Core team of 3-8, connected to broader networks of 20-100+
- *Space*: Community-based; can be largely digital for coordination
- *Materials*: Decision-making templates, communication channels (Signal, email lists), shared documents
- *Skills*: Project management, facilitation, fiscal sponsorship knowledge, fundraising
- *Energy Level*: High—sustainable mutual aid requires consistent presence and emotional labor
