## What It Is

Community-based response programs that address non-violent, non-criminal incidents, behavioral health crises, homelessness, and quality-of-life issues without police involvement. These programs employ civilians—social workers, paramedics, mental health counselors, and community members—who respond to calls using de-escalation, care, and harm reduction.

## How It Works

Unlike police, alternative response programs send teams of trained civilians (typically a medic/paramedic and a crisis worker) to respond to 911 and non-emergency calls involving behavioral health crises, substance use, homelessness, welfare checks, and low-risk situations. Teams are trained in non-violent conflict resolution, mental health first aid, de-escalation techniques, and harm reduction. They do not carry weapons and rely on building trust and genuine care to resolve situations. Programs offer immediate support while connecting people to longer-term resources and services. This approach both improves outcomes for people in crisis and dramatically reduces police costs and arrest rates. Between 2020-2022, 62% of the 50 largest U.S. cities established alternative response programs. These programs save money while creating safer, more humane responses.

## Who 'S Done It

CAHOOTS (Crisis Assistance Helping Out on the Streets) in Eugene, Oregon (30+ years, 24,000 calls/year); STAR program in Denver; Secure Resilient Communities program in San Francisco; similar programs in Seattle, Los Angeles, Oakland, Austin, and 60+ other communities. Expanding rapidly across the country.

## Connections

Related to harm reduction, public health approaches, community organizing, and racial justice movements. Connected to disability justice and neurodiversity advocacy. Part of broader movement to defund police and reinvest in community services. Related to restorative and transformative justice practices.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Planning: 6-12 months; training: 4-8 weeks; ongoing operation: 24/7 or expanding hours
- *People*: Medics/paramedics, crisis counselors/social workers, dispatchers, program coordinators, community members from served areas in leadership
- *Space*: Mobile response (vans/teams); communication center for dispatch; office for coordination; facilities for trainings
- *Materials*: Mobile crisis response vehicles; de-escalation/first aid equipment; communication systems; training curricula; resource connection materials
- *Skills*: Mental health first aid, de-escalation, medical response, trauma-informed care, community knowledge, cultural competency
- *Energy Level*: High—requires trained teams operating on call, ongoing availability
