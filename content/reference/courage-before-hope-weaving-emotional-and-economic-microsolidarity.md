# Courage Before Hope: Weaving Emotional and Economic Microsolidarity

## What It Is

Richard Bartlett's proposal for building communities of 30-200 people organized into small crews of 3-8, where emotional intimacy and economic cooperation develop together. This essay articulates the theory and vision behind microsolidarity as a response to isolated individualism and unsustainable activism.

## How It Works

The model works in nested layers: (1) Crews form as the basic unit—small groups that create conditions for genuine belonging through practices of enthusiastic listening, praising, challenging, and acknowledging each other; (2) crews develop emotional intimacy first, which creates the foundation for economic cooperation (shared resources, time-banking, collective purchasing, income-sharing); (3) multiple crews coordinate into larger structures (congregations of 30-200) that allow for movement-building while maintaining relational depth; (4) courage is cultivated collectively—people encourage each other to persist toward meaningful work without guarantees of success. The process emphasizes that courage and meaning-making are social capacities, developed through regular practiced intimacy with people who share values.

## Who 'S Done It

Microsolidarity crews have been piloted in community building spaces, activist networks, and cooperative housing projects. In one documented case, a London-based crew organized around social change work met bi-weekly for emotional check-ins alongside quarterly economic discussions about shared resources. After 18 months, the group had collectively funded three members' sabbaticals, started a community garden, and maintained connection through two members' major life crises that might have caused group fracture.

## Connections

Builds on commoning traditions, cooperative economics, queer kinship structures, and Buddhist sangha models; related to intentional communities, co-housing movements, and solidarity economy frameworks.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2 hours bi-weekly for crew meetings, 4-8 hours monthly for coordination across crews
- *People*: 3-8 per crew, 30-200 in a congregation, with a core facilitation team
- *Space*: Intimate gathering spaces (homes, cafes), larger spaces for congregation events
- *Materials*: Facilitation guides, shared economic tracking (spreadsheets or software), communication platforms
- *Skills*: Emotional facilitation, economic literacy, systems thinking, conflict transformation
- *Energy Level*: High—requires sustained emotional presence and regular practice
