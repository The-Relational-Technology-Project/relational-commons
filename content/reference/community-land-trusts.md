# Community Land Trusts

## What It Is

A mechanism for creating and maintaining affordable homeownership by separating land ownership from building ownership. A nonprofit community land trust retains ownership of land while residents purchase only the buildings, making ownership accessible to low- and moderate-income families while protecting long-term affordability.

## How It Works

Community Land Trusts operate through a dual ownership model: (1) A nonprofit or quasi-governmental organization owns the land. (2) Individual families purchase homes built on that land at significantly reduced prices (since they're not purchasing land). (3) CLT-established formulas calculate resale prices, ensuring units remain affordable when sold. (4) When a CLT homeowner sells, the next buyer must also follow the same affordability formula. (5) CLT governance includes homeowners, community members, and stakeholders to ensure accountability. This structure removes properties from real estate speculation, stabilizes neighborhoods, and creates pathways to homeownership for families earning approximately one-third of local median income.

## Who 'S Done It

Community Land Trusts operate across the U.S. and internationally, serving thousands of families. Research shows CLT homeowners build equity through forced savings and mortgage payments, with many accumulating enough wealth to purchase on the open market if desired. CLT communities demonstrate higher homeownership stability and contribute to neighborhood resilience. Examples include Atlanta Land Trust and numerous urban CLTs addressing housing justice.

## Connections

Part of broader housing justice, land reform, and cooperative economic movements. Relates to communal land ownership practices and alternatives to speculative real estate. Connects to community wealth building and anti-displacement organizing.
