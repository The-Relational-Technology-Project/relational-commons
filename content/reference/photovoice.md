## What It Is

A participatory research and community engagement method where community members use photography to document their lived experiences, identify community strengths and concerns, and share perspectives that influence policy. Rather than outside experts studying communities, residents become researchers and storytellers, creating visual evidence of community realities.

## How It Works

Community members are provided cameras and trained in basic photography and ethical practices. They photograph aspects of their community aligned with research questions or community priorities. In facilitated group discussions using VOICE methodology (Voicing Our Individual and Collective Experience), participants present photos, discuss meanings, share stories, and collectively identify themes. Photos and narratives are compiled into exhibitions, reports, or presentations to policymakers and service providers. The visual and narrative evidence often proves more compelling than statistics alone.

## Who 'S Done It

PhotoVoice (the original organization) began in rural China documenting women's experiences. The method has been applied globally in public health, international development, education, disability rights, and environmental justice. Used by NGOs, universities, and community organizations to give voice to marginalized populations.

## Connections

Developed by Caroline Wang in Yunnan, China in the 1990s. Connected to participatory research, community-based research, public narrative, and creative activism. Related to beautiful trouble and creative interventions as methods of social change documentation and communication.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-6 months for a full photovoice project; 6-12 sessions typical
- *People*: Community participants (photographers), trained facilitators, potential policymakers/stakeholders
- *Space*: Community gathering spaces for discussion, community locations to photograph
- *Materials*: Cameras (digital or phone), photo printing, discussion prompts, exhibition materials
- *Skills*: Facilitation, visual analysis, ethical research practices, community communication
- *Energy Level*: Moderate—involves photography, group discussion, exhibition work
