## What It Is

A community organizing approach that prioritizes and leverages existing relationships and networks to build collective power and mobilize communities. Rather than cold outreach, Relational Organizing works through people's personal networks of friends, family, coworkers, and acquaintances to rally support for issues and actions.

## How It Works

Organizers identify key people in a community who have natural credibility and strong existing relationships. These connectors then reach out through their personal networks using one-on-one conversations to understand people's values, concerns, and interests. The organizer helps clarify shared concerns and invites people to collective action. One-on-ones—intentional conversations with people in one's network—are central to this approach. The goal is to understand each person's motivations and identify common ground for action. Because the invitation comes through a trusted relationship rather than a cold call or mass message, people are more likely to listen, be receptive, and take action. Relational organizing builds durable partnerships through mobilizing these existing relationships for rapid, authentic community engagement.

## Who 'S Done It

Used by community organizations, health systems, and grassroots campaigns across the U.S. Health system collaborations using relational organizing to strengthen community partnerships. Faith-based organizations and community centers use this approach. Featured prominently in work by Southerners on New Ground and other grassroots organizations.

## Connections

Part of long tradition of grassroots organizing and movement-building. Connected to deep canvassing, narrative work, and asset-based community development. Related to network mapping and social network analysis. Emphasizes the wisdom of Indigenous and traditional community organizing practices that have always prioritized relationships.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing; relationship-building requires months to years; one-on-ones: 30-60 minutes each
- *People*: Key connectors/relationship-rich people; community organizers or coordinators; wider network of community members
- *Space*: Informal spaces: coffee shops, homes, workplaces, parks, community centers
- *Materials*: Contact tracking system; optional: conversation guides, background materials on issues
- *Skills*: Active listening, relationship-building, one-on-one facilitation, community knowledge, comfort with informal settings
- *Energy Level*: Medium—depends on existing relationships and communication style
