## What It Is

A network of public refrigerators placed in neighborhoods, public spaces, and community institutions where people can freely share food with neighbors. Community Fridges operate on gift economy and mutual aid principles, providing accessible food to anyone who needs it while building community connection and reducing food waste.

## How It Works

Community Fridges are standard refrigerators installed outside of storefronts, community centers, churches, nonprofits, or homes with community permission. Anyone can leave food they want to share—home-cooked meals, fresh produce, prepared foods from businesses, or pantry items. Anyone can take what they need with no questions asked and no shame. Fridges are maintained by community volunteers who keep them clean, stocked, and functioning. The practice requires minimal infrastructure but maximum community trust and coordination. Fridges operate on the principle of mutual aid—people helping neighbors based on human dignity and reciprocity, not charity. Rather than top-down distribution by organizations, Community Fridges are horizontally organized, community-led spaces. The fridges have become informal community gathering spots and symbols of neighborhood solidarity, often surrounded by art, poetry, and messages of welcome.

## Who 'S Done It

In Our Hearts NYC (established first fridge movement, February 2020, 70+ fridges in NYC); Share Food Program (30+ fridges in Philadelphia); The Love Fridge Chicago (17 fridges across Chicago); Evanston Community Fridges; North Brooklyn Mutual Aid; expanding networks in dozens of cities across the U.S.

## Connections

Part of broader mutual aid movement that emerged during COVID-19. Connected to food not bombs, free pantries, and gift economy practices. Related to community fridges as separate singular practice. Part of horizontal organizing, anarchist praxis, and community solidarity movements. Connected to food justice and anti-poverty organizing.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Initial setup: 2-4 weeks (including community organizing); ongoing maintenance: 5-10 hours/week distributed among volunteers
- *People*: 5-20+ core volunteer coordinators; 20-100+ community members contributing/accessing food; community partners for location and power
- *Space*: Accessible outdoor or semi-outdoor location (storefront, community center exterior, institutional building, home) with reliable electricity and foot traffic
- *Materials*: Refrigerator (new or reclaimed); electrical outlet; signage; optional: seating, art materials, lighting
- *Skills*: Community coordination, maintenance, conflict resolution, relationship-building, commitment to mutual aid principles
- *Energy Level*: Medium—requires consistent volunteer coordination and regular maintenance
