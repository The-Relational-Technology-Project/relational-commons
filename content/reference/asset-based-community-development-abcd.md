# Asset-Based Community Development (ABCD)

## What It Is

A foundational framework developed by John McKnight and John Kretzmann that shifts community development from a deficit-focused (problems-based) approach to an asset-focused (strengths-based) approach. ABCD emphasizes identifying and leveraging existing community assets—individual skills, local institutions, physical infrastructure, and social capital—as the foundation for sustainable development rather than starting with problems to be fixed.

## How It Works

Communities using ABCD begin by mapping local assets including individual skills and capacities, community associations, local institutions (schools, libraries, faith organizations), physical infrastructure, and economic resources. Facilitators help residents and organizations identify what already exists and works well, building on these strengths rather than focusing on deficits. The process involves asset mapping workshops, relationship building, and intentional connection of assets to create community-driven solutions that emerge from community capacity rather than external interventions.

## Who 'S Done It

The approach has been implemented globally by the ABCD Institute at DePaul University in Chicago, with successful applications in low-income neighborhoods in Chicago, Toronto, and hundreds of communities worldwide. Faith communities, neighborhood organizations, and municipal governments in the US and internationally have adopted ABCD frameworks.

## Connections

Developed in the late 1980s through McKnight and Kretzmann's research across 300+ neighborhoods in 20 cities. Published in their 1993 book 'Building Communities from the Inside Out.' Connects to abundant community thinking, structural connection thinking, and high-agency community building approaches. Influenced by critiques of deficit-based social work and development models.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-6 months for initial mapping, ongoing for integration
- *People*: Community facilitator, residents willing to identify strengths, local institutions, asset mappers
- *Space*: Community centers, faith buildings, neighborhood gathering spaces for workshops
- *Materials*: Asset maps, relationship diagrams, documentation tools, workshop materials
- *Skills*: Asset mapping facilitation, relationship building, systems thinking, community organizing
- *Energy Level*: Medium to high—requires sustained engagement and reframing mindsets
