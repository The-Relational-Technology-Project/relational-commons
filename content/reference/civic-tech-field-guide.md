# The Civic Tech Field Guide

> The world's largest collection of tech-for-good projects — 12,000+ entries, 500+ categories, a famous graveyard, and an open API — explicitly licensed for remixing. This shelf's civic tech research starts here.

The Civic Tech Field Guide (civictech.guide) is the field's collective memory: 12,370+ curated entries across a 506-category taxonomy, from participatory budgeting platforms to mesh networks to the craft of organizing. Conceived in 2016 by Micah Sifry, Matt Stempeck, and Erin Simpson at Civic Hall, built out by Stempeck, and now a project of Superbloom, it tracks not just what exists but what died — the Graveyard is its most-cited feature — and who's hiring, funding, and gathering.

It is deliberately a commons: content is licensed **CC BY-NC-SA** (remixable for non-commercial use with attribution), the site code is open source, and a free read API (documented at civictech.guide/build) invites exactly the kind of reuse this shelf represents.

The categories most useful at neighborhood scale, from this shelf's research sweep: neighborhood-forums, mutual-aid, resource-matching-and-sharing, issue-reporting, participatory-budgeting, deliberation, civic-maps, community-wifi and mesh-networks, relational-organizing, and the-craft-of-organizing. Notable gaps worth knowing: no timebanking category, and thin tenant-tech coverage — both places where RTP can contribute entries *back*.

A pleasing loop: the Field Guide already lists the Relational Technology Project, RT Studio, and several RB remix cards. The commons reads us while we read it.
