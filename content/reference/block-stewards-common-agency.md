# Block Stewards (Common Agency)

> Intergenerational neighbors designated as Block Stewards who serve as connectors for their block, helping neighbors discover each other, collaborate on projects, and self-organize around shared needs. The foundation is human relationship-building, not programs.

## What It Is

Intergenerational neighbors designated as Block Stewards who serve as connectors for their block, helping neighbors discover each other, collaborate on projects, and self-organize around shared needs. The foundation is human relationship-building, not programs.

## How It Works

A Block Steward is a committed neighbor who wants to strengthen connection on their block. Their role: get to know neighbors, learn about interests and assets, identify shared needs. They use walking maps, surveys, or casual conversations to understand what's happening. They facilitate connections — introducing neighbors, suggesting collaborations, inviting people to events. Stewards might organize a plant exchange, host multilingual story time, coordinate a garden, or hold regular coffee gatherings. They use Common Agency's technology to amplify work, but the foundation is relationships. They provide personalized recommendations, suggesting events, groups, and neighbors to connect with.

## Who 'S Done It

Lori, a Network Steward in Eastcore, organized a plant exchange after discovering many neighbors had plants to share. Vika in Metuchen saw a gap in multilingual children's services and created an ingenious solution: she glued translations into children's books and organized multilingual story time where neighbors read in their own languages. Both show how stewards identify real needs and create opportunities for neighbors to realize they already have everything they need to solve problems together. Deborah Tien leads this work at Common Agency.

## Connections

Connects to community organizing, neighborhood associations, and mutual aid traditions. Builds on asset-based community development — recognizing every neighborhood already has resources that can be activated. Challenges the assumption that change requires outside experts. Pairs well with block parties, neighborhood mapping, and Front Porch Forum.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 5-10 hours weekly (flexible, self-determined)
- *People*: 1-2 Block Stewards per block, neighbors
- *Space*: Block/neighborhood, homes, community spaces
- *Materials*: Maps, contact lists, technology platform optional
- *Skills*: Relationship-building, listening, convening, local knowledge
- *Energy Level*: Medium
