## What It Is

A worldwide initiative emerging in the late 1960s and early 1970s that fundamentally reimagined the purpose of gaming. Rather than emphasizing competition and winning, the movement created inclusive games where joy, cooperation, and community connection took precedence. Games became vehicles for imagining and practicing alternative social models.

## Connections

Foundational to modern play-based community organizing. Directly influences Come Out and Play Festival, contemporary community games programs, and broader play justice movements. Relates to Bernard DeKoven's philosophy and impacts initiatives emphasizing play in conflict resolution, education, and civic engagement.
