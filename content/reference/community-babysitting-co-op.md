## What It Is

A group of neighbors who trade childcare hours using a points or token system. Each family earns credits by watching others' children and spends credits when they need care. It's economical, but more importantly builds relationships, creates mutual dependence, and teaches kids they're part of a caring community.

## How It Works

Gather 4-12 families interested in sharing childcare. Establish your point system: how many points each family starts with (many start with 20), how many points per hour, whether nighttime or multiple kids earn double. Set up tracking: shared spreadsheet, printed tokens, or 'babysitting bucks.' Create guidelines: scheduling, cancellation policy, what happens if someone falls behind. Hold monthly meetings to check in, address issues, and build relationships. When you need care, contact members you trust, arrange the exchange, transfer points. The simplicity is key — no background checks, no corporate platform, just neighbors trusting neighbors.

## Who 'S Done It

Families in student housing at Montana State University formed a co-op with nothing but poker chips and monthly meetings — chips represented points exchanged at each sitting. On the East Coast, co-ops with 20-30 families have run for decades with sophisticated systems. Some print colorful 'babysitting bucks.' The average parent in a co-op saves $500+/year while deepening ties with neighbors. Kids grow up knowing multiple trusted adults — a sense of being cared for by community, not just parents.

## Connections

Part of the broader gift economy and time-banking movement. Connects to cooperative economics, credit unions, and mutual aid traditions going back centuries. Pairs naturally with other neighborhood practices — co-op members often become the core of a neighborhood community, hosting skillshares and gatherings together. Builds what sociologists call 'social capital' — trust and reciprocal relationships.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 1-2 hours initial planning, 10-15 min per exchange, quarterly meetings
- *People*: 4-12 families with children (geographically close)
- *Space*: Members' own homes, occasional meeting space
- *Materials*: Tracking system (spreadsheet, tokens, or printed bucks)
- *Skills*: Basic facilitation, trust and communication, flexibility
- *Energy Level*: Low to moderate — distributed across many people
