## What It Is

A coordinated system where community members take turns delivering home-cooked or purchased meals to someone experiencing a major life event or crisis. Meal Trains reduce stress for people going through challenging periods (new baby, surgery, loss, illness) by ensuring they have nutritious food without having to cook or shop.

## How It Works

One person organizes the Meal Train by setting up a calendar or online platform (Meal Train, Give InKind, Take Them A Meal, or MealRally) with available time slots and any dietary preferences. Community members sign up for specific dates and meal times. Participants prepare a meal at home or purchase prepared food and deliver it to the recipient's home on their assigned date. The calendar prevents duplicate meals and ensures consistent coverage over the planned period (typically 2-8 weeks). Modern platforms allow people to coordinate not just meals but also childcare, pet care, errands, and financial support through gift cards. The practice operates on the principle of collective mutual aid—the community surrounds the person in need with practical care.

## Who 'S Done It

Widely used in churches, religious communities, neighborhoods, and workplaces across North America. Popularized by platforms like Meal Train, Give InKind, Lotsa Helping Hands, Take Them A Meal, and MealRally. Common after births, deaths, hospitalizations, and major illnesses.

## Connections

Rooted in religious and faith-based traditions of community care and congregational support. Connected to broader mutual aid practices, meal-sharing, and collective care systems. Related to community meals, potlucks, and care webs. Part of informal neighborhood interdependence.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 20-40 minutes per delivery; coordination: 1-2 hours initial setup; typically runs 2-8 weeks
- *People*: 1 organizer; 5-20+ meal contributors
- *Space*: Recipient's home; participant kitchens or restaurants
- *Materials*: Meal Train platform or shared calendar (digital or paper); recipes or menus (optional); containers for food (can be returned or reusable)
- *Skills*: Meal planning/cooking (optional if purchasing meals), coordination, communication
- *Energy Level*: Low to medium—most people contribute one meal delivery
