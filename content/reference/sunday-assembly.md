## What It Is

A secular, community-based organization that brings people together in a worldwide network of over 90 chapters to celebrate life, build meaningful connections, and engage in collective action. Members gather regularly for events featuring inspiration, music, reflection, and community service.

## How It Works

Sunday Assembly operates through local independent chapters, each organized by volunteers. Chapters host regular gatherings that typically include curated speakers, music, and opportunities for members to volunteer in their communities. The organization provides startup chapters with resources including venue guidance, recommended speakers, curated playlists, and volunteer coordination structures. Attendees participate in the three core values: 'Live Better' (personal development), 'Help Often' (community service), and 'Wonder More' (intellectual curiosity). Chapters progress through growth stages—from informal gatherings to securing public venues, building volunteer teams, launching interest-based subgroups, and eventually establishing formal nonprofit structures. All chapters remain radically inclusive, free to attend, and volunteer-run while maintaining the secular philosophical foundation.

## Who 'S Done It

Sunday Assembly has grown to 90+ chapters across multiple countries, including North America, Europe, and Australia. Chapters range from small neighborhood groups to large community organizations. The movement has attracted diverse participants seeking secular community outside religious frameworks. For example, Sunday Assembly London regularly hosts 200+ attendees at gatherings featuring local speakers and musicians. Alumni describe the experience as providing meaningful connection and purpose. The organization has successfully scaled from a founding concept to a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit with chapters in cities from New York to San Francisco, each maintaining local autonomy while upholding core values.

## Connections

Sunday Assembly emerged from the broader 'secular community' and 'ethical gathering' movements of the 2010s. It connects to related practices like Moral Mondays, secular ethical societies, and community fellowship organizations. The model influences and draws inspiration from traditional religious gathering structures while creating explicitly non-religious spaces. It relates to civic assembly movements emphasizing public participation and shared values.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Weekly to monthly gatherings depending on chapter; 1-2 hours per meeting
- *People*: 10-20 core organizers; 50-300+ attendees per chapter
- *Space*: Public venues—community centers, theaters, halls with seating capacity
- *Materials*: Curated speaker lists, music playlists, volunteer coordination tools, promotional materials
- *Skills*: Event facilitation, volunteer management, public speaking, community outreach, organizational leadership
- *Energy Level*: Medium-to-high; requires sustained volunteer commitment and community engagement
