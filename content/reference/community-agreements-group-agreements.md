## What It Is

A collaborative process where a group of people come together to create explicit, shared norms and expectations for how they will interact, work together, and handle conflict. Community Agreements are co-created principles that establish the values, behaviors, and boundaries for a community, organization, or group gathering.

## How It Works

A group gathers to develop Community Agreements through participatory processes. This might involve small-group brainstorming, individual written suggestions followed by group discussion and voting, or whole-group dialogue. The group identifies the values they want to embody (respect, honesty, accountability, joy, safety), then translates these into specific behavioral agreements ('We listen without interrupting,' 'We assume good intent and address harm when it occurs,' 'We center the voices of those most affected'). Unlike rules imposed from above, Community Agreements are collectively created and owned by everyone. They make explicit what is usually implicit, reduce assumptions, and distribute responsibility for maintaining the group's culture. Communities revisit and refine agreements as needed. When conflict arises, the group has a shared framework for addressing it. Community Agreements work best when they are truly co-created, regularly reviewed, and understood as living documents that evolve as the group changes.

## Who 'S Done It

Educational institutions (colleges, universities, schools), organizing groups and nonprofits, faith communities, artist collectives, online communities, workplace teams, community organizing groups, and workshops/conferences across North America.

## Connections

Related to restorative justice and transformative justice practices. Connected to consensus-based decision making and participatory democracy. Part of broader culture of accountability and horizontal organizing. Related to conflict resolution and community accountability processes.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Initial creation: 1-3 hours; ongoing review: 1 hour every 3-6 months
- *People*: All group members; ideally diverse representation including those with less power in the group
- *Space*: Safe, comfortable meeting space where all can participate equally
- *Materials*: Flip chart and markers, or digital document for collaborative writing; optional: template or examples of community agreements
- *Skills*: Facilitation, group process, listening, creative communication, systems thinking
- *Energy Level*: Medium—generative and collaborative but requires sustained focus
