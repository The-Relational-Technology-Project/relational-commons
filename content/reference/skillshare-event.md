## What It Is

A community gathering where neighbors teach each other practical skills — bike repair to salsa dancing to home canning to storytelling — in multiple simultaneous classes. No curriculum set by experts, no tuition, no certification. Just skilled neighbors sharing what they know with curious neighbors.

## How It Works

Start planning 5-6 months out. Lock down a venue with multiple rooms. Begin recruiting instructors 4 months before — reach out to local artists, crafters, hobbyists, small business owners. Make it easy to say yes: simple one-page form, flexible timing. Build partnerships with local organizations for promotion. Confirm instructors, assign rooms, create schedules. On event day: clear signage, volunteer greeters, built-in breaks, social atmosphere. Typical skillshares feature 30-50 classes happening simultaneously over 4-6 hours, attracting 300-1000 people. Classes fill to 15-40 people depending on space.

## Who 'S Done It

The first annual Somerville Skillshare (Somerville, MA, March 2014) attracted nearly 800 residents with 40 free classes — urban beekeeping, storytelling, jewelry making, community organizing, printmaking, yoga, tai chi. People learned what they don't teach in school, in the same room as neighbors, from instructors who lived around the corner. Similar events have launched in dozens of communities. The consistent finding: communities are hungry for this kind of learning and connection.

## Connections

Draws on peer teaching, apprenticeship, and knowledge-sharing traditions worldwide. Connects to maker movement, DIY culture, and popular education. Works well alongside other community practices — often emerges from Front Porch Forum organizing, deepens block party connections, teaches skills that support co-ops and mutual aid.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 150-200 hours planning over 5-6 months, 8-10 hours event day
- *People*: 3-5 organizing team, 30-50 instructors, 20-40 volunteers, 300-1000 attendees
- *Space*: Venue with 4-8 classrooms, accessible
- *Materials*: Signage, program materials, supplies per class, refreshments
- *Skills*: Event coordination, outreach, facilitation, volunteer management
- *Energy Level*: High for organizers; moderate for instructors; low for attendees
