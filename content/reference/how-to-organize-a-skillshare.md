# How to Organize a Skillshare

> Shareable's comprehensive guide to organizing successful skillshare events where community members teach and learn practical skills from each other. Skillshares are non-commercial, skill-centered gatherings that democratize knowledge and build community.

## What It Is

Shareable's comprehensive guide to organizing successful skillshare events where community members teach and learn practical skills from each other. Skillshares are non-commercial, skill-centered gatherings that democratize knowledge and build community.

## How It Works

Begin by creating a shared spreadsheet to coordinate logistics, budget, contacts, and publicity with other organizers. Select a venue with adequate classroom spaces, capacity, water access, and available furniture. Consider space constraints to determine how many classes you can offer. Build an online presence through a simple website, Facebook page, or social media using free tools like WordPress. Create an event identity through a logo and consistent branding. Develop fundraising strategies including benefit concerts, business donations, Kickstarter campaigns, or grant applications to cover overhead, food, and supplies. Promote widely through Eventbrite, local papers, blogs, listservs, Meetup.com, and all social media platforms. Recruit teachers and volunteers from your network and publicly advertise skill areas being sought.

## Who 'S Done It

Skillshares have been organized in communities worldwide as low-cost alternatives to formal education and skill-building. The model has been adapted for diverse communities, from urban neighborhoods to rural areas, demonstrating flexibility and accessibility.

## Connections

Skillshares build on traditions of collective learning, apprenticeship, and knowledge-sharing in communities. They align with the sharing economy, alternative education movements, and commons-based organizing.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 8-12 weeks planning; 1-2 days execution
- *People*: Core organizing team; community teachers/volunteers; participants
- *Space*: Community center, school, library, or flexible venue with multiple classroom areas
- *Materials*: Spreadsheets, promotional materials, refreshments/snacks, supplies for classes
- *Skills*: Project management, community outreach, basic event coordination
- *Energy Level*: Moderate planning, high energy for execution and community teaching
