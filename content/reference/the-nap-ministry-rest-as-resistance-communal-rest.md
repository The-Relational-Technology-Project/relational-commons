# The Nap Ministry: Rest as Resistance & Communal Rest

> A community practice and social movement founded by Tricia Hersey that frames rest as an act of radical resistance against white supremacy, capitalism, and overwork culture. The Nap Ministry provides communal rest experiences, spiritual practices, and educational resources center

## What It Is

A community practice and social movement founded by Tricia Hersey that frames rest as an act of radical resistance against white supremacy, capitalism, and overwork culture. The Nap Ministry provides communal rest experiences, spiritual practices, and educational resources centered on Black liberation, collective healing, and the liberatory power of naps and rest.

## How It Works

The Nap Ministry facilitates immersive nap sessions, performance art installations, and community gatherings where people rest together in sacred, protected spaces. These events might include guided breathing, meditation, music, sensory experiences, and the simple act of sleeping or resting alongside others. Sessions create what the Ministry calls 'portals' for imagination, invention, and healing—spaces where people can temporarily escape the demands of capitalism and white supremacy. The practice is rooted in Black liberation, womanism, somatics (body-based practices), and Afrofuturism. Beyond nap events, the Ministry offers educational resources (the REST DECK—50 practices to resist grind culture), trainings, and workshops that help people and organizations understand rest as foundational to personal and collective wellbeing and liberation. The work is explicitly political, viewing rest as a tool for dismantling systems of oppression.

## Who 'S Done It

The Nap Ministry (founded 2016, founded by Tricia Hersey and others); community organizations, universities, and organizations internationally adopting Nap Ministry frameworks and practices; artists and community groups creating nap and rest events.

## Connections

Rooted in Black radical tradition, disability justice, and somatic practices. Connected to healing justice, community care, and resistance practices. Related to rest activism, anti-capitalist movements, and feminist economics. Influenced by African diaspora practices and spiritual traditions.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Nap sessions: 30 min-2 hours; immersive events: 4-8 hours; training/workshops: 2-4 hours
- *People*: Facilitators trained in somatic and healing practices (ideally from Black communities); community participants; optional: musicians, artists, healers
- *Space*: Sacred, comfortable spaces: community centers, parks, galleries, churches; requires soft surfaces (rugs, mats, blankets), dim lighting, sound management
- *Materials*: Blankets, pillows, mats; optional: music/sound, visual art, sacred objects; REST DECK cards or other educational materials
- *Skills*: Facilitation, somatic awareness, spiritual grounding, cultural competency, creating psychological safety
- *Energy Level*: Low physically, high spiritually—restorative and nourishing
