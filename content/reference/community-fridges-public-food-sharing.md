## What It Is

Refrigerators placed in accessible public or semi-public locations where community members can freely share perishable food. Unlike little free pantries focused on shelf-stable goods, community fridges extend mutual aid food sharing to fresh produce, dairy, prepared meals, and other temperature-sensitive foods that require refrigeration.

## How It Works

Refrigerators (outdoor or sheltered) are installed in high-traffic community locations (parks, storefronts, community centers) with clear signage inviting donations and sharing. Anyone can leave food they don't need; anyone can take what they need—no questions asked, no income verification. Community members keep fridges clean and organized. Best practices include food safety guidelines, regular monitoring, and community maintenance. Operates on principles of mutual aid and dignity, removing barriers (hours, paperwork, stigma) that traditional food access has.

## Who 'S Done It

Community fridges are grassroots initiatives spreading rapidly across cities. Notable examples in Brooklyn, New York; Washington DC; Baltimore; and other cities. Community Fridge and similar organizations document and support the network. Growth accelerated during COVID-19 pandemic for food security.

## Connections

Extends little free pantry concept to refrigerated foods. Connected to mutual aid networks, buy nothing groups, and food security justice. Part of broader solidarity economics and care infrastructure movements that reduce reliance on formal charities.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing maintenance; stocking/taking as needed, rotating daily
- *People*: Community members donating and taking food, volunteer maintenance coordinators
- *Space*: Public or semi-public accessible location, protection from elements ideally
- *Materials*: Commercial or reclaimed refrigerators, shelving, signage, cleaning supplies
- *Skills*: Basic maintenance, food safety knowledge, community communication
- *Energy Level*: Low—simple donation/taking, occasional maintenance and organizing
