# Hosting a Civic Saturday

> A secular gathering modeled on faith-based services, designed to strengthen civic participation and community connection through shared ritual, meaningful texts, and collective reflection on what it means to be a citizen together.

## What It Is

A secular gathering modeled on faith-based services, designed to strengthen civic participation and community connection through shared ritual, meaningful texts, and collective reflection on what it means to be a citizen together.

## How It Works

Plan a gathering for 50-200 people in a welcoming venue. Create a flowing agenda: opening poems or quotes to set intention, communal singing (protest songs or hymns reimagined for civic purpose), a 'civic sermon' — a 20-30 minute keynote on a civic theme like citizenship, democracy, or community. Include readings of meaningful civic texts: passages from the Constitution, civil rights speeches, poetry about community, or statements from local community members. Build in moments of silence for reflection. Close with a recommitment ritual where people share what they're committing to civically. Create an atmosphere that feels celebratory and sacred: good sound, thoughtful lighting, flowers, refreshments afterward. The ritual structure intentionally creates space to ask: 'What are your civic values? How do we form a cohesive fabric together?'

## Who 'S Done It

Citizen University, founded by Eric Liu, has hosted Civic Saturdays across multiple cities. In one southern city, the event focused on 'what it means to be a neighbor' and drew 120 people. A young Black woman from a housing advocacy nonprofit gave the civic sermon. Attendees reported not having had space like that in their adult lives — a place that treated civic participation as sacred. Several participants formed study groups afterward. Another city used Civic Saturday as a platform for emerging leaders, breaking down isolation by creating space to think about politics as shared work, not partisan disagreement.

## Connections

Civic Saturday is rooted in congregational traditions, public ritual, and the civic renewal movement. It assumes secular community needs ritual spaces just like faith communities do. It connects to World Café and story suppers as practices for collective thinking, and to the work of Sunday Assembly (secular congregation). The Civic Saturday Fellowship trains people across the country to host their own.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 months planning, 2-3 hours for the event
- *People*: 2-4 core organizers, 30-200+ attendees
- *Space*: Community center, church, auditorium, outdoor venue with good acoustics
- *Materials*: Microphone/sound system, flowers, printed programs, refreshments
- *Skills*: Facilitation, curating meaningful speakers and texts, comfort with ritual design
- *Energy Level*: Moderate — logistics are straightforward but emotional curation takes thought
