## What It Is

The intentional practice of sitting on front porches and having extended conversations with neighbors as a form of neighboring and community building. When people occupy porches visibly, it creates informal accountability, invites casual relationship-building, and makes it hard to ignore neighbors—recreating the social infrastructure of pre-television, walkable neighborhoods.

## How It Works

Neighbors commit to regular porch time—sitting on front steps or chairs visible to the street with openness to conversation. Low barriers: bring tea or coffee, be visible and welcoming, chat with passing neighbors. Can be intentional community practice where multiple neighbors coordinate porch time, or individual practice of being present on the porch. Extended porch conversations emerge organically as relationships deepen. Front Porch Republic as a conceptual framework emphasizes how physical design (porches close to street, shaded areas, seating) enables neighboring that car-dependent architecture prevents.

## Who 'S Done It

Front Porch Republic is both a publication and grassroots movement of communities reclaiming porch culture. Individual neighborhoods, blocks, and faith communities practice intentional porch sitting. Some New Urbanist neighborhoods design with front porch visibility intentionally.

## Connections

Rooted in pre-automobile America when porches were necessary cooling spaces and primary socializing venues. Connected to front porch tradition, neighborhood design, placemaking, and pedestrian-scale community building. Related to porch stewards and neighborhood associations that emphasize visible community presence.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 30 minutes to several hours; can be regular routine
- *People*: Neighbors, passersby, visitors who join conversations
- *Space*: Front porch, stoop, or street-facing yard with seating
- *Materials*: Seating (chairs, steps), optional: beverages, snacks, welcoming signage
- *Skills*: Hospitality, conversation, attentiveness, approachability
- *Energy Level*: Low—sitting, talking, leisure activity
