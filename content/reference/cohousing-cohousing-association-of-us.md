## What It Is

Cohousing is an intentional, collaborative neighborhood model that combines private homes with shared indoor and outdoor spaces. Rather than a legal structure, cohousing emphasizes active resident participation in design and governance, fostering community connection while maintaining privacy and interdependent living.

## How It Works

Cohousing communities follow a collaborative development process: (1) Future residents participate actively in design decisions, creating spaces that reflect community values and needs. (2) Private homes provide personal space; shared facilities (kitchens, dining, laundries, gardens, recreation) foster regular interaction. (3) Communities establish governance structures and cultural practices supporting connection—shared meals, decision-making processes, maintenance coordination. (4) Residents maintain individual autonomy while building community interdependence. The Cohousing Association of the US (CohoUS) supports this movement through directory services, educational resources, training programs, advocacy, and community building.

## Who 'S Done It

Cohousing communities exist across the U.S. and internationally, ranging from rural ecovillages to urban infill projects. Residents report increased social capital, friendships across generations and backgrounds, shared caregiving, and reduced isolation. Established communities like Glacier Circle (Davis, CA) and Earthaven (North Carolina) demonstrate long-term viability and deep community bonds.

## Connections

Cohousing originated in Denmark in the 1970s and spread internationally. Relates to intentional communities, ecovillages, and cooperative housing movements. Connects to practices of shared resources, participatory decision-making, and alternative living arrangements.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-5 years development; ongoing community management
- *People*: 8-50+ households; requires committed organizing group
- *Space*: Land for development; architectural design supporting shared/private space balance
- *Materials*: Design guides, legal templates, governance tools, CohoUS resources
- *Skills*: Community development, group facilitation, project management, financial planning
- *Energy Level*: High during development; medium for ongoing community management
