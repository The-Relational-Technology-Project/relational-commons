## What It Is

An American tradition of participatory community singing using four-shape musical notation that enables non-trained singers to sight-read and sing complex choral music together. Sacred Harp singings are unrehearsed, non-performance community gatherings where participants sit in hollow squares facing each other, with rotating leaders selecting songs and setting the tempo through arm movements.

## How It Works

Singers arrange themselves in a hollow square with each voice part on one side (treble, alto, tenor, bass) facing inward. A singer steps to the center as leader, selecting hymns from tunebooks like The Sacred Harp and conducting tempo. Singers first sing syllables (fa, sol, la, mi) corresponding to the note shapes, then sing the words. Each singer has equal power to lead. No audience or performance; all singers participate fully. Singings happen regularly in communities (monthly or more) and at regional gatherings, continuing American traditions dating to 1800s.

## Who 'S Done It

Sacred Harp singing is most vibrant in the American South but has revival communities nationwide. Hundreds of singings happen annually. Interest has grown among younger people and diverse communities. Related singing traditions (shape note, fasola) persist in Appalachian regions and are experiencing renewed participation.

## Connections

Emerged from singing school traditions of 1700s-1800s New England, perpetuated in Southern Appalachia. The Sacred Harp tunebook published 1844. Connected to folk traditions, participatory music, community gathering, and collective joy. Related to shape note harmony, community song traditions, and democratic participation in cultural expression.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-3 hours per singing; monthly or more frequent regular singings
- *People*: All are welcome regardless of experience; rotating leaders and singers
- *Space*: Acoustically good community spaces (churches, community centers, outdoor venues)
- *Materials*: Sacred Harp or other shape note tunebooks, seating arranged in hollow square
- *Skills*: Basic music literacy helpful but can be learned; willingness to participate fully
- *Energy Level*: Moderate—sitting and singing, high emotional/spiritual engagement
