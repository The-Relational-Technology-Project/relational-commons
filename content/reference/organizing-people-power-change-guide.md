# Organizing: People, Power, Change (the Organizer's Handbook)

> The 50-page guide to the Ganz organizing framework — five practices, public narrative, 1:1s, snowflake teams, strategy, tactics, and worksheets — adapted for community use and free to share and adapt with attribution.

The core reference behind this shelf: a 50-page guide to community organizing in the Marshall Ganz tradition, originally adapted from the work of Dr. Marshall Ganz of Harvard University and resources from the Leading Change Network and the New Organizing Institute, revised collaboratively by organizers at the Dogwood Initiative, Leadnow, One Cowichan, the David Suzuki Foundation, Stonehouse Institute, and genius (Shea Sinnott and Peter Gibbs, eds., 2014).

It covers the whole arc in teachable form: what organizing is (people, power, change); public narrative (self / us / now, with full worksheets); building relationships (the 1:1, hard asks, the three nos, recruitment and retention); coaching (the five-step process, head/heart/hands); structuring teams (shared purpose, interdependent roles, explicit norms; the snowflake model; the ladder of engagement; team stages); strategizing (people/problem/goal, power-with and power-over, nested goals, theory of change); and acting (the tactics sweet spot, motivational engagement, campaign timelines, the organizing sentence). The appendix worksheets — developing your public narrative, coaching stories, and tracking down the power — are ready to run with any group.

The guide is free to reproduce, distribute, and adapt for your own training (attribution must stay; it may not be sold), which is why its practices could be carried onto this shelf in full. Most recipes on this shelf cite it as their source.
