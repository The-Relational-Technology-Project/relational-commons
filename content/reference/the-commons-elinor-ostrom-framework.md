## What It Is

Elinor Ostrom's groundbreaking research and theoretical framework demonstrating how communities can successfully govern and manage shared resources (common pool resources) through collective action, local institutions, and graduated systems of rules and sanctions. Her work challenged the 'tragedy of the commons' assumption and won the Nobel Prize in Economics for proving that people can cooperatively manage shared resources sustainably.

## Who 'S Done It

Ostrom studied traditional commons (irrigation systems in Nepal and Spain, forests in Switzerland, fisheries in Canada, pasture lands globally) and modern applications including water systems, intellectual property commons, and digital commons. Her framework has informed sustainable resource management worldwide and influenced policy on environmental governance.

## Connections

Ostrom's 1990 'Governing the Commons' built on earlier commons theory while fundamentally challenging Garrett Hardin's 'tragedy of the commons' narrative. Her work connects to community land trusts, cooperative governance, sustainable livelihoods, and relational tech frameworks. Influenced by institutional economics and polycentric governance theory.
