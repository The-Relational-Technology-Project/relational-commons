# The Abundant Community (McKnight & Block)

> The 2010 book arguing that consumer society turned citizens into clients — and that neighborhoods hold three abundant powers: the giving of gifts, the power of association, and hospitality.

John McKnight and Peter Block's 'The Abundant Community: Awakening the Power of Families and Neighborhoods' (Berrett-Koehler, 2010) is the ABCD tradition's most readable statement of *why*. Its argument: consumer society converted citizens into consumers and clients, outsourcing to systems the functions that only communities can actually perform — raising children, sustaining health, assuring safety, caring for the vulnerable. The way back runs through three powers every neighborhood already holds in abundance: **the giving of gifts**, **the power of association**, and **hospitality** — 'our doors are open; there are no strangers here, just friends we have not met.'

The book directly inspired the Abundant Community Edmonton block-connector model (a story on this shelf), and its companion site, abundantcommunity.com, is itself a large archive of neighborhood stories worth mining. Read it as the bridge between this shelf's resource question and its commitment question: gifts, associations, and hospitality are what neighbors commit *to each other* before any campaign gives them a target.
