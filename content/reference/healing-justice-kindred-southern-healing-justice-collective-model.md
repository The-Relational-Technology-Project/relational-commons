# Healing Justice (Kindred Southern Healing Justice Collective Model)

## What It Is

A political strategy and framework for building community-led, survivor-centered responses to collective trauma and systemic oppression. Healing Justice centers healing as a tool for liberation and social transformation, integrating traditional healing practices with social justice organizing to address generational trauma and build collective resilience.

## How It Works

Healing Justice operates on the principle that healing is political and collective, not just individual. The framework brings together healers (spiritual practitioners, wellness workers, artists, therapists), organizers, and community members to: map existing healing resources and traditions in communities; create healing spaces in movement work and at times of collective crisis; facilitate spaces for people to process trauma together; develop healing practices rooted in southern (and other) traditions of resilience; and integrate healing into organizing strategy and decision-making. Kindred Southern Healing Justice Collective, for example, has organized healing justice spaces at movement-building events and in response to collective crises, mapping healers and practitioners throughout the Southeast and creating networks of healing support. The work honors the fact that organizers and activists experience trauma, burnout, and secondary trauma, and that sustainable movements must center healing alongside organizing. Healing Justice rejects the idea of healing as consumer commodity and centers community accountability, cultural wisdom, and collective transformation.

## Who 'S Done It

Kindred Southern Healing Justice Collective (founded 2005-2006, based in Atlanta); Black Rose Anarchist Federation; healing circles in racial justice movements; various local organizations adopting healing justice frameworks; practitioners throughout the Southeast and beyond building healing justice networks.

## Connections

Rooted in Black radical traditions, healing practices from African diaspora, and southern resilience traditions. Connected to disability justice, transformative justice, and community healing practices. Part of broader convergence of healing and justice work. Related to somatic practices, community therapy, and collective care.

- *Time*: Ongoing; healing circles: 2-4 hours; immersive trainings: 1-3 days; integration into organizing: ongoing
- *People*: Healers (spiritual/alternative practitioners, bodyworkers, artists, therapists from communities); organizers; community members and people with lived experience of trauma
- *Space*: Community gathering spaces, healing centers, outdoor spaces; creates intentional, sacred containers
- *Materials*: Art supplies, music/sound, plants/herbs, healing ritual materials, documentation/mapping tools
- *Skills*: Healing facilitation, somatic awareness, cultural competency, trauma awareness, organizing, community knowledge
- *Energy Level*: Medium to high—emotionally and spiritually engaged, requires care and rest
