## What It Is

A strength-based change methodology that shifts community conversations from identifying problems toward discovering what's already working and envisioning what could work better. It uses a 4-D cycle (Discover, Dream, Design, Destiny) to mobilize collective intelligence and inspire action for positive change.

## How It Works

Begin with Discover: craft open-ended questions that prompt stories about peak moments of excellence in your community's history. Train interviewers, conduct one-on-one or small group interviews, and gather stories about times when the community functioned well. Analyze these stories to identify core strengths, unique factors (leadership, relationships, culture), and repeated themes. Move to Dream: invite community members to imagine how the community could grow and evolve, identifying common aspirations across stories. In Design, create 'provocative propositions'—possibility statements describing the community if it amplified its best qualities. These become strategic commitments. Finally, Destiny focuses on implementation: design systems, processes, and strategies that enable these possibilities. Many communities conduct this work through 2-4 day summits that bring the whole system together. Ensure participation is representative and broadly accessible across diverse community members.

## Who 'S Done It

Appreciative Inquiry emerged from organizational development researchers David Cooperrider and Diana Whitney at Case Western Reserve University. It's been adopted by community development organizations, municipal governments, and nonprofits addressing everything from education to neighborhood revitalization. The Center for Appreciative Inquiry has documented applications across diverse communities seeking positive change. Environmental organizations, social justice groups, and rural development initiatives have used AI to mobilize residents around shared visions.

## Connections

Appreciative Inquiry builds on strength-based social work, asset-based community development, and positive psychology. It contrasts with problem-focused approaches by focusing inquiry on excellence and possibility. It connects naturally with other dialogical practices like Circle and World Café, and complements Open Space Technology.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 3-12 months for full cycle; 2-4 day summit for intensive work
- *People*: 30+ community members representing diversity of perspectives; trained interviewers
- *Space*: Interview spaces; summit venue with breakout areas
- *Materials*: Interview guides, recording equipment, flip charts, story synthesis tools
- *Skills*: Appreciative listening, strength-spotting, facilitation of visioning, familiarity with asset mapping
- *Energy Level*: Moderate; builds momentum through story collection and shared vision
