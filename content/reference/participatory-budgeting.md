## What It Is

A democratic process in which community members directly decide how to spend a public budget through collective deliberation and voting. Rather than elected officials or bureaucrats alone determining municipal spending, residents propose and vote on projects that benefit their neighborhoods, from infrastructure improvements to community services.

## How It Works

The process typically unfolds over several months: community members brainstorm and submit project ideas addressing local priorities; volunteers and staff develop ideas into concrete proposals with budgets; communities vote on which projects to fund; and selected projects are implemented and reported back to the community. Participatory budgeting can include ranked choice voting, dot voting, or other democratic decision-making methods. Multiple engagement events ensure accessibility for different community members.

## Who 'S Done It

Over 11,500 participatory budgeting processes have been implemented globally. In the US: Boston's Youth Lead the Change invested $1M in youth-identified projects; Cambridge, MA allocated $1M in 2024-25; King County, Washington distributed $11.75M across 42 community projects in 2024. International examples span Europe, Latin America, and Asia with significant growth in 2024-2025.

## Connections

Originated in Porto Alegre, Brazil in 1989 as a radical democratic innovation. Connects to participatory democracy practices, action-oriented civic engagement, and shared decision-making models. Related to community wealth building and placing power in community hands rather than expert-led development.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 6-12 months for a full cycle, ongoing annually
- *People*: Community facilitators, project managers, city staff, volunteer voter guides, diverse residents
- *Space*: Multiple accessible venues for brainstorming and voting (libraries, community centers, schools, online)
- *Materials*: Proposal templates, budget worksheets, voting ballots, community education materials
- *Skills*: Facilitation, budget literacy, project management, accessibility coordination
- *Energy Level*: High—requires sustained outreach and multiple engagement touchpoints
