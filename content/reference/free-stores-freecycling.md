## What It Is

Community spaces and networks where people give and receive goods for free, operating on gift economy principles to combat waste, build community connections, and meet material needs without payment. Free Stores provide physical gathering spaces while Freecycling networks coordinate exchanges online and locally.

## How It Works

Free Stores operate as permanent or pop-up physical locations (storefronts, market stalls, warehouses, or street corners) where community members drop off unwanted items and pick up new-to-them goods at no cost. Freecycle networks function through online forums or apps where members post items they want to give away or request, then arrange local pickup. Both models prioritize reuse and keeping goods out of landfills. Free Stores often become informal community hubs where neighbors meet and build relationships while accessing needed items. No purchase, trade, or bartering required—the fundamental ethic is generosity and collective care.

## Who 'S Done It

Freecycle Network (global, 5+ million members); Free Store Project (New York City, founded by Myles Smutney, 2020); Really Really Free Markets (distributed across North America); Buy Nothing groups; numerous independent Free Stores in cities across the U.S. and Canada.

## Connections

Part of broader gift economy and sharing economy movements; connected to Buy Nothing groups, mutual aid networks, and community sharing practices. Rooted in anti-consumerism and environmental sustainability activism. Related to repair cafés and tool libraries as alternative resource access models.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: Ongoing (daily for Free Stores; weekly to monthly for Freecycling groups)
- *People*: 4-10 core organizers/volunteers; 20+ regular participants; wider community for drop-offs/pickups
- *Space*: Physical storefront, warehouse, shipping container, market booth, or street-level space for Free Stores; digital platform (email, Facebook, Freecycle app) for Freecycling networks
- *Materials*: Shelving, display tables, storage system; internet connection and digital platform for online networks; optional: signage, maps, logistics tracking
- *Skills*: Community coordination, basic logistics, marketing/outreach, conflict resolution, systems thinking
- *Energy Level*: Medium—requires consistent volunteer coordination and light physical work
