# Intersection Repair (City Repair Project)

## What It Is

A community-led placemaking practice that transforms urban street intersections into vibrant public squares through collaborative design, artistic expression, and infrastructure building. Intersection repairs combine public art (murals), seating and gathering spaces, tool libraries, little free libraries, and community message boards to reclaim streets as community spaces.

## How It Works

Identify an intersection in need of community activation and gather interested neighbors. Form a design team to envision the transformation, creating sketches and proposals that reflect community input. Seek city permits if required, or work with City Repair's legal toolkit if pursuing permitted community art. Organize a work party or 'barn raising' where volunteers contribute labor. Tasks include designing and painting a large central mural, building a kiosk or tea station, installing a tiny library box, creating seating areas, and developing community message boards. The design should reflect the neighborhood's character and values while serving practical community functions. After completion, identify stewards for ongoing maintenance. City Repair's Village Building Convergence provides annual opportunities for intensive intersection repairs, training, and connection with other practitioners.

## Who 'S Done It

City Repair Project, a Portland-based 501(c)(3) founded in 1996, pioneered intersection repair methodology. Their flagship project, Share-It Square at SE 9th and SE Sherrett in Portland, transformed a dangerous intersection into a beloved community gathering space. Since then, hundreds of community-led intersections repairs have occurred in Portland and other cities. The annual Village Building Convergence brings together thousands of volunteers across 50+ sites to conduct natural building, permaculture design, and intersection repairs simultaneously.

## Connections

Intersection repair draws from radical urban planning, participatory design, street art traditions, and indigenous place-making. It connects to broader placemaking movement, DIY urbanism, and right-to-the-city activism. It shares DNA with community art projects, tactical urbanism, and movements reclaiming public space for community use.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 2-4 weeks for planning; 1-3 days for construction/painting
- *People*: 5-50+ volunteers; core team of 3-5 organizers and designers
- *Space*: Street intersection; adjacent space for materials and tool storage
- *Materials*: Paint and brushes, wood for structures, tools, plants, mulch, optional bricks/pavers, signage materials
- *Skills*: Artistic design, community organizing, basic construction, painting, negotiation with city
- *Energy Level*: High; requires sustained physical labor and creative energy
