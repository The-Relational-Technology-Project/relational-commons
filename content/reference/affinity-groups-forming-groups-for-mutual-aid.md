# Affinity Groups: Forming Groups for Mutual Aid

> A practical guide for forming affinity groups—small, decentralized groups of 3-8 people who work together based on shared values, trust, and commitment. This resource specifically addresses the structural and relational foundations needed for sustainable mutual aid organizing.

## What It Is

A practical guide for forming affinity groups—small, decentralized groups of 3-8 people who work together based on shared values, trust, and commitment. This resource specifically addresses the structural and relational foundations needed for sustainable mutual aid organizing.

## How It Works

The guide walks groups through: (1) clarifying shared values and assessing affinity (Do we genuinely want to work together?); (2) establishing agreements around decision-making, confidentiality, and communication; (3) defining the work you'll do together and your role within broader networks; (4) setting up regular meeting rhythms and accountability structures; (5) planning for growth, conflict, and sustainability. The process is intentionally relational—groups spend significant time getting to know each other, building trust, and understanding each person's capacity, skills, and vulnerabilities. This foundation allows groups to respond quickly, make decisions without external approval, and sustain work through crisis.

## Who 'S Done It

Affinity group models have been foundational to squatter networks, anti-police organizing, prison abolition work, and mutual aid projects across North America. One documented example: a housing justice group in the Pacific Northwest formed as affinity groups and was able to prevent two evictions and coordinate longer-term housing solutions for seven families within six months, moving quickly because of established trust and clear agreements.

## Connections

Draws from anarchist organizing traditions, affinity group models developed in anti-authoritarian movements, and queer kinship structures; closely aligned with microsolidarity's crew concept.

## Key Ingredients

- *Time*: 8-12 hours for initial formation, 2-4 hours monthly for ongoing coordination
- *People*: 3-8 people with genuine affinity and commitment
- *Space*: Private meeting space (homes, community center rooms)
- *Materials*: Agreements template, shared calendar, communication platform
- *Skills*: Active listening, facilitation, conflict navigation, strategic thinking
- *Energy Level*: High—requires emotional presence and regular coordination
