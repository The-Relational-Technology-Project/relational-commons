# Savannah Kruger and The Neighborhood Accelerator, Boulder

> Savannah Kruger and her partner lived in a 31-unit building knowing nobody — until cute flyers and a potluck unlocked a community.

<p>Savannah Kruger and her partner Jon Bo lived in a 31-unit apartment building in Boulder, Colorado, and knew almost nobody in it. The loneliness of proximity — surrounded by neighbors but disconnected from all of them — was the starting condition for what became a small experiment in neighborhood building. They made cute flyers with handwritten sticky notes and knocked on every door in the building. Fifteen people came to the first picnic potluck in the park. After the gathering, Jon asked the group to start a group thread — they tried Discord, then landed on WhatsApp — and what had been a one-time event became an ongoing fabric of connection.</p><p>Neighbors began coordinating events, planning impromptu hangouts, watering each other's plants, giving away things they no longer needed, and helping each other with everyday crises like getting locked out or needing a car jump-started. The simple act of knocking on doors and hosting a potluck had unlocked a small but real ecosystem of mutual support. The journey of neighborhood building can be lonely, and Savannah found that while the work was deeply rewarding, she craved peers who were doing similar things in their own places. So she created the Neighborhood Accelerator — a cohort-based program for people trying to build community where they live.</p><p>The first cohort drew participants from across the Supernuclear readership — a community of people interested in co-living and neighborhood design. When Savannah shared three case studies from the program, the response was overwhelming: fifty applications poured in for the next round, and the second cohort grew to thirty to thirty-five neighborhood stewards. What Savannah's story illustrates is that the hardest step in neighborhood building is often the first one — the willingness to knock on a door and say, essentially, "I live here too, and I'd like to know you." The technology is a flyer, a group chat, and a potluck. The innovation is the courage to use them.</p>
