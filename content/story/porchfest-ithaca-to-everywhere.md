# Porchfest, Ithaca to Everywhere

> In 2007, two ukulele-playing neighbors in Ithaca created Porchfest — a free music festival played on front porches that has spread to over 230 locations worldwide.

<p>In 2007, in Ithaca, New York, Lesley Greene and her husband were playing ukuleles on their front steps when a neighbor — Gretchen Hildreth — walked by. They got to talking about how nice it was to hear music outside. They looked around their neighborhood of Fall Creek and realized there were so many musicians living nearby that there could practically be a festival of outdoor music. After months of saying they should do it, they did. In September of that year, they gathered twenty bands and created Porchfest — a free, un-curated music festival played on the front porches, driveways, and lawns of a residential neighborhood.</p><p>There are no tickets. No stages. No paid performers. No curated lineup — anyone who signs up before the deadline and has a porch to play on is in. You don't have to prove yourself to anyone. People walk, bike, push strollers, or drive golf carts from porch to porch, discovering music they'd never seek out and meeting neighbors they'd never otherwise encounter. For years, Lesley and Gretchen funded it out of pocket. Eventually, Ithaca Neighborhood Housing Services provided sponsorship support. But Porchfest stayed stubbornly grassroots — organized by volunteers, powered by donated time, dependent on nothing but porches and people.</p><p>What started as a small event in one neighborhood has become an internationally recognized model. There are now over 230 Porchfests worldwide, including in Australia and Canada. The spread happens person to person — someone attends in Ithaca, moves somewhere else, and starts one. Porchfest is a nearly perfect piece of relational technology. It requires no special infrastructure, no budget, no permissions beyond neighborly goodwill. It turns the porch — the architectural threshold between private life and public space — into a stage for connection. The music is the medium, but the message is: we live here together.</p>
