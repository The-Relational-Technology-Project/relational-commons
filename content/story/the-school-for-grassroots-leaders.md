# The School for Grassroots Leaders

> In Philadelphia, BOLT supports grassroots leaders through an eight-month Saturday cohort and a youth fellowship placing 60 young people in paid internships. Their philosophy: low-income neighborhoods don't lack leaders — they lack infrastructure, connection, and resources. Scale means depth, not spread.

<p>In Philadelphia, Hillary Do runs BOLT — an organization built on a conviction that leaders already exist in every neighborhood, even (and especially) the ones that outsiders have written off.</p>

<p>BOLT supports grassroots leaders through two core programs. The School for Grassroots Leadership is an eight-month cohort that meets three hours every Saturday. It teaches the practical infrastructure of community work: fundraising, budgeting, fiscal sponsorship, hiring, navigating the resource ecosystem. The Youth Leaders Fellowship places 60 young people in paid summer internships, with academic-year training at partner schools where they lead peer-driven Community Action Projects.</p>

<p>But what makes BOLT distinctive is its ecosystem role — connecting grassroots leaders to each other and to systems of power they've often been shut out of. BOLT brings youth to City Hall to meet elected officials, many for the first time. It connects community leaders to funders who can open new financial streams. The goal isn't dependence on BOLT but building the capacity so leaders don't need BOLT long-term.</p>

<p>The people in BOLT's network are doing extraordinary, tangible work. Ron runs Ordinarie Heroes in North Philly. Danni leads Diamond Beauty Academy, a cosmetology program. Cleo leads A Home from Shana Foundation, helping families who've lost children to gun violence — before BOLT, she couldn't afford Ubers for grieving families to get to services; BOLT helped her get a fiscal sponsor and access real funding. Ryan runs As I Plant This Seed. These aren't programs designed in conference rooms. They're responses to real needs by people who live in the neighborhoods they serve.</p>

<p>Hillary pushes back against the idea that low-income neighborhoods lack leaders. The leaders are there. What they lack is infrastructure, connection, and resources. BOLT's philosophy is "going deep, not expanding" — working intensively in a small geography rather than spreading thin across a city. Scale, for BOLT, means depth.</p>

<p>One young person in the program — Mecca — was inspired by watching neighborhood people build from nothing. Now she wants to open an arts rec center and understands how to access the resources and support to do it. Young people in BOLT have higher school attendance than the district average.</p>

<p>Hillary's critique of drive-by solutions is sharp: too many programs treat communities as problems to be solved from the outside. BOLT's approach is different. If you want to understand a community, you should be invited to dinner — not parachute in with a plan. The leaders are already there, already working. The question is whether the rest of the system will recognize, resource, and connect them.</p>
