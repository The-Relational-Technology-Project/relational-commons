# Repair Cafe

> In 2009, Martine Postma organized the first Repair Cafe in Amsterdam — free neighborhood gatherings where people fix broken items together over coffee.

<p>In 2009, Martine Postma — a Dutch journalist who had been writing about sustainability and waste reduction — attended an art exhibition on repair and was so inspired that she organized the world's first Repair Cafe in Amsterdam. The idea was simple: create a free, neighborhood gathering where people could bring broken items — toasters, lamps, bicycles, clothes, laptops — and fix them together with the help of volunteer repair specialists, all over coffee and conversation. Postma's insight was that repair had become invisible. People threw things away not because items couldn't be fixed, but because repair felt inconvenient, expensive, and socially unmoored — something your grandfather did, not something your neighborhood did together. She wanted to make repair attractive again: available around the corner, cheaper than buying new, and fun.</p><p>The first Repair Cafe's success prompted Postma to start Repair Cafe International Foundation in 2011, providing support to groups worldwide wanting to start their own. The movement grew to over 2,000 Repair Cafes across 37 countries. Together, they prevented roughly 420,000 kilograms of waste in 2019 alone — about 10 million kilograms of CO2. But the environmental numbers, while impressive, don't capture what actually happens at a Repair Cafe. A person brings in a broken coffee machine. A retired electrician opens it up, explains what went wrong, and shows the owner how to fix it. Over the course of thirty minutes, knowledge is transferred, a relationship is formed, and a person walks away not just with a working machine but with a shifted sense of what's possible.</p><p>As Postma says, "Once you have experienced the possibilities of repair yourself, you will start to think differently." The Repair Cafe is relational technology disguised as environmental activism — or perhaps it's environmental activism that works precisely because it's relational.</p>
