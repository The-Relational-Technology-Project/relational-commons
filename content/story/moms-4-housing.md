# Moms 4 Housing: The Vacant House on Magnolia Street

> Homeless and marginally housed Black mothers occupied an investor-owned vacant house in West Oakland for 58 days — and came out with the house in a land trust and a new state law against bulk foreclosure-flipping.

In November 2019, a group of homeless and marginally housed Black mothers — publicly led by Dominique Walker, with organizer Carroll Fife alongside — moved into a vacant house at 2928 Magnolia Street in West Oakland. The house belonged to Wedgewood Inc., a Southern California flipping firm that had bought it at foreclosure auction. The mothers' claim was a power analysis you could photograph: Oakland had more vacant homes than homeless people.

They held the house for 58 days, through an armed pre-dawn eviction in January 2020 that drew hundreds of neighbors into the street. Walker: 'We moved into that home and wanted to reclaim it back into the hands of the community.' Under public pressure brokered through the governor's office and the mayor, Wedgewood agreed to sell the house to the Oakland Community Land Trust, which renovated it for the mothers' return. The fight then scaled into law: California's SB 1079 (2020) blocked bulk purchases at foreclosure auctions and gave tenants and families a window to match investor bids. Fife was elected to Oakland's City Council that November.

What it teaches: direct action works when it *dramatizes* a power analysis rather than substituting for one — the vacant-homes-versus-homeless-mothers frame did the arguing. And escalation has a shape: occupation → negotiation → institutional fix (the land trust) → structural fix (state law). Each rung converted the previous one's attention into something durable.
