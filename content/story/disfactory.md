# Disfactory: Anonymity as a Relational Design Choice

> A small Taiwanese environmental group brought a problem to a g0v hackathon; civic hackers built a map where villagers could report illegal factories on farmland without rupturing village relationships. Seven factories down.

Taiwan was losing roughly 1,500 hectares of farmland a year to illegal factory construction, and the official reporting process required citizens to attach their personal identification — which meant reporting the factory often meant reporting your neighbor, with your name on it. Reports didn't come.

A staff member of Citizen of the Earth, Taiwan — a small crowdfunded environmental group with no developers — brought the problem to a **g0v hackathon** in 2019. Volunteer civic hackers built Disfactory: drop a pin and a photo of a suspect factory, anonymously; the organization files the official paperwork with its own name on it. MIT-licensed code, reports released as open data.

In ten months: 1,500+ reports, 200+ official filings, seven factories demolished, and a reporting pipeline that finally matched how villages actually work.

What it teaches: anonymity here isn't anti-social — it's *relational* design, protecting the reporter's standing with neighbors while still protecting the farmland. (Contrast the design-for-care framework: anonymity aimed at land and buildings, never at people.) And the collaboration model is the cleanest on this shelf: the community group brings the problem and the legitimacy; the civic hackers bring the build; the institution files the paperwork.
