# Lamplight Summer Camp

> A free three-week summer program in Alabama where teenagers practice self-governance by actually running the camp — voting on budgets with real money, doing root cause analysis on community problems, and building projects. One camper's mother was so inspired she started a nonprofit that now funds three organizations.

<p>Daniel Marshall's grandfather, D.C. Waid, built a cabin on Lake Guntersville in the 1950s. Decades later, Daniel — a social studies teacher from Southern California who'd landed in New York — was given a book by his mentor: <em>The Long Haul</em>, the autobiography of Myles Horton, founder of the Highlander Folk School. The book, the cabin, a group of teacher friends with summers off, and a question — what would it look like for teenagers to practice genuine self-governance? — became the raw materials for Lamplight.</p>

<p>Lamplight is a free, three-week summer program in Guntersville and Birmingham, Alabama, where teenagers practice leadership by actually running the camp and doing projects for their community. Inspired by Highlander's principles and action civics, the model puts real decisions in young people's hands.</p>

<p>A day at Lamplight might look like this: mornings spent on labor projects — building benches, painting murals, constructing a dance floor. Lunch, then a "Pack Meeting" where campers vote on budgets with line-item proposals and quotes. They handle real money with full financial transparency, including seeing staff pay. Dinner with communal chores. A closing circle with shout-outs and occasional roasts.</p>

<p>Before the projects begin, campers identify problems facing their community through interviews and surveys — sometimes collecting over 100 responses at places like Walmart and local parks. They do root cause analysis and often land on something like isolation as the underlying issue. Then they vote democratically on what to build or do about it.</p>

<p>The learning runs deep. At one point, campers figured out how to set voting options so they'd get their preferred movie night pick — demonstrating they'd internalized how power works. At another moment, kids organized a Walmart fundraiser involving air guitar Christmas caroling, raising $300 for their projects. These aren't lessons from a textbook. They're practiced.</p>

<p>An unexpected ripple: Felicia Jones, mother of a first-year camper, was so moved by what she saw that she started Caring Heart Outreach to address community needs in Guntersville. The organization now funds three nonprofits including Lamplight, runs a teen pantry feeding hundreds weekly, and is acquiring a community center.</p>

<p>Daniel's long-term vision is patient and specific. Over many years, teenagers from Guntersville and Birmingham who know each other — trained in action civics and self-governance — might collaborate on regional change. As he puts it, if you take a really long time with something, you actually get really good at it by the time it matters.</p>

<p>Lamplight isn't trying to scale or replicate itself in distant places. It's trying to go deep in a particular place, with particular people, over a particular stretch of time. The results aren't quick, but the relationships are real — and the young people running the show are building muscles they'll use for life.</p>
