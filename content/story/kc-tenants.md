# KC Tenants: Evicted People Run the Union

> Founded through door-knocking and 1:1s with tenants like Tiana Caldwell — evicted while fighting cancer — KC Tenants won a Tenants' Bill of Rights within a year, then a citywide right to counsel in eviction court.

In 2019, Tara Raghuveer came home to Kansas City after researching its eviction crisis and started doing what the research couldn't: meeting tenants. Among the first was Tiana Caldwell, who with her husband and son had been evicted in late 2018 while she fought a second round of ovarian cancer. Caldwell didn't become a case study; she became a founding leader — of KC Tenants and of a national homes-guarantee campaign.

The union built itself the way this shelf teaches: base-building through shared lived experience, leaders drawn from the constituency, escalation with a strategy. Within a year, the City Council passed a **Tenants' Bill of Rights** (December 2019): notice before entry, utility-cost disclosure, and the legal right to organize. During the pandemic the union fought evictions with direct action — including chaining courthouse doors shut in October 2020. And in June 2022 Kansas City launched a citywide **right to counsel**, putting a lawyer beside every tenant facing eviction, won by the union's sustained campaign.

The union — run by poor and working-class tenants across race and neighborhood — has since moved into electoral organizing and social-housing advocacy, and helped seed a national federation of tenant unions.

What it teaches: Ganz's definition made flesh — the constituency's commitment is the decisive resource. The people with the problem ran the organization that solved it, and every campaign left the union bigger and its leaders more practiced. When the next fight comes, Kansas City's tenants won't be starting over.
