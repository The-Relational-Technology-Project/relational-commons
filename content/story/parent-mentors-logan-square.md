# Parent Mentors: The Program Where the Win Is the People

> Logan Square Neighborhood Association put parents — mostly Latina and Black mothers — into classrooms two hours a day, with organizing training on the side. Many became organizers, and 925 mentors across 117 schools won millions in state funding.

In 1995, Chicago's Logan Square Neighborhood Association — schooled by years of anti-displacement fights — launched a program disarming in its simplicity: parents work two hours a day in their children's classrooms, a hundred-plus hours a semester, with a stipend, and weekly training that mixes instructional practice with community-organizing skills.

The classroom hours are real work. But the program's engine is the ladder underneath: parent mentors — mostly Latina and Black mothers, many of whom had never been asked to lead anything — went on to run campaigns for community learning centers, school annexes to relieve overcrowding, and immigrant-family protections. Many became LSNA organizers and staff; Leticia Barrera, a former parent mentor, rose to direct the program itself.

The model replicated the way good organizing does: the Southwest Organizing Project picked it up in 2004; the resulting Parent Engagement Institute won a million dollars in state funding its first year; and by spring 2019, 925 parent mentors in 117 schools across 30 organizations had won $3.5 million from the Illinois legislature to sustain the work.

What it teaches: this is the ladder of engagement built as a program — supporter to team member to leader, with a paid, structured first rung. It answers 'are we stronger now?' with a roster: every cohort leaves the neighborhood with dozens more people who know how to run a meeting, make a hard ask, and move an institution. The schools got help; the neighborhood got organizers.
