# Grow Food Northampton, Massachusetts

> A community raised $670,000 to purchase 121 acres of farmland, creating a commons for local food sovereignty with ten small farms and a 320-plot garden.

<p>Grow Food Northampton began with a community raising $670,000 to purchase 121 acres of farmland — an act of collective investment in a vision of local food sovereignty. The land now hosts a community farm where ten small farms and farming collectives lease low- and no-cost plots, including four farms owned and operated by farmers of color and refugee farmers. A 320-plot organic community garden serves hundreds of families, with roughly thirty percent receiving subsidized or no-cost plots.</p><p>The organization runs a volunteer-powered Giving Garden that grows over 6,500 pounds of fresh vegetables annually, all delivered to local soup kitchens and the Northampton Survival Center. A Tuesday Market matches the first ten dollars spent with SNAP benefits each week, resulting in $25,000 in local food purchases by low-income families. Over 1,200 public school children visit the farm each year. All of the food and land access programming is guided by a Food Access Advisory Committee comprised of people who have been harmed and marginalized by the conventional food system.</p><p>What makes Grow Food Northampton a relational technology story is the way the land itself becomes a commons — a shared resource that restructures relationships between people and their food, between long-established residents and newcomers, between those who have and those who need. The farm doesn't just grow food. It grows a web of accountability, participation, and mutual care anchored to a specific piece of ground.</p>
