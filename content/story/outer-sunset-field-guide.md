# Outer Sunset Field Guide

> A neighbor built a walking guide app for the Outer Sunset that connects local history, present-day life, and future visions — all within a few blocks of home.

<p>Folks in the Outer Sunset kept asking for a self-guided history walking tour. The idea showed up multiple times in neighborhood meeting notes, so one neighbor decided to stop workshopping and start building. Inspired by a local history archive and some futures-thinking work, he sketched out a past / present / future experience with his daughter on a rainy afternoon — and by the next day there was a working first version at outersunset.place.</p><p>The app organizes hyperlocal content — old photos, present-day stories, and neighbors' visions for the future — around a map of the neighborhood. The primary navigation mechanic is a "Past / Present / Future" toggle that frames each place as part of a living continuum. Within a week, neighbors were contributing photos from the 1950s, sharing stories about longtime businesses, and imagining what a vacant lot could become. A retired teacher offered to narrate audio segments; a local shop owner asked to be featured.</p><p>What started as a simple walking guide became a relational tool — a way for neighbors to see each other's knowledge, memory, and imagination layered onto the same few blocks. The builder shared daily updates with a small network of fellow builders, and the feedback loop between building, sharing, and co-creating with neighbors made the project richer at every step. It's a vivid example of what happens when you build technology that starts from a real request, stays close to home, and invites contribution rather than consumption.</p>
