# Long-Handled Spoons Dinners (Vermont)

> NOFA-VT's dinner micro-grants are named for the long-handled spoons parable: people can only eat by feeding each other. Meals that 'grow joy, power, and safety by building strong relationships and keeping each other fed', with ingredients from local organic farms; up to $200 on the NOFA page ($250 per the With Neighbors listing).

## The invitation
Headline: "Long-Handled Spoons Dinner Micro-Grants". Promise: community-building meals that "grow joy, power, and safety by building strong relationships and keeping each other fed." It is for NOFA-VT members across Vermont. At the time of fetching, the page reads "Applications are currently closed", so no apply button was visible.

## The grant
The NOFA-VT page says up to **$200** per applicant; the With Neighbors listing says $250 (the discrepancy is noted here, not resolved). Asked of hosts: source ingredients from "local, organic, and small-scale farmers" and send a photo of the gathering. Dates for the next round are not stated on the site.

## What the landing page does well
- The name is a parable, and the page explains it with two drawings: one of people struggling to feed themselves with spoons too long to reach their own mouths, one of them feeding each other. The images carry the whole ethos.
- "How Does a Long-Handled Spoon Dinner Work?" walks through the steps so the parable becomes a recipe for an evening.
- It ties the microgrant to the host organization's mission (local farmers get the food budget), so each dinner is both a gathering and a market.
- Sections run intro and founding context, how it works and who funds it, the illustrations, the steps, the status notice, then a donate ask: a complete arc even when applications are closed.
- Tone: warm, justice-grounded, about collective care rather than fun for its own sake.
- Visual feel: illustration-led; colors not stated on the fetched page.
